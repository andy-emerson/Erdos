## 0. The author's decisions (Oct 29, 2026)

1. **Two rows only.** **Q32** is layer 1 with 16-bit index fields (a 15-bit index plus the sign bit), with a capacity of 32,768 whole numbers. **TC**, the textbook control, holds plain rationals: two 64-bit values per rational, stored in lowest terms, computed by the gcd-first method. L1-opt and all its tricks, the Q64 rows, FLINT `fmpq`, GMP `mpq` and the FLINT ceiling are **retired**. Their code and results stay in the history and in the archives. They are not maintained or run (§16).
2. **TC gets the full set of value-comparison shortcuts.** For each of its three algorithms, TC compares stored values (magnitudes, with the signs handled separately) and checks every condition that the paper's §5.2 table lists for that operation: equal denominators, equal numerators, the cross matches, reciprocals and equal values, and the shared-numerator addition row. It takes the matching shortcut, and otherwise falls back to the gcd-first method. TC stays textbook: it has no structures, no caching and no lookups, and it reduces on entry.
3. **Correct programming, not clever engineering**, in both rows. Work whose answer the operation's own logic already knows is not done. This includes:
   - looking up an index the operation already holds;
   - multiplying or dividing by a known 1;
   - computing x − x instead of returning zero;
   - any other step whose result is already known.

   The principle applies symmetrically to both rows. Every other former trick stays out: fingerprints, reserving memory in advance, and remembering where 0 and 1 are stored. §11 lists every known-answer skip.
4. **Three algorithms per row:** addition and subtraction (one algorithm), multiplication, and division.
5. **Systematic optimization comes later.** This round establishes the two textbook baselines. A **profiling build** gives a time breakdown by phase for each row (§13.4).
6. **The ±1 operand shortcut** (added Oct 29, 2026, on the same line as decision 3). Both rows check each operand of a multiplication or division for ±1: by value in TC, and by index in Q32, symmetrically. Then:
   - multiplying by ±1 gives the other operand, with its sign changed for −1;
   - dividing by ±1 likewise;
   - a dividend of ±1 gives the divisor's reciprocal, with the sign fixed.

   These are cases `one_x` and `one_y` (§4 item 1.5, §9, §10, K-15).

## 3. Terms and notation

**Changed:** the rows, *known answer*, *profile phase*.

All terms of v0.1 §3 hold: width $w = 64$, whole number, index, rational, lowest terms, class, handle, stored, entry, operation, operands, case, and $\text{bits}(x)$. In addition:

- **Q32** and **TC** are the two rows (§5). **Q32-null** is the same binary as Q32, run as a separate row for the null control (rule-r2 item 8).
- **Operands.** As in v0.1 §3, $x = s_x \cdot a/b$ and $y = s_y \cdot c/d$. For subtraction, $s_y$ is first replaced by $-s_y$. In Q32, $a, b, c, d$ are whole numbers at indices $i_x, j_x, i_y, j_y$. In TC they are the magnitudes of the stored values: $a = |m_x|$, $b = d_x$, $c = |m_y|$, $d = d_y$.
- **Same.** `same(p, q)` is the comparison of two of $a, b, c, d$: an index comparison in Q32 and a comparison of 64-bit magnitudes in TC. Each whole number is stored once in Q32, so both give the same answer for the same operands.
- **±1 operand.** An operand equal to $+1$ or $-1$. In lowest terms an operand has $a = b$ only when it is $\pm 1/1$, so `same(a, b)` tells that $x$ is ±1, and `same(c, d)` that $y$ is.
- **Known answer.** The result of a step is known when the operation's own logic already determines it (§4).
- **Profile phase.** One of the parts of an operation's time that the profiling build measures separately (§13.4).

## 4. The principle: correct programming, not clever engineering

**New.**

1. **A step whose result the operation already knows MUST NOT be computed.** In this round the operation knows a result in exactly these four ways:
   1. **By its case.** Equal indices or equal magnitudes settle a gcd (paper §5.2), and the case fixes the result. Examples: $\pm 1/1$ in `both`, and $0/1$ in `zero` (§9).
   2. **By its own earlier steps.** These include:
      - a quantity the algorithm itself set, such as $h = 1$ when $g = 1$ in case `num` of addition;
      - an index the operation already holds (Q32);
      - a whole number it has already read (Q32);
      - a value whose sign is known, so the sign is applied by negation and never by multiplication.
   3. **By one test of a gcd it has just computed against 1**, made once, right after the gcd $g$. When $g = 1$, every division by $g$ is skipped. In Q32, every lookup of a result whole number that is then an operand's whole number is skipped too.
   4. **By the invariants of the representation.** For example, every result with the numerator $n = 0$ already has the denominator $e = 1$ (§10.5).
   5. **By an operand equal to ±1** (the author's addition, decision 6). `same(a, b)` or `same(c, d)` tells that an operand is ±1 (§3): an index comparison in Q32 and a value comparison in TC, which give the same answer. The result is then known without arithmetic: $(\pm 1) \cdot y = \pm y$, $x \cdot (\pm 1) = \pm x$, $x / (\pm 1) = \pm x$, and $(\pm 1) / y = \pm d/c$ with the sign $s$ (cases `one_x` and `one_y`, §9).
2. **Not known**, and therefore computed as usual: anything that would take a further comparison of a stored value with a constant, or memory kept across operations. Examples: whether an operand is an integer ($b = 1$), whether it is zero, or where 0 and 1 are stored. Such tests are optimizations. They are left for the systematic step (decision 5; §17 R2-3). The ±1 operand test of item 1.5 is the one exception, added by the author. It compares an operand's two whole numbers with each other, as the comparisons of §9 do, and needs no constant and no memory.
3. **Symmetry.** Both rows MUST apply the principle to the same steps. Their arithmetic is therefore **identical, call for call** (§14.1 item 3). The rows differ only in structure: index comparisons against value comparisons, lookups and indirection against pairs, and the memory each structure takes.
4. **Excluded, by the author:**
   - fingerprints in the lookup;
   - reserving memory in advance;
   - remembering the indices of 0 and 1, and every test that relies on them (the former O-2, O-3 and O-4's index of 1);
   - inline small values; an axis or complex tag;
   - partial sharing; clusters, reference counts and garbage collection;
   - deferred reduction and unreduced stored values;
   - deduplicating rationals;
   - caching gcds, cofactors, products or results;
   - widths other than 64 bits;
   - threads, SIMD, batching and fast multiplication.

   The former O-1 and O-5 tricks, and O-4's skips after a gcd that came out as 1, are now known-answer skips required of the baseline (§11). The other former tricks stay out.

## 6. Common requirements

**Changed:** the interface (prefixes; no `reserve`); four builds instead of three.

1. **Interface:** v0.1 §4.1 for both rows, with the prefixes `q32_` and `tc_`. TC has no `wholes`. `profile(class, report)` is added; it fills the report of §13.4 in the profiling build and returns `SK_ENOTBUILT` in the others.
2. **Values, width, overflow, errors:** as v0.1 §§4.2–4.5, for both rows. TC takes the 08 ranges: numerators in $[-(2^{63}-1), 2^{63}-1]$ and denominators in $[1, 2^{63}-1]$.
3. **Language:** C11, single-threaded, with only the C standard library and the 08 module (v0.1 §4.6 item 1).
4. **Builds.** Both rows and the driver are compiled by one compiler with one set of flags (`gcc -std=c11 -O2`). There are **four builds** of each row, differing only in compile-time settings:
   - **timing:** no counters, audits or profile probes;
   - **counting:** `SK_COUNT`;
   - **test:** `SK_COUNT` and `SK_AUDIT`;
   - **profiling:** `SK_PROFILE` alone (§13.4). It is never used for a time verdict.

   Counters, audits and profile probes MUST NOT change the control flow or the result of any operation. The only branches they add are their own: the profiling build's guards on its sampling flag and on growth (§13.4).

## 7. Q32: structures

**Changed:** this restates v0.1 §5 with the Q32 amendment (v0.1 §18.1), which now applies to the only layer-1 row.

Q32 MUST keep exactly three structures:

| Structure | Shape | Each entry stores | Used bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Whole numbers | list of $W$ | one whole number in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, 64 bits | $8W$ |
| Lookup | array of $S$ slots | a 16-bit index, or the empty mark $\text{FFFF}_{16}$ | $2S$ |
| Rationals | list of $N$ | a 16-bit numerator field (the sign tag in the top bit, a 15-bit index) and a 16-bit denominator index | $4N$ |

1. **Lists** grow only at their end, by doubling, and an index or handle never changes (v0.1 §5 item 1). Each distinct whole number is stored exactly once, in one list shared by numerators and denominators (v0.1 §5 item 2).
2. **The lookup** is the 08 lookup's algorithm (v0.1 §5 item 3): the multiplicative hash with $\phi = \text{9E3779B97F4A7C15}_{16}$, linear probing, $S = 16$ at first, and doubling before an insertion would make the table more than half full, with every index placed again in index order. It is built at 16-bit slots by `lib/src/plk.c` with `-DL1_Q32`, **unchanged**, and compiled as its own translation unit, as in round 1 (lib D-25; §17 R2-2).
3. **Capacity:** at most 32,768 whole numbers, at indices 0 to 32,767. `intern` checks the count once for each new whole number, in every build, and appending the 32,769th aborts with a message. In the test build, every index written to a 16-bit field or slot is read back (v0.1 §18.1 item 3). The "no index" value is $2^{32}-1$ in 32-bit variables, and it is never stored (v0.1 §18.1 item 2).
4. **Handles** (positions in the rational list) stay 32-bit (v0.1 Q-2).
5. **Nothing is stored inline** and nothing is stored in advance: 0 and 1 are interned when first used, like any other whole number (v0.1 L-5).

**Bytes per rational:** $B = 4 + (8W + 2S)/N$.

## 8. TC: structure

**New** (TC replaces the 08 control of v0.1 §9).

TC MUST keep exactly one structure: a list of $N$ pairs $(m, d)$, each two 64-bit signed integers. Each pair is in lowest terms with $d \ge 1$, and zero is $(0, 1)$. The list grows by doubling (`SK_RESERVE`, initially 16). **Bytes per rational:** $B = 16$.

TC has no other structure: no lookup, no cache, no record of earlier operations, and no reserved capacity.

## 9. Comparisons and the choice of case

**Changed:** TC makes the same comparisons, on magnitudes. Addition gains the case `zero` (x − x), which is taken first. Multiplication and division gain the ±1 operand cases `one_x` and `one_y`, which are taken first.

Every operation reads its operands' comparison keys (Q32: the four indices and two tags; TC: the two pairs, from which it forms $a = |m_x|$ and $c = |m_y|$). It then makes its comparisons, all of them and in this order, before any case is chosen (v0.1 §7, L-13): the four below in every operation, and in multiplication and division the two ±1 tests after them.

| Comparison | Equal means | Made in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `same(b, d)` | same denominator | every operation |
| `same(a, c)` | same numerator, in magnitude | every operation |
| `same(a, d)` | cross: $a = d$ | every operation |
| `same(c, b)` | cross: $c = b$ | every operation |
| `same(a, b)` | $x = \pm 1$ | multiplication, division |
| `same(c, d)` | $y = \pm 1$ | multiplication, division |

Only division's zero test comes before them (§10.3). The case with the fewest gcds left is taken. Ties are broken by fewer multiplications, and then by the paper's order of rows (v0.1 L-8):

| Algorithm | Order of cases (gcds left) |
| --- | --- |
| addition and subtraction | `zero` (0) · `cross` (0) · `den` (1) · `num` (1 or 2) · `general` (1 or 2) |
| multiplication | `one_x` (0) · `one_y` (0) · `both` (0) · `den` (0) · `num` (0) · `cross_ad` (1) · `cross_cb` (1) · `general` (2) |
| division | `one_x` (0) · `one_y` (0) · `both` (0) · `cross_ad` (0) · `cross_cb` (0) · `num` (1) · `den` (1) · `general` (2) |

**Cases `one_x` and `one_y`** are taken when $x$, or $y$, is ±1 (§3). Like `both`, they need no gcd and no multiplication. Unlike it, their result's whole numbers are an operand's, whose indices the operation holds, so Q32 needs no lookup (K-Q1), where `both` needs one (K-Q2). So they come first: `one_x` before `one_y`, in operand order. When both operands are ±1, `one_x` is taken. Every operand pair that `both` took in v0.1 with an operand of ±1 now takes `one_x` or `one_y`, with the same result.

**Case `zero`** is taken when $b = d$ and $a = c$ and the effective tags differ, which is $x + (-x)$, including $x - x$. Its result, $0/1$, is known (§4 item 1.1). It needs no gcd and no multiplication, so it comes first. *Non-normative:* in v0.1, $\pm\tfrac{1}{1} \mp \tfrac{1}{1}$ took case `cross`. It now takes `zero`, and nothing else moves.

*Non-normative.* Apart from case `zero`, exactly one addition gives 0: $0 + 0$. Zero is always stored as $0/1$ with the tag $+$, so in $0 + 0$ the tags are equal and case `zero` is not taken. The sum takes case `den` with $t = 0$, and $h = \gcd(0, 1) = 1$, so K-3 stores $0/1$. In full, a zero sum needs $s_x\,a\,d = -s_y\,c\,b$:
- if $a = 0$, then $c = 0$ and, in lowest terms, $b = d = 1$. Equal effective tags give $0 + 0$ (case `den`, $t = 0$); different ones give $0 - 0$ (case `zero`);
- if $a \ne 0$, then $a\,d = c\,b$ in lowest terms gives $a = c$ and $b = d$, with different effective tags: case `zero`.

So the zero results of an operation come from case `zero`, from $0 + 0$ in case `den`, from a product with a zero factor, from a quotient whose dividend is zero, and from entering zero. Each of them has the denominator 1 (§10.5).

## 10. The three algorithms

**Changed:**
- one pseudocode for both rows;
- the known-answer skips are written in, marked `[K-n]` (§11);
- multiplication works on magnitudes with the sign $s$, in every case;
- multiplication and division take the ±1 operand cases `one_x` and `one_y` first (decision 6);
- Q32 reads only the whole numbers that its case uses;
- the store's zero normalization is gone (§10.5).

The pseudocode below is followed by **both rows**, in the order of gcds, divisions and multiplications written. A quantity written twice is computed once (08 DEC-18). The rows differ only where marked:
- **"read"**: in Q32 this reads a whole number from the list, once per distinct index (K-Q3). In TC the magnitude is already at hand from the comparisons.
- **`store(n, e)`** and its index hints `i:` and `j:` (§10.5): Q32 uses a hint to skip a lookup. TC ignores the hints.

Applying a sign $s$ or a tag is a conditional negation. It is never a multiplication, is not counted (08 counting rule 1) and cannot overflow (K-13). $\text{sgn}(s, v)$ denotes this: it is $v$ when $s = +1$ and $-v$ when $s = -1$. Written $s_x \cdot a$ in the pseudocode, it means the same. Every multiplication, addition and subtraction is checked, as in v0.1 §4.4. The overflow step names follow v0.1 lib D-4.

The pseudocode writes `let` for an assignment and `=` for a comparison. `let a′ = a` names a value already held and computes nothing.

### 10.1 Addition and subtraction

```text
function add(x, y)                                  subtraction: let s_y = −s_y, then add(x, y)
    read the comparison keys; make the four comparisons of §9
    if same(b, d) and same(a, c) and s_x ≠ s_y then                       case zero      [K-2]
        return store(0 over 1)
    end if
    if same(a, d) or same(c, b) then                                      case cross: gcd(b, d) = 1
        read a, b, c, d
        return store((s_x·a · d + s_y·c · b) over (b · d))
    end if
    if same(b, d) then                                                    case den: gcd(b, d) = d
        read a, c, d
        let t = s_x·a + s_y·c                                             (t = 0 only for 0 + 0: §9)
        let h = gcd(t, d)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-3]
            return store(t over d;  j: j_x)                               [K-Q1]
        end if
        return store((t / h) over (d / h))
    end if
    if same(a, c) then                                                    case num: gcd(a, b·d) = 1
        read a, b, d
        let g = gcd(b, d)
        if g = 1 then                                                     [K-4]
            let t = s_x·d + s_y·b
            return store((a · t) over (b · d))
        end if
        let b′ = b / g, d′ = d / g
        let t = s_x·d′ + s_y·b′
        let h = gcd(t, g)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-5]
            return store((a · t) over (b′ · d))
        end if
        return store((a · (t / h)) over (b′ · (d / h)))
    end if
    read a, b, c, d                                                       case general
    let g = gcd(b, d)
    if g = 1 then                                                         [K-6]
        return store((s_x·a · d + s_y·c · b) over (b · d))
    end if
    let b′ = b / g, d′ = d / g
    let t = s_x·a · d′ + s_y·c · b′
    let h = gcd(t, g)
    if h = 1 then                                                         [K-7]
        return store(t over (b′ · d))
    end if
    return store((t / h) over (b′ · (d / h)))
end function
```

1. Case `cross` with $a = d$ reads three distinct whole numbers in Q32, and with $c = b$ likewise (K-Q3).
2. In case `num`, the result's tag is the sign of $t$, since $t$ already carries $s_x$ and $s_y$. It is also the sign of $a \cdot t$, since $a > 0$ in this case ($a = c = 0$ would give $b = d = 1$, which is case `den`). $t \ne 0$ here: $t = 0$ would need $b = d$ (when $g = 1$) or $b' = d' = 1$ (when $g \ne 1$), and either means $b = d$, which is case `den` again.
3. In case `den`, $t = 0$ only for $0 + 0$. Then $d = 1$ and $h = \gcd(0, 1) = 1$, and K-3 stores $0/1$ (§9).
4. Against v0.1:
   - `num` with $g = 1$ no longer divides by $h = 1$ (v0.1 lib D-6 is reversed);
   - `den`, `num` and `general` skip their divisions when $h = 1$;
   - `zero` is new.

   Every stored value is unchanged.

### 10.2 Multiplication

```text
function multiply(x, y)
    read the comparison keys; make the six comparisons of §9
    let s = s_x · s_y
    if same(a, b) then                                                    case one_x: x = ±1   [K-15]
        read c if s_x = −1                                                (Q32 only: see note 1)
        return store(sgn(s, c) over d;  i: i_y, j: j_y)                   [K-Q1]
    end if
    if same(c, d) then                                                    case one_y: y = ±1   [K-15]
        read a if s_y = −1                                                (Q32 only: see note 1)
        return store(sgn(s, a) over b;  i: i_x, j: j_x)                   [K-Q1]
    end if
    if same(a, d) and same(c, b) then                                     case both: a = d, c = b
        return store(sgn(s, 1) over 1;  j: the numerator's)               [K-12, K-Q2]
    end if
    if same(b, d) then                                                    case den: no gcd
        read a, c, d
        return store(sgn(s, a · c) over (d · d))
    end if
    if same(a, c) then                                                    case num: no gcd
        read a, b, d
        return store(sgn(s, a · a) over (b · d))
    end if
    if same(a, d) then                                                    case cross_ad: a cancels
        read c, b
        let h = gcd(c, b)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-8]
            return store(sgn(s, c) over b;  i: i_y, j: j_x)               [K-Q1]
        end if
        return store(sgn(s, c / h) over (b / h))
    end if
    if same(c, b) then                                                    case cross_cb: b cancels
        read a, d
        let h = gcd(a, d)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-8]
            return store(sgn(s, a) over d;  i: i_x, j: j_y)               [K-Q1]
        end if
        return store(sgn(s, a / h) over (d / h))
    end if
    read a, b, c, d                                                       case general
    let g_1 = gcd(a, d)
    let g_2 = gcd(c, b)
    if g_1 = 1 then let a′ = a, d′ = d                                    [K-9]
    else let a′ = a / g_1, d′ = d / g_1
    if g_2 = 1 then let c′ = c, b′ = b                                    [K-9]
    else let c′ = c / g_2, b′ = b / g_2
    return store(sgn(s, a′ · c′) over (b′ · d′))
end function
```

1. In cases `one_x` and `one_y`, the result is the other operand with the sign $s$. When the ±1 operand is $+1$, the result is the other operand itself, tag included, and Q32 needs none of its whole numbers. When it is $-1$, Q32 reads the other operand's numerator, so that a zero result keeps the tag $+$ (§10.5): its tag is $s$ unless that numerator is 0. TC has every magnitude at hand and negates (K-13). Neither row does any arithmetic in these cases, so their arithmetic stays identical.
2. The general case works on magnitudes, with the sign in $s$. v0.1 took $\gcd(m_x, d)$ on the signed numerator, which gives the same gcd, the same gcd work and the same counts (§17 R2-8).

### 10.3 Division

```text
function divide(x, y)
    read the comparison keys
    read c                                                                (Q32: by its index, before the comparisons)
    if c = 0 then fail with division by zero
    make the six comparisons of §9
    let s = s_x · s_y
    if same(a, b) then                                                    case one_x: x = ±1, x/y = s·d/c   [K-15]
        return store(sgn(s, d) over c;  i: j_y, j: i_y)                   [K-Q1]
    end if
    if same(c, d) then                                                    case one_y: y = ±1, x/y = s_y·x   [K-15]
        read a if s_y = −1                                                (Q32 only: see note 3)
        return store(sgn(s, a) over b;  i: i_x, j: j_x)                   [K-Q1]
    end if
    if same(a, c) and same(b, d) then                                     case both: x = ±y, y ≠ 0
        return store(sgn(s, 1) over 1;  j: the numerator's)               [K-12, K-Q2]
    end if
    if same(a, d) then                                                    case cross_ad: a²/(b·c), no gcd
        read a, b                                                         (c already read)
        return store(sgn(s, a · a) over (b · c))
    end if
    if same(c, b) then                                                    case cross_cb: a·d/b², no gcd
        read a, d                                                         (b = c, already read)
        return store(sgn(s, a · d) over (c · c))
    end if
    if same(a, c) then                                                    case num: a cancels
        read d, b
        let h = gcd(d, b)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-10]
            return store(sgn(s, d) over b;  i: j_y, j: j_x)               [K-Q1]
        end if
        return store(sgn(s, d / h) over (b / h))
    end if
    if same(b, d) then                                                    case den: b cancels
        read a                                                            (c already read)
        let h = gcd(a, c)
        if h = 1 then                                                     [K-10]
            return store(sgn(s, a) over c;  i: i_x, j: i_y)               [K-Q1]
        end if
        return store(sgn(s, a / h) over (c / h))
    end if
    read a, b, d                                                          case general
    let g_1 = gcd(a, c)
    let g_2 = gcd(b, d)
    if g_1 = 1 then let a′ = a, c′ = c                                    [K-11]
    else let a′ = a / g_1, c′ = c / g_1
    if g_2 = 1 then let b′ = b, d′ = d                                    [K-11]
    else let b′ = b / g_2, d′ = d / g_2
    return store(sgn(s, a′ · d′) over (b′ · c′))
end function
```

1. The zero test reads $c$ before the comparisons (v0.1 L-12), so that $\tfrac{0}{1} \div \tfrac{0}{1}$ is reported as division by zero. In TC it tests $m_y = 0$. In Q32, the $c$ read for the test is the $c$ used later (K-Q3).
2. Case `cross_cb` writes $c \cdot c$ for $b \cdot b$. They are the same whole number, already read, so there is one read and the same multiplication as v0.1.
3. In case `one_x`, the result $s \cdot d/c$ is the divisor's reciprocal with the sign $s$. It is in lowest terms, $c \ne 0$ by the zero test, and $d \ge 1$, so its tag is $s$ and Q32 reads nothing more. In case `one_y`, the result is $x$ with the sign $s$, as in multiplication (§10.2 note 1): Q32 reads $a$ only when $s_y = -1$.

### 10.4 Entry

```text
function enter(m, d)
    if d = 0 then fail with division by zero
    if d < 0 then let m = −m, d = −d
    let g = gcd(m, d)
    if g = 1 then                                                         [K-1]
        return store(m over d)
    end if
    return store((m / g) over (d / g))
end function
```

Both rows reduce on entry (decision 2).

### 10.5 Storing a result

The operations pass `store` a signed numerator $n$ and a denominator $e \ge 1$ in lowest terms. **Whenever $n = 0$, $e = 1$ already** (§9, non-normative note; v0.1 §8 and paper §2.4). So **`store` MUST NOT test whether $n = 0$, and MUST NOT assign $e = 1$** (K-14). In the test build, the store checks this and aborts if it fails.

```text
function store(n over e; optional hints i, j)     Q32
    if n < 0 then let tag = − else let tag = +
    if no hint i then let i = intern(|n|)         the numerator first (v0.1 L-4); a given i is [K-Q1]
    if hint j = "the numerator's" then let j = i                                    [K-Q2]
    else if no hint j then let j = intern(e)                                        a given j is [K-Q1]
    append (tag, i, j) to the rationals; return its handle
end function

function store(n over e)                          TC: hints are ignored
    append (n, e) to the pairs; return its handle
end function
```

`intern` is as in v0.1 §6: it looks the value up and appends it if it is new, with the capacity check of §7 item 3. In the test build, every hint is checked against the list: $W[i] = |n|$ and $W[j] = e$, and a mismatch aborts.

### 13.4 The profiling build

**New** (decision 5). **Revised after review:** an operation takes about 40–80 ns, too little to carry a probe at every phase boundary of every call. The probes now run on a sample of the calls, every read is fenced on both sides, and the build's slowdown is limited. Its purpose is to show the next step where each row's time goes. Its figures are reported, never judged (rule-r2 item 10).

1. **Build.** `SK_PROFILE`, with no `SK_COUNT` and no `SK_AUDIT`, by the same compiler and flags, as a separate binary per row. It is the timing build plus three things: the phase probes, each guarded by a test of the class's sampling flag; the M probes, guarded by the growth test (item 4); and the driver's batch reads (item 3.1). The probes change no result, and no control flow other than their own guards. The build is never used for a time verdict.
2. **Clock.** The time-stamp counter (x86-64; the machine has `constant_tsc` and `nonstop_tsc`). **Every read** in this build, probe or batch, is `_mm_lfence(); __rdtsc(); _mm_lfence()`. The fence before makes earlier work finish before the read; the fence after keeps later work from starting before it. Within a sampled call, probes are **chained**: each read closes the current phase and opens the next, so a boundary costs one read. Cycles are converted to nanoseconds by the TSC rate, measured over the invocation against `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`.
3. **Two levels of measurement.**
   1. **Batch totals, on every call.** The driver reads the clock at the start and the end of each part that the timing build times: entry, operations and read-out, in each run of a rep. Two reads per part cost almost nothing, so these totals give each part's time with the probes off, as the **batch time per operation**.
   2. **Phase probes, on one call in K.** The driver sets the class's sampling flag before a sampled call and clears it after. The chained probes inside the row run only while the flag is set. An unsampled call pays only the untaken guards. **K = 32** (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026). The gaps between sampled calls are drawn uniformly from 1 to $2K - 1$ by a generator with the fixed seed 20261029 (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026), so their mean is K and they cannot fall in step with a periodic workload. Sampling covers entry, every operation and read-out alike. The driver also reads the clock just before and just after each sampled call, and that outer pair gives the call's total.
4. **Phases** (the author's list, with the remainder made explicit):

   | Phase | Q32 | TC |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | **F** operand fetch | reading the two rationals' fields (indices, tags); reading the case's whole numbers (K-Q3) | reading the two pairs; forming $a$, $c$ |
   | **C** shortcut checks | the comparisons of §9 (the ±1 tests included), the case dispatch, the `zero` test, division's zero test | the same, on values |
   | **A** arithmetic, split into **A.gcd** (each gcd call bracketed alone) and **A.rest** (multiplications, exact divisions, additions, sign negations, the tests of a gcd against 1) | same | same |
   | **I** result interning or lookup | `intern`: lookup searches, appending a new whole number, placing it in a slot (not growth) | — (TC has none: reported as 0) |
   | **S** record write | the sign split and the append of the rational's two fields | the append of the pair |
   | **M** memory management | the growth of the whole-number list, of the rational list, and of the lookup (doubling and placing every index again) | the growth of the pair list |
   | **U** unattributed | a sampled call's outer total, less the sum of its phases and its probes' cost: call overhead and the guards | same |

   F is entered twice in Q32 (fields, then whole numbers) and accumulates both.

   **M is bracketed on every growth**, sampled or not, and is reported in full, not as a sample. Growths are few (one per doubling of a list or table), so sampling would miss most of them, and probing them all costs little. The call site tests the growth condition in the profiling build only; for the lookup, it tests whether $2(k+1) > S$ before calling the unchanged `plk.c`. So a store without growth pays no M probe. In a sampled call, the time of a growth is taken out of the phase in which it happened.
5. **Granularity.** Accumulators are kept, from the sampled calls only, per **job phase** (entry, add, sub, mul, div, read-out) × **case** × **profile phase**, with the number of sampled calls and of probe reads charged to each. M is kept per job phase, from every growth. Read-out has only F (and S = 0).
6. **Probe cost.** Each invocation first measures the cost of one fenced read: the median over 1,000 blocks of 1,000 back-to-back reads (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026). Each phase is reported **raw** and **corrected**, where corrected = raw − reads × cost, floored at 0, together with the cost itself.
7. **Estimates.** Within each job phase, a profile phase's **share** is its corrected time over the sum of the corrected times of the sampled calls (M excluded). Its **estimated time per operation** is its share times (the job phase's batch time per operation − M's time per operation). M's own time per operation is its full total over the job phase's operations. Per case, only shares and the sampled calls' mean corrected times are given.
8. **Slowdown limit.** A profiling invocation's **slowdown** is its batch total (entry, operations and read-out) over the timing build's median total for the same row and job. It MUST be at most **1.5** (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026). The rule checks it in the pilot, before the run, and on every profile invocation; an invocation over the limit is reported with its slowdown, and its phase figures are withheld (rule-r2 item 10). *Non-normative:* a sampled call makes about ten fenced reads of some tens of cycles each, about 100 ns in all; at one call in 32, that adds about 3–4 ns to an operation of 40–80 ns, plus the guards.
9. **Indicative only.** The fences serialize execution at every probe of a sampled call, so its phases cannot overlap. In the timing build they do: a whole number's load overlaps the comparisons, and a lookup's memory latency overlaps the record write. A phase's sampled time is therefore not its time in the timing build, and the shares do not split the timing build's time exactly. **The per-phase Q32 − TC differences are indicative only**: they show where to look next, not how much a change would save. The batch times per operation are close to the timing build's (item 8); the phase figures are not.
10. **Report.** `profile(class, report)` returns the accumulators and the batch totals. The driver sums them over the $r$ runs of a rep and prints them per job phase, case and profile phase, with K, the number of sampled calls and the slowdown.

*Not available:* a system sampling profiler (`perf` is not installed on the machine), so sampling is done inside the build.
