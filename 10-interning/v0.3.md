## 0. Why round 3, and the author's decisions (Oct 29, 2026)

**Why.** Round 2's Q32 (v0.2 §7) had **three** structures: the list of rationals (pairs of 16-bit index fields), the list of whole numbers, and a separate hash lookup from value to index (`plk.c`, 16-bit slots, doubled when half full). The author's model has **two**. Round 2's result ([`test/results-r2/`](test/results-r2/results.md)) stays on record, unchanged, labelled **"three-structure Q32 (retired)"**. Round 3 redoes round 2 with the author's two-structure model, in two forms.

The author's decisions:

1. **Exactly three rows**, each with every structure named and counted here. Nothing else may exist in a row (§6 item 5).
   - **TC**, the textbook control (v0.2 §8): **one** structure. *Amended Oct 29, 2026 (§17):* its source is `lib/src/tc3.c`, round 2's `tc.c` with the special cases of §17.3; `tc.c` stays unchanged as round 2's record.
   - **Q32-index**: **two** structures (§7): the rationals, whose two 16-bit handles are **indices**; and a list of whole numbers in arrival order, searched **from top to bottom**. There is **no hash and no lookup structure**.
   - **Q32-hash**: **two** structures (§8): the rationals, whose two 16-bit handles are **slots**; and a **hash map of whole numbers** with exactly **32,768 slots**, fixed in size, never resized. There is **no separate list**. A whole number's slot is its handle.
2. **Everything else is identical across the rows**, as in round 2: the arithmetic, the cases and comparisons, the special cases (±1 operands, x − x), the known-answer skips, the three algorithms, and reduction on entry (v0.2 §§4, 9–11). The rows differ only in storage. Round 2's call-for-call arithmetic check is kept, across all three rows (§12.1).
3. **Q32-hash has no fill limit and no rejection mechanism.** It fills as needed. Only a truly full table (all 32,768 slots used) is an error, detected, never silent (§8 item 5). No job of this round reaches it. **The fill level is set by the problems given to the row, not by the row.** Round 2's jobs fill the table to about 1% (job 3), 33% (10), 39% (11), 47% (2), 65% (4), 85% (8), 87% (9) and 91% (1). Each job's fill and probes per store are reported, and speed against fill is shown across the jobs (rule-r3 item 13). *A dedicated sweep of fill levels can be designed in a later round, by generating problems sized to target fills.* It is not part of this round.
4. **Handles stay 16 bits**: 15 bits plus the sign bit on the numerator's handle. Capacity is 32,768 whole numbers in both Q32 rows. No handle is widened and the table size does not vary.
5. **Measures** (rule-r3 item 7): creation time and operation time, each with its own verdict, and the total reported; bytes per rational, with the hash table's fixed 256 KB counted in full; the gcd-work check. Also reported per job: the hash table's fill and average probes per store, and the index list's average entries examined per search.

## 3. Terms

All terms of v0.2 §3 hold. Where v0.2 says "index" for a whole number's position, read **handle**. Changed or new terms:

- **Rows:** **TC**, **Q32-index**, **Q32-hash** (§5). **Q32-hash-null** is Q32-hash's binary run as a separate row for the null control (rule-r3 item 8). It is not a fourth design.
- **Handle (whole-number handle).** One of a rational's two 16-bit fields that name a whole number: an **index** into the list in Q32-index, a **slot** of the table in Q32-hash. The numerator's field carries the sign tag in its top bit and the handle in its low 15 bits. The denominator's field is its handle. Valid handles are 0 to 32,767.
- **Rational's handle.** A rational's position in the list of rationals, which the operations return. It stays a 32-bit value, as in v0.2 §7 item 4. It is not one of the 16-bit handles.
- **`same(p, q)`** compares two handles in both Q32 rows. Each whole number is stored once in each Q32 row, so equal handles mean equal values, as in v0.2 §3.
- **Search** (Q32-index). Looking for a whole number in the list, top to bottom (§7 item 3). **Entries examined**: the list entries compared with the value in one search.
- **Store** (Q32-hash, of a whole number). Hashing a value and probing to its slot (§8 item 3). **Probes**: the slots read in one store, the home slot included.
- **Fill** (Q32-hash). The number of used slots over 32,768.

## 7. Q32-index: two structures

Q32-index MUST keep exactly these two structures:

| # | Structure | Shape | Each entry stores | Used bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) | Rationals | list of $N$ | a 16-bit numerator field (the sign tag in its top bit, a 15-bit **index**) and a 16-bit denominator **index** | $4N$ |
| (b) | Whole numbers | list of $W$, in **arrival order** | one whole number in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, 64 bits | $8W$ |

1. **Arrival order.** A new whole number is appended at the end of list (b). Its index is its position, 0 for the first, and **never changes**. Each distinct whole number is stored exactly once, shared by numerators and denominators.
2. **No hash, no lookup structure.** Nothing maps a value to its index except the search of item 3.
3. **Storing a whole number** whose index the operation does not already hold (that is, every store not skipped by K-Q1 or K-Q2, v0.2 §11.2) is a **textbook linear search**:

   ```text
   function intern(v)                                   Q32-index
       for k = 0, 1, ..., W − 1                          top to bottom
           if list[k] = v then return k                  found: reuse its index
       end for
       if W = 32,768 then fail: capacity exceeded         (item 5)
       append v as list[W]; let W = W + 1; return W − 1  not found: append
   end function
   ```

   The search reads entries in index order and stops at the first match. That is its only exit before the end of the list: no ordering, sentinel or other shortcut is added. Searching all W entries for a value that is not there is the textbook cost of this structure.
4. **Growth.** List (a) and list (b) grow at the end by doubling, from 16 entries, as in round 2 (`SK_RESERVE`; v0.2 §7 item 1) (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, R3-3). List (b) never holds more than 32,768 entries (256 KB). Positions never change on growth.
5. **Capacity:** 32,768 whole numbers, at indices 0 to 32,767. `intern` checks the count once for each new whole number, in every build. Appending the 32,769th fails with a message and aborts, as in v0.2 §7 item 3. In the test build, every index written to a 16-bit field is read back.
6. **Reading an operand's whole number** is direct access by index: `list[i]` (v0.2 K-Q3 unchanged).

**Bytes per rational:** $B = 4 + 8W/N$.

## 8. Q32-hash: two structures

Q32-hash MUST keep exactly these two structures:

| # | Structure | Shape | Each entry stores | Used bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (a) | Rationals | list of $N$ | a 16-bit numerator field (the sign tag in its top bit, a 15-bit **slot**) and a 16-bit denominator **slot** | $4N$ |
| (b) | Hash map of whole numbers | **exactly 32,768 slots**, allocated once in `create`, **fixed size, never resized** | each slot is **empty** or holds **one** whole number in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, 64 bits | $8 \times 32{,}768 = 262{,}144$ (256 KB), always |

1. **No separate list.** The table is the only store of whole numbers. A whole number's slot is its handle, and it never changes: nothing is moved, deleted or rehashed.
2. **The empty mark** is the value −1 in the slot's 64-bit signed word, which is never a whole number (whole numbers are at least 0). `create` sets every slot to −1 (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, R3-2).
3. **Open addressing, textbook** (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, R3-2):
   - **Hash function.** Multiplicative (Fibonacci) hashing, Knuth's method, with the constant of round 2's lookup: $h(v) = \lfloor ((v \cdot \phi) \bmod 2^{64}) / 2^{49} \rfloor$, $\phi = \text{9E3779B97F4A7C15}_{16}$. These are the top 15 bits of the 64-bit product, a slot in $[0, 32{,}767]$. The multiply is computed in `uint64_t` (unsigned), so its wrap modulo $2^{64}$ is defined and it has no undefined overflow.
   - **Collision:** linear probing to the next slot, $s \leftarrow (s + 1) \bmod 32{,}768$.

   ```text
   function intern(v)                                   Q32-hash: store a whole number
       let s = h(v)
       repeat at most 32,768 times                       one probe per slot read
           if table[s] = v then return s                 found: reuse that slot
           if table[s] is empty then
               let table[s] = v; let W = W + 1; return s stored in the empty slot
           end if
           let s = (s + 1) mod 32,768
       end repeat
       fail: the table is full                           (item 5)
   end function
   ```
4. **Reading an operand's whole number** is direct access by slot: `table[s]`. It needs no hashing.
5. **Full table.** The table has no fill limit and refuses nothing before it is full. Only when all 32,768 slots hold whole numbers and a new value comes is the store an error. It is detected by the bounded probe loop above, the textbook "hash table overflow". It fails with a message and aborts, in every build, as Q32-index's capacity does. The count $W$ of used slots is kept in the class header for the reports (fill $= W/32{,}768$). The loop's bound, not the count, detects the full table. No job of this round fills the table (§0 item 3).
6. In the test build, every slot written to a 16-bit field is read back.

**Bytes per rational:** $B = 4 + 262{,}144/N$. The table counts in full, used or not.

*Non-normative.* At the fills of round 2's jobs, textbook estimates for linear probing at fill α give about $\tfrac12(1 + 1/(1-α))$ probes for a successful store and $\tfrac12(1 + 1/(1-α)^2)$ for one that places a new value. At job 1's final fill of 91%, a new value would take about 63 probes, but the average over the job's growing fill is much lower, likely a few probes per store. The probes are counted exactly and predicted by the model (§12.1). They are not assumed.

### 11.2 Memory

- **TC:** used bytes $16N$, and allocated bytes at capacity (unchanged).
- **Q32-index:** used bytes $4N + 8W$, and allocated bytes at capacity (both lists).
- **Q32-hash:** used bytes $4N + 262{,}144$, and allocated bytes at capacity (the rational list's capacity, plus 262,144). The fill $W/32{,}768$ is reported beside them.

### 11.3 Timing

As v0.2 §13.3. The driver reads the clock only between calls, and also just before and after `create` (§6 item 4), and times three parts per run:
- **creation**: `create` and every entering call, building the stored rationals of the entered values. `create`'s own time, from the same two clock reads around it, is also reported per run on its own, with its share of the creation part. It is reported, not judged;
- **operations**: every add, sub, mul and div call, each including the store of its result;
- **read-out**: reading every stored value.

The parts are accumulated across switches between entering and operating calls, as in round 2. Destroying the class and reading the workload are not timed.

### 11.4 The profiling build

As v0.2 §13.4, with every approved number carried over (K = 32, seed 20261029, fenced TSC reads, probe cost over 1,000 blocks of 1,000 reads, slowdown limit 1.5), for all three rows. The phases are adapted to each structure:

| Phase | TC | Q32-index | Q32-hash |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F** operand fetch | as v0.2 | the rationals' fields; the case's whole numbers by index | the rationals' fields; the case's whole numbers by slot |
| **C**, **A.gcd**, **A.rest**, **S**, **U** | as v0.2 | as v0.2 | as v0.2 |

## 12. Correctness

### 12.1 Exact checks

As v0.2 §14.1, for all three rows:
1. every stored value of every run against Python's `fractions`;
2. against the generator's prediction (the model `test/r2_model.py`, extended):
   - $W$ in both Q32 rows, after entry and at the end;
   - every case count and K counter, in all three rows;
   - Q32-index's whole-number list, in index order (by sha256);
   - Q32-index's searches (found and appended) and **entries examined**, per phase;
   - Q32-hash's table, slot by slot (by sha256);
   - Q32-hash's stores (found and placed), **probes** and longest store, per phase.

   The model simulates both structures exactly: it appends in arrival order, and it hashes with §8's function and probes linearly;
3. **call for call:** in the counting runs, the per-call gcds, gcd work, exact divisions, multiplications and additions of Q32-index's core and of Q32-hash's core MUST each equal TC's. *The gcd work of the three rows is therefore equal by construction* (rule-r3 item 7).
4. **Q32-index against Q32-hash:** the same read-out, the same K counters, cases and reads, the same number of `intern` calls with the same found/new split, and the same set of whole numbers.

### 12.2 Invariants and audits

- **TC:** as v0.2 §14.2.
- **Q32-index:** each handle below $W$; each rational in lowest terms with denominator at least 1; zero as $+0/1$; list (b) in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, its entries **distinct**, each used by a rational, and $W \le 32{,}768$. The full audit checks distinctness by sorting a copy of list (b) and comparing neighbours, which is $O(W \log W)$. A search per entry would be $O(W^2)$ per audit, too slow for the gate's schedule. The incremental audit checks each new entry against the list by the search of §7 item 3, which must return its own index.
- **Q32-hash:** each handle names a used slot; rationals as above; every used slot holds a value in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, is used by a rational, and is **found at its own slot** by the store's probe sequence from $h(v)$. This also shows that no value is stored twice, since a second copy lies on the same probe sequence. The full audit checks every slot, and that the used-slot count equals $W$ and the empty slots hold −1. The incremental audit needs no new structure: it checks only the slots referenced by the rationals added since the last audit (found from the count of rationals at the last audit, kept in the class header's audit state). Every new whole number is used by at least one of those rationals, so every newly used slot is among them. Each such slot MUST be used, hold a value in $[0, 2^{63}-1]$, and be found at its own slot by the probe sequence from $h(v)$. There is no persistent list of changed slots.

The audits change no counter (v0.2 §14.2).

### 12.3 Tests

As v0.2 §14.3 for all three rows, both switches included. The Q32 tests run for each Q32 row, plus:
- **capacity**, in both Q32 rows: 32,768 whole numbers stored and audited; the next new whole number aborts, by entry and by an operation. In Q32-hash, this is the full table: every slot used, with the next new value failing and a known value still found;
- **Q32-hash probing:** values sharing a home slot (found by search in the test), a probe sequence that wraps from slot 32,767 to slot 0, and a found value after several probes;
- **Q32-index search:** a value found at index 0, in the middle and at the end, and a new value after a full search;
- **the three rows against each other**, on random operands of every case: equal values, equal per-call arithmetic, and equal Q32 found/new splits.

**The gate:** as v0.2 §14.3, for all three rows on every job of rule-r3.

## 13. Limits

- **Q32-index:** at most 32,768 whole numbers; fewer than $2^{32}-1$ rationals.
- **Q32-hash:** at most 32,768 whole numbers (a full table); fewer than $2^{32}-1$ rationals.
- **TC:** fewer than $2^{32}-1$ pairs.

Exceeding any limit aborts with a message.

## 17. Amendment of Oct 29, 2026: 0 and 1 at fixed handles, and the special cases of 1 and 0

**Dated Oct 29, 2026, by the author's decisions, before any measurement of round 3.** Nothing of round 3 had been timed or run when this was written. The author first decided (1) 0 and 1 stored at creation at fixed handles, and (2) integer special cases in all three rows. A review of the parallel paper edit then fixed the order of the cases by the cost of their result. The author then clarified that the new special cases are exactly two families: a denominator equal to 1, in addition and multiplication, and a numerator equal to 0, with no arithmetic; division gets no integer case of its own. This section records the result. The rule's revision of the same date is at the top of [`research/rule-r3.md`](research/rule-r3.md).

### 17.1 What changes

- Both Q32 rows: 0 and 1 are stored in `create`, at handles 0 and 1 (§17.2).
- All three rows: the special cases of §17.3, with integer and zero tests among the comparisons.
- Everything else stands: the structures and their count, the textbook search and hashing, the fixed table and its size, the capacity, the measures, and the identical arithmetic, call for call, across the three rows.

### 17.2 0 and 1 at fixed handles

1. **Q32-index.** `create` allocates list (b) at its first capacity of 16 entries (§7 item 4) and stores 0 at index 0 and 1 at index 1, before any rational is entered. Everything else is appended after them, as before. The search (§7 item 3) is unchanged: it finds 0 after 1 entry examined and 1 after 2.
2. **Q32-hash.** `create` empties the table (§8 item 2) and stores 0 at slot 0 and 1 at slot 1. Those two slots are reserved. A store checks first whether the value is 0 or 1. If it is, its handle is known: it is found with no slot read, and counts 0 probes. Any other value is hashed and probed as in §8 item 3. Slots 0 and 1 are never empty, so the probe passes over them as over any used slot, and no other value is ever placed there.
3. **Why the exclusion is lifted.** 0 and 1 are always relevant: every integer has the denominator 1, and every zero is $0/1$. Their handles are fixed by construction, as an index or a slot is, and are not remembered from earlier operations. Testing a handle against them is correct programming, not a trick.
4. **Counted honestly.** $W$ counts both values in both rows, used or not.
   - Q32-index: $B = 4 + 8W/N$ as before, with $W$ including 0 and 1. `create` now allocates the list's first 16 entries (128 bytes), which `create`'s own time includes (§11.3).
   - Q32-hash: the table is still 256 KB, counted in full. The fill is $W/32{,}768$, including the two slots.
   - **Capacity stays 32,768 handles, 0 and 1 included:** at most 32,766 other whole numbers. A full table is still all 32,768 slots used.
   - Effect on round 2's jobs (exact, by the model): $W$ grows by 1 on jobs 1, 3, 4 and 10, where 0 was not otherwise stored, and is unchanged on the others, which store both already. Every fill changes by at most 0.0031% (table in §17.6).
5. **Counters.** A Q32-hash store of 0 or 1 is *found* with 0 probes. §11.1's "at least 1 per store" applies to the other values. The longest store is unchanged in meaning.
6. **Audits** (§12.2), both Q32 rows: handles 0 and 1 hold 0 and 1, in every audit. They are exempt from "used by a rational". No other handle holds 0 or 1. In Q32-hash, slots 0 and 1 are checked by value, not by a probe sequence: $h(1) \ne 1$.
7. **Division's zero test** (v0.2 §10.3 note 1) is made in both Q32 rows on the handle: a numerator handle of 0 means zero. $c$ is still read before the comparisons, as before, so the reads are unchanged.

*Note.* Q32-hash's store tests $v = 0 \lor v = 1$ before hashing because handles 0 and 1 are fixed and 1's home slot $h(1)$ is not slot 1; this is a test in the store, not a structure.

### 17.3 The special cases: a denominator of 1, a numerator of 0

Two families, tested by value in TC and by handle in both Q32 rows:
- **an integer:** the denominator is 1 (TC: $b = 1$; Q32: the denominator's handle is 1);
- **a zero:** the numerator is 0 (TC: $a = 0$; Q32: the numerator's handle is 0).

All four tests ($b = 1$, $d = 1$, $a = 0$, $c = 0$) are made with the comparisons of §9, before a case is chosen, and counted with them. So `cmp` is 8 per addition or subtraction (was 4) and 10 per multiplication or division (was 6). The shortcuts switch of the test build turns them off with the others.

Notation as v0.2 §10: $x = s_x\,a/b$, $y = s_y\,c/d$, magnitudes in lowest terms, with subtraction flipping $s_y$.

**The cases, derived exactly** (each result is in lowest terms as stated):

| Case | Condition | Result | Why lowest terms | Arithmetic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| add `int_both` | $b = d = 1$ | $(s_x a + s_y c)/1$ | denominator 1 | 1 addition |
| add `int_x` | $b = 1$ | $(s_x a d + s_y c)/d$ | $\gcd(s_x a d + s_y c,\ d) = \gcd(c, d) = 1$ | 1 multiplication, 1 addition, **no gcd** |
| add `int_y` | $d = 1$ | $(s_x a + s_y c b)/b$ | $\gcd(s_x a + s_y c b,\ b) = \gcd(a, b) = 1$ | 1 multiplication, 1 addition, **no gcd** |
| add `zero_x` | $a = 0$ | $s_y\,y$: $y$'s handles, sign applied (tag + if $y = 0$) | $y$ is | none |
| add `zero_y` | $c = 0$ | $x$: its handles and tag | $x$ is | none |
| multiply `int_both` | $b = d = 1$ | $s\,(a c)/1$ | denominator 1 | 1 multiplication, **no gcd** |
| multiply `int_x` | $b = 1$ | $s\,(a' c)/d'$, $h = \gcd(a, d)$, $a' = a/h$, $d' = d/h$ | $\gcd(c, b) = \gcd(c, 1) = 1$, so only **one** cross gcd | 1 gcd, 2 exact divisions (skipped if $h = 1$, K-8), 1 multiplication |
| multiply `int_y` | $d = 1$ | $s\,(a c')/b'$, $h = \gcd(c, b)$ | as `int_x`, mirrored | as `int_x` |
| multiply `zero` | $a = 0$ or $c = 0$ | $0/1$ | | none |
| divide `zero` | $a = 0$ (and $c \ne 0$ by the zero-divisor test) | $0/1$ | | none |

Subtraction follows through addition, as before. **Division has no integer case** (the author's clarification). It keeps its zero-divisor precondition and its cases of v0.2 §10.3, and it gets the zero case above, as multiplication by the flipped divisor would. *Finding, recorded for the author:* dividing by an integer, $a/b \div c/1$ with $b \ne 1$, is **not** reached as a one-gcd case. Unless an earlier case applies (one_x, one_y, both, cross_ad, cross_cb, num, den), it takes case general: $\gcd(a, c)$ and the trivial $\gcd(b, 1)$, and two multiplications ($a' \cdot 1$ and $b \cdot c'$). Two integers take case den: $a/c$ after $\gcd(a, c)$, with no multiplication.

**Order: by the cost of the result, cheapest first.** The cost of a case's result is its gcds, then its other arithmetic (exact divisions, multiplications, additions). Ties go to the cheaper test, then to v0.2 §9's order. Two exceptions put `zero` first, with no effect on cost: addition's `zero` ($x + (-x)$) is checked before `zero_x` and `zero_y`, and multiplication's and division's `zero` before `one_x` and `one_y`. The tests are made together and counted alike, and the cases overlap only where both results cost nothing and are equal ($0 - 0$; $\pm 1 \cdot 0$, $0 \cdot \pm 1$, $0 \div \pm 1$). All three rows check in this order:
- **add and subtract:** `zero` ($x + (-x)$) · `zero_x` · `zero_y` · `int_both` · `int_x` · `int_y` · `cross` · `den` · `num` · `general`;
- **multiply:** `zero` · `one_x` · `one_y` · `int_both` · `both` (reciprocals, opposite reciprocals included) · `den` · `num` ($c = \pm a$) · `cross_ad` · `cross_cb` · `int_x` · `int_y` · `general`;
- **divide** (after the zero-divisor test): `zero` · `one_x` · `one_y` · `both` · `cross_ad` · `cross_cb` · `num` · `den` · `general`.

One point differs from the order the review proposed, which put "one integer" before "the cross matches" in multiplication. Where both apply, the cross match gives a cheaper result: one gcd and **no** multiplication, against one gcd and one multiplication. For example, take $2/1 \cdot 3/2$. `cross_ad` makes $\gcd(3, 1)$ and returns $3/1$. `int_x` would make $\gcd(2, 2) = 2$, two divisions and $1 \cdot 3$. Under the stated principle, cost of result first, the cross matches come first. The exhaustive test of §17.5 confirms that with this order, no case is ever taken when another applicable case gives a cheaper result.

**Known answers and counters.**
- **K-8** also covers multiplication's `int_x` and `int_y` with $h = 1$: the same skip, one cross gcd of 1 and its two divisions.
- **K-2's skipped arithmetic** is that of the case taken without it: none for $0 - 0$ (`zero_x`), one addition for an integer $x$ (`int_both`), and v0.2's case den otherwise. $\pm 1/1 \mp 1/1$ now falls to `int_both`, not `cross`.
- **K-13:** multiplication's and division's `zero` apply no sign. Addition's `zero_x` and `zero_y` apply one.
- **Hints (K-Q1)** in both Q32 rows:
  - `int_both` of addition and multiplication: 1's handle, as the result's denominator;
  - `int_x` and `int_y` of addition, and the $h = 1$ path of multiplication's: the denominator handle of the operand that is not an integer, which is the result's denominator;
  - `zero_x` and `zero_y`: the other operand's two handles, with nothing read when the hints and K-13 are on, as in v0.2's `one_x` and `one_y`.

  The zero results of multiplication and division are stored like addition's `zero` result: 0 and 1 are found at once.
- **The cases are appended** to round 2's numbering (`lib/include/r2.h`, round-3 builds only), so every old case keeps its number in the case files.
