## 19. Amendment of Oct 30, 2026: Packed-coprime

**Build language** (item 10). The build moves to Nim. The spec is language-neutral: it names no language, source file, prefix or routine as a requirement. Where v0.3 and v0.4 name C files (`mh3.c`, `mh4.c`, `tcm.c`, `sk_gcd_raw`), they stay places to check, not requirements.

### 19.0 Names and terms

| Name | What | Here |
| --- | --- | --- |

| **Packed** | v0.4's model (mh4) | run |

| **Packed-coprime** | Packed plus denominators factored over a coprime base of the matrix's own denominators. Formerly Pack-coprime (v0.3 §16.1 row 4), which this replaces (§19.14) | specified here; run |

**Terms** (as v0.3 §16.2 and v0.5, with these). The letters $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, $P$, $Q$, $R$, $S$, $U$ stay the matrices' names, and $K$ stays v0.1's bound.
- **Coprime base** (the standard term; also called a gcd-free basis): a set of whole numbers above 1 that are pairwise coprime, such that each number of a given set is a product of their powers.
- **Base** $\Gamma$: a matrix's own coprime base of its denominators (§19.3). Item 11 splits off the primes 2 and 3, so it is not always the coarsest coprime base of the matrix's denominators. $\Gamma[j]$ is its piece at slot $j$, and $\Gamma_L$, $\Gamma_R$ are a step's operands' bases.
- **Piece**: an element of a base (v0.3's term). A **prime piece** is one found by trial division (item 11), or a known prime (C14). Every other piece is treated as composite, whether or not it is.
- **The trial-division bound** $T = 3$: pieces are trial-divided by the primes 2 and 3 (item 11, §19.4).
- **Known prime** (C14): a piece that has no factor up to $T$ and is below $25 = 5^2$, the square of the next prime. It is therefore prime, and it is flagged as a prime piece. Under the draw, 5 and 7 are known prime when they become pieces.
- **Slot**: a piece's position in its base, $0, \ldots, \lvert \Gamma \rvert - 1$. An entry's exponents are positional: its exponent at slot $j$ is the exponent of $\Gamma[j]$.
- **$\kappa$**: the largest number of pieces a base may hold. $\kappa = 4$ (item 3, §19.10).
- **Absorb** (from 09's design, v0.3 §16.4 item 4): the scan that makes a whole number a product of a base's pieces, refining the base where it must (§19.4).
- **Split**: replacing one piece by the pieces of a finer coprime base, when a new number shares part of it (§19.4).
- **Merged base** $\Theta$: the coprime base of the union of a step's two operand bases, made at the start of the step (§19.5).
- **Unfactored part** (v0.5 item 13): the part of a value that is not written over the base. A stored denominator has none: it is always a product of its matrix's pieces (§19.4). A temporary's denominator, or a gcd, may have one (§19.6).
- **Leftover** (v0.3 §16.8): a gcd with a composite piece that is not a product of pieces' powers. Example, under the draw: the piece $35 = 5 \cdot 7$ and $\gcd(5, 35) = 5$. With item 11, a leftover needs a piece treated as composite. Under the draw, such a piece is a product of 5s and 7s of at least 25.
- **Factor work**: the counted work of finding and using factors, counted apart from the core (v0.5 §18.5, C6).

### 19.2 Why (what rounds m3 and m4 found; not decisions)

- **Every gcd of the gcd-first arithmetic has a denominator, or a divisor of one, as an argument** (v0.3 §16.0). So denominators' factors are enough for every reduction (item 2).
- **Pack-coprime's eager base refinement was 84–92% of every operation** (m3-cost-breakdown, weak point 2). Pass 1 scanned a base of up to 4,579 pieces per number at $n = 64$, almost all of them pieces of numerators. **With denominators only, a base has at most 4 pieces under this draw** (the survey, §1 item 2; proved in §19.10). So pass 1's scan is at most 4 pieces long (items 2, 3 and 6).
- **Pass 2 repeated pass 1's scan as divisions** (weak point 3; the fix named there is "record the exponents during pass 1"). Item 4 does that.
- **Exponent merges saved nothing.** Pack-primes' merge path cost 192M instructions on mul at $n = 64$, against Control's whole binary-gcd bill of 151M (weak point 8). Item 5 rules merges out.
- **v0.3 had four structures per Pack-coprime matrix** ((a), (l), (q), (f)) and a fifth per step ((r)). Item 3 keeps one stored structure. The survey shows that (r) is not needed when only denominators are factored: each base is then made of denominator factors only, so $D_X$ is the base itself (survey §1 item 1).
- **Reconciliation was at most 3% in m3** (weak point 10), but m3 repeated whole passes and rebuilt (f) once per split per holder (survey C0). Item 7 takes the survey's recommendation:
  - survey C2: merge one coprime base into the other;
  - survey C3: equal pieces first;
  - survey C4: one rewrite per operand;
  - survey C5: seed the result's base.
- **Where reconciliation matters** (the survey's simulation, §1 item 5, non-normative): bases converge to the primes once a matrix has more than a few dozen distinct denominators. Mismatches come only from small pools ($s = n^2 / \rho \le 64$). Typical ones are 49 against 7, 75 against $\{3, 5\}$, and 21 against $\{3, 7\}$.
- **Item 11 is survey C9 in a bounded form:** small primes are split off by trial division, but only 2 and 3, Packed-23's known primes. The survey's risk was that splitting off every prime of the draw (2, 3, 5 and 7) would leave the general machinery unexercised. With the bound 3, 5 and 7 are still found through gcds, and splits, rewrites and leftovers do occur under the draw, though rarely (the next item).
- **How often, under the draw** (the coordinator's simulation of the bound 3 with C14; non-normative). It uses pooled draws that follow v0.4 §17.9's steps but not its exact call order, so the figures are statistical, from 4–40 jobs per point:

  | Pool $s = n^2/\rho$ | Splits in pass 1, per input | $A$ and $B$ bases identical (R1 alone) | Steps needing a rewrite | Reconciliation gcds per step | Reconciliation splits per step | Composite pieces per base | S2 on mul's cross gcds |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 4 ($n = 16$, $\rho = 64$) | 0.07 | 8% | 15% | 1.35 | 0.12 | 0.24 of 2.35 | 95.7% |
  | 16 ($n = 16$, $\rho = 16$) | 0.36 | 60% | 8% | 0.12 | 0 | 0.06 of 3.81 | 99.1% |
  | 16 ($n = 32$, $\rho = 64$) | 0.28 | 65% | 10% | 0.25 | 0.07 | 0.07 of 3.88 | 99.7% |
  | 64 ($n = 32$, $\rho = 16$; $n = 64$, $\rho = 64$) | 0.40–0.42 | 100% | 0% | 0 | 0 | 0 of 4 | 100% |
  | 256–4096 (the rest of the grid) | 0.3–1.5 | 100% | 0% | 0 | 0 | 0 of 4 | 100% |

  - Splits in pass 1 happen in every job, at about 0.1–1.5 per input: for example, 35 met before 5, or 25 before 5.
  - Reconciliation does real work only at pools $s \le 16$.
  - The result of an add step, seeded from $\Theta$, made no split in any job simulated.
  - Without C14, 5 and 7 would never be known prime. S2 would then fire on 62–78% of the same gcds, instead of 96–100%.
- **The survey's other candidates are not taken** (items 6, 7 and 11 name the ones taken):
  - survey C1, naive refinement of the union;
  - survey C6, lazy refinement per cell;
  - survey C7, one base shared across matrices;
  - survey C8, perfect-power-free pieces;
  - survey C10, full primes by trial division to $\sqrt{q}$;
  - Bernstein's product and remainder trees.
- **Why the last gcd needed a special case** (the first draft's open point 2, now item 11b). With composite pieces, S1 cannot know whether the pieces left in the denominator share a factor with the numerator. So it always ran its last gcd, and in the common case that was Control's own gcd. Once the pieces are known to be prime, a piece whose division test failed shares nothing with what is left of the numerator, and the gcd can be skipped.
- **The inner sums of mul were about 70% of Control's gcd work in multiplication, on m3's draw** (v0.3 §16.6 item 4). Item 8 brings them within reach of S1.
- **The coordinator's check** (untimed, a simulation in Python, not in the repo):
  - absorb with the nested split of C3 gives exactly the coprime base that naive refinement gives, on 4,000 sets of numbers with up to 6 primes, before item 11;
  - "base full" fired exactly when that base had more than $\kappa$ pieces;
  - every recorded exponent vector multiplies back to its number;
  - reconciliation's $\Theta$ equals the coprime base of the union, and every operand entry, re-expressed, multiplies back to its value;
  - S1 equals $\gcd(u, z)$, and the quotient rule's factors multiply back to $z / k$, on 200,000 random cases with prime and composite pieces;
  - with `enter` at the bound 3, on 20,000 sets, every base is pairwise coprime, every piece flagged prime is prime, and every exponent vector multiplies back.

### 19.3 The packed list's entries (items 3 and 4; 1c; C1)

**During the build**, (l) is exactly Packed's: $2rc$ whole numbers of 64 bits, with nothing else. All of v0.4 §17.3–17.5 applies as written. **At the pack** (v0.4 §17.3, unchanged): (h) is freed; the list is narrowed to $w$ and shrunk to $W$ entries; the fields are narrowed. Then §19.4's pass changes the list's layout.

**After §19.4's pass**, the list has $W + \lvert \Gamma \rvert$ entries of the same size:
- entries $0, \ldots, W - 1$: the whole numbers, by handle, as in Packed;
- entries $W, \ldots, W + \lvert \Gamma \rvert - 1$: **the base's pieces**, slot $j$ at entry $W + j$. So the base is found without a scan.

$\lvert \Gamma \rvert \le \kappa$ is kept beside $W$ and $w$ in the matrix's header, as $W$ is. A header field is not a structure.

**Entry $k$** has these fixed fields, in this order (coordinator's call C1):

| # | Field | Symbol | Width | Byte offset in the entry | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | flag | $\phi_k$ | 1 byte | 0 | 0: a whole number with no factors (a numerator only, or the mark's 0); 1: a **factored denominator**; 2: a **piece** treated as composite; 3: a **prime piece** (item 11a, C13, C14) |
| 2 | whole number | $v_k$ | $w$ bits | 1 | the value, as in Packed; for a piece, the piece |
| 3–6 | exponents | $e_{k,0}, \ldots, e_{k,3}$ | 1 byte each | $1 + w/8 + j$ for slot $j$ | when $\phi_k = 1$: $v_k = \prod_j \Gamma[j]^{e_{k,j}}$; 0 otherwise, and 0 at every slot $j \ge \lvert \Gamma \rvert$ |

- **Size:** no padding and no alignment, so an entry is $E(w) = w/8 + 1 + \kappa = w/8 + 5$ bytes (see §19.9). Entry $k$ starts at byte $k\,E(w)$, and its whole number at byte $k\,E(w) + 1$.
- **No unfactored part field.** A stored denominator is always a product of its matrix's pieces, since pass 1 makes the base from these very denominators. Its unfactored part is 1, so it is not stored. This is where the layout differs from Packed-23's (v0.5 §18.3).
- **A piece's entry is never a cell's handle.** A piece may equal a whole number of the list (the denominator 7 and the piece 7) and still has its own entry. *Why* (C1): a split rewrites a piece's value in place, and a cell's whole number never changes.
- **What the fields' facts give** (not decisions): for $v < 2^{63}$ and pieces of at least 2, $\sum_j e_{k,j} \le 62$. So each exponent fits one byte.
- **Width of a piece:** every piece kept in a stored base divides some denominator of its matrix, since the drop keeps only used pieces (see §19.4). So it fits at the list's width $w$.
- **Reads by handle** read $v_k$ at width $w$, sign-extended, as Packed reads (l), and the other fields at their widths.

### 19.4 Finding the factors: the pass after the pack (items 2, 3, 4, 6, 7d and 11; C2, C3, C13, C14)

**When:** in one pass at the end of each matrix's build, right after the pack. Each matrix is factored before the next build begins.
- For an input: inside its last `enter` call.
- For an intermediate or a result: at the end of its `madd` or `mmul` call.

**The working base** (C2, C10) is the base $\Gamma$ while pass 1 runs. It is held as data: at most $\kappa$ pieces of 64 bits, with one "used" bit and one "prime" bit per slot. It starts:
- **empty**, for an input;
- **as the step's merged base $\Theta$** (§19.5), with its prime bits, for an intermediate or a result (item 7d). Every used bit starts at 0.

**The "used" bits** (C2): slot $t$ is used when some entry with $\phi = 1$ has a nonzero exponent there. They are kept up to date as the pass goes:
- **when absorb records** an exponent $i > 0$ at slot $t$ into an entry, it sets used[$t$];
- **when a split re-expresses** the entries (below), its pass visits every entry with $\phi = 1$, and the current one. It recomputes the used bits of the split's derived slots from the exponents it writes there. No other slot's bit changes, since no other slot's exponents change.

**The pass, in this order** (coordinator's call C2):
1. **Grow.** The list's allocation grows from $W\,w/8$ to $(W + \kappa)\,E(w)$ bytes. This is an **accounting event**, as v0.5 C8 counts its grow.
2. **Rewrite in place**, as v0.5 C1: one backward pass, $k = W - 1, \ldots, 0$. Entry $k$'s whole number is read from byte $k\,w/8$. Entry $k$ is then written at byte $k\,E(w)$, with $\phi_k = 0$ and its exponents 0. v0.5 §18.4 gives the reason nothing is lost; it holds for any $E(w) \ge w/8$.
3. **Pass 1: walk the cells' denominator fields**, as v0.5 C8, in cell order (row-major). For each cell, take its denominator's handle $k$:
   - if $\phi_k = 0$ and $v_k \ne 0$: $\text{absorb}(v_k, \text{entry } k\text{'s exponents}, \varnothing)$, then set $\phi_k = 1$;
   - otherwise go on. So each whole number used as a denominator is factored once, at its first meeting. **The mark's guard** (v0.5 item 12): the whole number 0 is never absorbed, since $\gcd(0, q) = q$ would never let the loop end.
4. **Drop every unused piece**, whether it was seeded from $\Theta$ or made in this pass: every slot whose used bit is 0. If any is dropped, the kept slots are renumbered in order, $0, 1, \ldots$. Then **one call of `remap`** (below) moves the exponents of the entries with $\phi = 1$ to the new slots.
   - A dropped slot's exponent is 0 in every entry, so nothing is lost.
   - If nothing is dropped, `remap` is not called: there is nothing to move.
5. **Write the base:** piece $j$ at entry $W + j$, with $\phi = 3$ for a prime piece and $\phi = 2$ otherwise, $v$ the piece, and exponents 0.
6. **Shrink** the list to $(W + \lvert \Gamma \rvert)\,E(w)$ bytes: an accounting event.

**`divide_out`** is v0.5 §18.4's routine, unchanged in its steps. Its header note there says "$p$ is 2 or 3". Here it is widened to any piece.
- **Precondition:** $p \ge 2$; $m \ge 0$; and $m \ge 1$ when the limit is $\infty$. With $m = 0$ and no limit, $0 \bmod p = 0$ always, and the loop would never stop.
- **Every call here meets it:**
  - absorb calls it with `rest` above 1;
  - `enter` with $x > 1$;
  - the split with $x \ge 1$, from $q$ down;
  - S1 calls it with a finite limit, $e_j(z)$, so $u' = 0$ is allowed there.

**absorb** (item 4; from v0.3 §16.4 item 4, with the exponents recorded). The routine is shared by pass 1, the split and reconciliation (item 7a). $\Gamma$ is the base it works on: the working base in pass 1, $\Theta$ in reconciliation.

```text
function absorb(v, target, skip)        v ≥ 1; Γ pairwise coprime
                                        target: the exponents to record v's into, or none
                                        skip: slots not to scan (§19.5); ∅ in pass 1
    let rest = v, j = 0
    while rest > 1 and j < |Γ|
        if j ∈ skip: j <- j + 1; continue
        let q = Γ[j], h = gcd(rest, q)              the new gcd (v0.4 §17.7)
        if h = 1
            j <- j + 1
        else if h = q
            (rest, i, ·) = divide_out(rest, q, ∞)   rest > 1, so the precondition holds
            target[j] <- target[j] + i              stay at j; in pass 1, set used[j]
        else
            split(j, h, target)                     1 < h < q; stay at j
    if rest > 1: enter(rest, |Γ|, target)           rest is coprime to every piece
end function
```

- The scan never goes back. It ends when `rest` is 1, or after the last slot, which may be one a split or `enter` appended.
- On return, $v = \prod_j \Gamma[j]^{\text{target}[j]}$, and $\Gamma$ is still pairwise coprime.

**enter: a new piece, split into primes when cheap** (item 11a; coordinator's calls C13 and C14). Every piece enters a base through this routine: an absorbed remainder, and a split's first part.

```text
function enter(x, j, y)                x > 1, coprime to every piece of Γ other than slot j's
                                       j: the slot for the first piece (|Γ| to append); y: a vector, or none
    for p = 2, 3                                      the trial-division bound T = 3
        (x, a, ·) = divide_out(x, p, ∞)
        if a > 0: place p at slot j, marked prime; y[j] <- y[j] + a (if y); j <- |Γ|
    if x > 1: place x at slot j, marked prime if x < 25 (C14), otherwise not; y[j] <- y[j] + 1 (if y)
end function
```

- **place** writes the piece at slot $j$. If $j = \lvert \Gamma \rvert$, it appends, and appending to a base of $\kappa$ pieces stops with the error "base full" (§19.10). A recorded exponent sets the slot's used bit in pass 1, as absorb's does.
- *Exact:* $x = 2^{a_2} 3^{a_3} x'$, and the parts are pairwise coprime. $x$ is coprime to every other piece, so they are too, and the base stays pairwise coprime.
- **The cofactor** $x'$ has no prime factor up to 3. It stays as it is, one piece.
  - Below 25 it is prime: its least prime factor is at least 5, and $5^2 = 25$. So it is flagged prime (C14).
  - From 25 up it is treated as composite, as every piece was in the first draft. It may in fact be prime (29) or not (25, 35); nothing more tests it.
- **Under the draw**, a cofactor is $5^a 7^b$. So the pieces are:
  - 2 and 3, prime;
  - 5 and 7, known prime;
  - composite pieces such as 25, 35, 49 and 175.

  A composite piece splits when a later value shares part of it (35, then 5). These splits, and the leftovers and rewrites they cause, do occur under the draw (non-normative frequencies in §19.2's table). Once a matrix's pieces are all prime, its base is $\subseteq \{2, 3, 5, 7\}$, and it splits no more.
- *Cost:* at most $2 + \sum_p a_p$ remainders per entering piece ($T = 3$, so $p \in \{2, 3\}$), with $\sum_p a_p$ exact divisions and multiplications, plus C14's one comparison. All of it is factor work. Pieces enter rarely: at most $\kappa$ per base and per working base, plus what splits make.
- **The risk** (item 11c). With larger primes in a workload, trial division's cost would return: m3's Pack-primes spent 97–98% of its instructions on trial division (m3-cost-breakdown, answer 3). The fixed bound caps it here at two primes per entering piece. Pieces with larger primes stay as they are, and lose S2 (§19.6) unless known prime.

**split** (coordinator's call C3). It replaces piece $q = \Gamma[j]$ by the coprime base of $\{h, q/h\}$ (v0.3 §16.4 item 4), made by absorb itself, with item 11's primes split off:

```text
function split(j, h, cur)              q = Γ[j]; 1 < h < q; h divides q
                                       cur: the vector being recorded, or none
    let q = Γ[j], m = |Γ|                         m: the number of slots before the split
    enter(h, j, none)                             h's pieces: the first at slot j, the rest appended
    absorb(q / h, none, {0, ..., m - 1} \ {j})    q/h divides q, so it is coprime to every other old piece
    if cur is none: return                        a nested split (inside this absorb): nothing recorded refers to it
    the derived slots: j, and m, ..., |Γ| - 1     the pieces made from q
    let x = q
    for each derived slot t: (x, a_t, ·) = divide_out(x, Γ[t], ∞)    q = prod Γ[t]^a_t; x ends at 1
    re-express every recorded vector y, and cur:  let ν = y[j]
        if ν > 0: y[j] <- 0, then y[t] <- y[t] + ν · a_t for each derived slot t
    in pass 1: recompute used[t] for each derived slot t from the vectors just visited
end function
```

- **Split only happens at a piece treated as composite.** A prime piece $p$ gives $h = 1$ or $h = p$.
- **Nesting:** absorb may call split, which calls absorb on a strictly smaller number, so the depth is at most $\log_2 q < 63$. Each call holds a few numbers. Recursion depth is not a structure (C3). Only the outermost split, whose absorb has a target, re-expresses. No vector refers to the pieces that nested splits make, because those pieces are made during the split.
- **The recorded vectors:**
  - in pass 1: every entry with $\phi = 1$ so far. Re-expressing them is **one pass over the $W$ entries per split** (coordinator's call C3), which also recomputes the derived slots' used bits. This is the simplest correct option. Item 7c's batching cannot carry over to pass 1, because a split piece's slot is reused by its first new piece;
  - in reconciliation: the tracked vectors of §19.5, a fixed handful.
- **Why it is exact:** $q = h \cdot (q/h)$, and after the inner absorb both $h$ and $q/h$ are products of the derived pieces. So $q$ is too, and $q^\nu = \prod_t \Gamma[t]^{\nu a_t}$. No piece outside the derived slots changes.
- **Why "base full" is judged on the working base** (a fact). "Base full" fires when `enter` would place a piece beyond $\kappa$ in the working base (C7). In a result's pass the working base holds the seeded pieces of $\Theta$ too, so they count, even those that the drop would later remove.
  - Within one working base the number of pieces never falls before the drop. A split replaces one piece by at least one; `enter` places one piece or more; nothing else removes a piece.
  - So a working base that would exceed $\kappa$ at some point would exceed it at the end of pass 1 too, before the drop.
- **Nothing more** (items 6 and 11):
  - no index on the base, no lazy refinement, no product or remainder trees;
  - no trial division beyond 3, no primality test beyond C14's one comparison, no table of primes beyond the two constants.

**remap** (coordinator's call C2; one routine for the drop and for reconciliation's rewrite, §19.5):

```text
function remap(X, vec, n')             vec[j]: X's old slot j as exponents over n' new slots
    for each entry k of X with φ_k = 1
        let y = 0 (n' exponents)                      a scratch vector
        for each old slot j with e_{k,j} > 0: y <- y + e_{k,j} · vec[j]
        write y as entry k's exponents, 0 at every slot ≥ n'
end function
```

- **For the drop:** $\text{vec}[j]$ is the kept slot $j$'s new number, with exponent 1. A dropped slot has exponent 0 in every entry, so its vector is never read.
- **For the rewrite:** $\text{vec}[j]$ is $\text{vec}_X[j]$, restricted to $\Gamma'_X$'s slots (§19.5).
- *Exact:* entry $k$'s value is $\prod_j \Gamma[j]^{e_{k,j}}$, and $\Gamma[j] = \prod_t \Gamma'[t]^{\text{vec}[j][t]}$. So it is $\prod_t \Gamma'[t]^{\sum_j e_{k,j} \text{vec}[j][t]}$.
- *Cost:* one pass over the $W$ entries.

**Free factors** (item 2): a numerator equal to a denominator of the same matrix is the same entry, so it has the denominator's exponents with no extra work. Numerators are never absorbed.

**Timing:** the grow, the rewrite, pass 1, the drop, the write and the shrink are part of measure 1 for an input, and of measures 2–5 for an intermediate or result. They are never part of the workload's generation.

**Cost** (facts):
- one read per cell's denominator field;
- per factored entry, at most about two gcds per slot (one that finds $h = q$, and one after the division), so at most about $2\kappa = 8$, plus `divide_out`'s remainders, divisions and multiplications. m3's pass 1 scanned the whole base, about $0.3\,W\,\lvert q \rvert$ gcds per matrix (m3-cost-breakdown, answer 4);
- each split: its nested gcds, its pieces' trial divisions, at most $\kappa$ `divide_out` calls, and one re-expressing pass over the entries in pass 1;
- the drop: one `remap` pass, only when a piece is dropped.

*Expected* (non-normative, §19.2): about 0.1–1.5 splits per input under the draw.

### 19.5 Reconciliation (item 7; C6)

**Where:** first thing in every step with two stored operands $L$ and $R$, timed in the operation:
- add jobs and mul jobs;
- sop's $A \cdot B$, $C \cdot D$ and $P + Q$;
- pos's $A + B$, $C + D$ and $S \cdot U$.

**What it makes:** the merged base $\Theta$, the base of $\Gamma_L \cup \Gamma_R$, with item 11's primes split off through `enter`. $\Theta$ is used:
- by every factor the step reads or forms, over $\Theta$'s slots (C6), including mul's temporaries (item 8, §19.6);
- by the result's pass 1, as its starting working base (item 7d, §19.4).

```text
function reconcile(L, R)
    Θ <- Γ_L, in L's slots, with its prime bits; for each slot j of L: vec_L[j] <- slot j alone, exponent 1
    matched <- ∅
    for each piece r_m of Γ_R, in R's slots:                                      R1 (item 7b)
        if r_m = Θ[j] for some j: σ_R[m] <- j, matched <- matched ∪ {j}, vec_R[m] <- slot j alone
    for each unmatched piece r_m of Γ_R, in R's slots:                            item 7a
        vec_R[m] <- 0; absorb(r_m, vec_R[m], matched)
            (its splits re-express vec_L, the vec_R made so far, and vec_R[m])
    for X in L, R:
        if every vec_X[j] is one slot t with exponent 1: σ_X[j] <- t; X is not rewritten
        else: rewrite X (item 7c, below), in every step (C6)
end function
```

- **The branch "X is not rewritten" is part of item 7c**, not a special case. Item 7c rewrites a matrix once for all its splits. An operand none of whose pieces changed has no split to apply, so its one rewrite is no rewrite.
- **Order of $\Theta$** (C6): $L$'s slots first, where each split piece's first new piece keeps its slot (§19.4); then the pieces appended, in the order made.
- **Correct:** at the end, $\Theta$ is pairwise coprime, and every piece of $\Gamma_L \cup \Gamma_R$ is a product of its pieces' powers. Each $L$ piece is $\prod_t \Theta[t]^{\text{vec}_L[j][t]}$, and each $R$ piece likewise. Only one side's pieces are pushed into the other's, since pairs within a side are already coprime (survey C2).
- **Why matched slots can be skipped:** if $r_m$ equals the $L$ piece $q$, then $q$ is coprime to every other piece of $R$, because $r_m$ is. So no unmatched $r$ needs a gcd with $q$, and no split ever touches $q$ (survey C3's proof).

**Special case R1, equal pieces (item 7b).**
- *Condition:* a piece of $R$ equals a piece of $L$, by value.
- *Path:* it is matched by a comparison, with no gcd. Its slot in $\Theta$ is the $L$ piece's slot, and no gcd of reconciliation scans that slot: it is in absorb's skip set, and every nested split's skip set holds it too, as an old slot.
- *When the bases are identical* (every piece of $R$ matched, and $\lvert \Gamma_R \rvert = \lvert \Gamma_L \rvert$): reconciliation is the comparisons alone. There is no gcd, no split and no rewrite, and $\Theta = \Gamma_L$. $\sigma_L$ is the identity, and $\sigma_R$ is the matching, the identity when the two bases are in the same order.
- *Why it is faster:* up to $\lvert \Gamma_L \rvert \cdot \lvert \Gamma_R \rvert \le 16$ comparisons replace the gcds. The survey measured 0.24–2.4 gcds per step with matching first, against 4.7–15 for all pairs (survey C3).
- *Exact:* a comparison by value decides equality, and the coprimality follows from the proof above.
- *Expected under the draw* (non-normative, §19.2):
  - at pools $s \ge 64$, every pair of input bases simulated was $\{2, 3, 5, 7\}$ on both sides, so R1 alone did the step;
  - at pools $s \le 16$, 8–65% of steps had identical bases, and 8–15% needed a rewrite. Examples: $R$'s 35 against $L$'s 5 and 7, or $L$'s 25 against $R$'s 5.

**The rewrite (item 7c), in every step.** An operand $X$ is rewritten when some piece of $X$ is not a single piece of $\Theta$: it split, or it is a product of $L$'s pieces (an $R$ piece 35 against $L$'s 5 and 7). Then, **once for all its splits**:
1. $X$'s new base $\Gamma'_X$ is the slots $t$ of $\Theta$ with $\text{vec}_X[j][t] > 0$ for some $j$, in $\Theta$'s order (C6). So it holds only pieces that divide $X$'s old pieces, which fit $X$'s width $w$. $\sigma_X$ maps each slot of $\Gamma'_X$ to its slot in $\Theta$.
2. **`remap`** (§19.4) with $\text{vec}_X$, over $\Gamma'_X$'s $\lvert \Gamma'_X \rvert$ slots: one pass over $X$'s entries with $\phi = 1$.
3. $X$'s piece entries are replaced by $\Gamma'_X$, with their flags.

**The list never shrinks in a rewrite** (a fact). Each old piece of $X$ becomes at least one piece of $\Gamma'_X$, and two old pieces, being coprime, never share one. So $\lvert \Gamma'_X \rvert \ge \lvert \Gamma_X \rvert$.
- The list grows by $\lvert \Gamma'_X \rvert - \lvert \Gamma_X \rvert$ entries, at most $\kappa - 1$, since $\lvert \Gamma_X \rvert \ge 1$ whenever there is a piece to split.
- A growth is an accounting event.

- *Cost:* one pass over $W_X$ entries per rewritten operand per step, instead of one per split per holder (m3).
- *Values, cells and whole numbers never change*, nor do numerators' entries. Only the exponents of factored entries and the piece entries change. So matching Control value for value is unaffected (survey §3).
- *The operands are changed:* their bases become finer. A base only gets finer, so a later step with the same operand meets fewer splits. Inputs are rebuilt on every run, so nothing carries over between runs (v0.3 §16.7).

**One rule for every step** (coordinator's call C6, settling the first draft's open point 5). Add steps and mul steps reconcile, rewrite and read their factors over $\Theta$ in the same way. *Why:*
- it is the simplest correct option: one routine and one reading of factors in every step (RULES, Algorithm design 3);
- an add step does not need the rewrite for its arithmetic, since it forms no temporary with factors. But the rewrite is exact;
- the first draft's alternative, add steps merging without rewriting, needed a second way of reading factors.

**Cost** (facts):
- at most $\lvert \Gamma_L \rvert \cdot \lvert \Gamma_R \rvert \le 16$ comparisons;
- gcds only against unmatched slots: about $2\kappa$ per unmatched piece of $R$ at most. Absorb runs the gcd again after each `divide_out`, and it also scans the slots appended during the step;
- plus each split's nested gcds and each entering piece's trial division;
- at most two `remap` passes per step.

There is no (r), no scan of the cells and no repeat-until-stable loop: the survey's item 1 shows that $D_X = \Gamma_X$ once only denominators are factored.

### 19.6 Reduction and temporaries' factors (items 5, 8 and 11b; C4, C5, C9)

**Notation.** For a value $z$ whose factors are at hand, $F(z) = (e_0(z), \ldots, e_{\kappa-1}(z);\ r_z)$: its exponents over a base and its unfactored part, with $z = r_z \prod_j \Theta[j]^{e_j(z)}$. For a stored denominator, $r_z = 1$.

**Which base** (C6). In every step, every $F$ is over the merged base $\Theta$'s slots, and S1's pieces are $\Theta$'s, with their prime bits. A stored operand's exponents are mapped by its slot map: $e_{\sigma_X[j]} = e_{k,j}$.
- In mul, $A$'s are mapped once per row, into the row buffer (C9), and $B$'s by handle, at each read.
- In add, both operands' exponents are mapped by handle, at each read.
- The mapping moves at most $\kappa$ bytes. It is not counted as factor work.

**S1 for a base: the model's general path (item 5).** It gives $k = \gcd(u, z)$ for a value $u$ and a value $z$ with $F(z)$ at hand, over the pieces $\Theta[j]$.
- *Path:* it works on $\lvert u \rvert$, as the new gcd works on magnitudes.
  1. **Divide out the pieces:** $u' = \lvert u \rvert$, $\pi = 1$. For each slot $j$ with $e_j(z) > 0$: $(u', i_j, \pi_j) = \text{divide\_out}(u', \Theta[j], e_j(z))$, and $\pi \leftarrow \pi \cdot \pi_j$. Every other $i_j = 0$. $u'$ is what is left of the numerator.
  2. Unless S2 applies: **what is left of $z$**, $\zeta = z / \pi$, one exact division (coordinator's call C4).
  3. Unless S2 applies: $r_k = \gcd(u', \zeta)$, by the new gcd (v0.4 §17.7).
  4. $k = \pi \cdot r_k$, one multiplication, and $F(k) = (i_0, \ldots, i_{\kappa-1};\ r_k)$.
- *Exact:*
  - $\pi_j = \Theta[j]^{i_j}$ with $i_j \le e_j(z)$, and the pieces are pairwise coprime. So $\pi$ divides $z$, and $\pi$ divides $u$ by construction;
  - so $\gcd(u, z) = \gcd(\pi u', \pi \zeta) = \pi \gcd(u', \zeta)$;
  - every division is exact, and $\pi$, $\pi_j$ and $k$ divide $z$, so no step can overflow;
  - for $u = 0$, every loop stops at its limit, so $\pi = z / r_z$ and $\zeta = r_z$. Then $\gcd(0, \zeta) = \zeta$ and $k = z$, as Control's $\gcd(0, z) = z$.
- **Why $\zeta$ and not $r_z$** (C4). Packed-23's S1 runs its last gcd on the unfactored part $r_z$. That is exact there because 2 and 3 are prime: a prime whose loop stopped short does not divide $u'$. A piece treated as composite may. With the piece 35, $z = 35$ and $u = 5$, the loop finds $i = 0$, yet $\gcd(5, 35) = 5$. So the last gcd must see the pieces' powers that are left as well. When every piece is prime, $\gcd(u', \zeta) = \gcd(u', r_z)$, so C4 is Packed-23's S1 in that case. The author's words, "the new gcd on the unfactored part", are read here as "the new gcd on what is left of $z$" (C4).
- *What it costs:*
  - up to $\sum_j (e_j(z) + 1)$ remainders, and as many exact divisions and multiplications as `divide_out`'s steps succeed;
  - the products into $\pi$ and the product $\pi \cdot r_k$;
  - unless S2 applies, the division $z / \pi$ and one new gcd on $u'$ and $\zeta$.
- *What it saves without S2* (a fact): only the bits that $\pi$ removes from both operands. When no piece divides $u$, $\pi = 1$, and the last gcd is Control's own $\gcd(u, z)$, after the remainders.

**Special case S2, the remaining pieces all prime (item 11b).** A **remaining piece** is a slot $j$ with $i_j < e_j(z)$: one whose power still divides $\zeta$.
- *Condition:* inside S1, after step 1:
  - $r_z = 1$;
  - every remaining piece is a prime piece, by the prime bits of those slots.
- *Path:* steps 2 and 3 are not made: no division $z / \pi$ and no gcd call. $r_k = 1$, so $k = \pi$ and $F(k) = (i_0, \ldots, i_{\kappa-1};\ 1)$.
- *Exact:* with $r_z = 1$, $\zeta = \prod_{\text{remaining } j} \Theta[j]^{e_j(z) - i_j}$.
  - For a remaining piece, `divide_out` stopped before its limit, on a failed test $u' \bmod \Theta[j] \ne 0$.
  - The piece is prime. So it does not divide $u'$, what is left of the numerator, and neither does any power of it.
  - Hence $\gcd(u', \zeta) = 1$. With no remaining piece, $\zeta = 1$, and the same holds.

  The non-division is a consequence of the condition, not a part of it: it costs no test beyond step 1's.
- *Why it is faster:* it saves the gcd call and the division, on every reduction against a denominator whose remaining pieces are prime and which has no unfactored part. Packed-23's S2 saves the call for a $\{2, 3\}$-smooth denominator in the same way (v0.5 §18.5).
- *Where it arises:* whenever every remaining piece is 2, 3 or a known prime, and $r_z = 1$. Each such gcd then costs only `divide_out`'s remainders and the products into $\pi$.
- *Not taken:* when a remaining piece is treated as composite (25, 35, 49, … under the draw), or when $r_z > 1$ (a folded temporary). Then steps 2 and 3 run as written.
- *Expected* (non-normative, §19.2): 96–100% of mul's cross gcds under the draw.

**Which path each gcd takes** (item 5): as v0.5 §18.5's table (its C5), row for row. A gcd with a factored denominator as an argument uses S1; every other gcd uses the new gcd on the values. No gcd merges exponents. Control's cases, their order and its values are unchanged.
- an input's entry (v0.1 §8): the new gcd on the values, since no factors exist until after the pack;
- add, `den`: S1 with $F(d)$;
- add, `num` and `general`: $g = \gcd(b, d)$ by S1 with $u = d$ and $F(b)$, which gives $F(g)$. Then, if $g > 1$, $h = \gcd(t, g)$ by S1 with $F(g)$;
- mul, `cross_ad`, `int_y`: S1 with $F(b)$. mul, `cross_cb`, `int_x`: S1 with $F(d)$. mul, `general`: both;
- mul's inner sums, `add(s, p)`: as add, with $s$'s and $p$'s carried factors.

Two more rules carry over:
- **$F(g)$** is formed only when $g > 1$.
- **The mark:** an operand that is the mark is caught by v0.4 §17.10 rule 2's test $d = 0$ first, so S1 never sees the mark's denominator.

**Where the factors come from:**
- a stored operand's denominator: its entry, which has $\phi = 1$ by §19.4, with $r = 1$;
- in mul, $A$'s from the row buffer and $B$'s by handle, both mapped to $\Theta$'s slots;
- a temporary's: its carried factors;
- a gcd's: S1's $F(k)$.

**Read counters** (v0.4 §17.6's rule, as v0.5 §18.5): the exponents are read with their denominator's entry, and the pieces with their matrix. They add no read count. So every read counter equals what Packed's by-handle reads give.

**Temporaries' factors (item 8; C5).** In mul, the product $p$ and the running sum $s$ each hold their value, plus their denominator's exponents over $\Theta$'s slots ($\kappa$ bytes) and its unfactored part (64 bits). These are data, part of the temporary's value, not a structure (§19.8). A temporary's factors are always present, so it carries no flag. It never holds its numerator's factors.
- **Where it applies:** as v0.5 C7, only to a temporary that a later addition of the same cell reads: every product when $n \ge 2$, and every running sum but the cell's last. $F$ is not formed for a cell's last sum, the one product of a cell at $n = 1$, add jobs' results, or the add steps of sop and pos. A stored cell's factors come only from §19.4's pass on its matrix.
- $F$ is formed after the case's value. A cell that overflows stops and is stored as the mark (v0.4 §17.10 rule 4), with no factors formed.

**The two rules** (C5), in the same order as Control forms each denominator:
- **Product:** $F(z_1 z_2) = (e_j(z_1) + e_j(z_2);\ r_{z_1} r_{z_2})$. It costs $\lvert \Theta \rvert$ exponent additions and one multiplication. $r_{z_1} r_{z_2}$ divides $z_1 z_2$, a value Control computes, so it cannot overflow.
- **Quotient by a gcd $k \mid z$:** always $\lvert \Theta \rvert$ comparisons $i_j(k) \le e_j(z)$ and **one remainder**, $r_z \bmod r_k$. Then:
  - *if* every comparison holds and the remainder is 0: $F(z / k) = (e_j(z) - i_j(k);\ r_z / r_k)$, with $\lvert \Theta \rvert$ subtractions and **one exact division**, $r_z / r_k$, made even when $r_k = 1$;
  - *otherwise, a leftover*: $F(z / k) = (0, \ldots, 0;\ z / k)$, the quotient **folded** whole into the unfactored part. $z / k$ is the value Control's arithmetic has just formed.
  - There is no other path. In particular, the remainder and the division are not skipped when $r_k = 1$. So each quotient's work matches v0.5 §18.5's tables ("exp, 1 div"), plus its test.
- *Exact:* both branches give $z / k$. The test is needed because $k$ need not come from S1 on $z$ itself: in add's `general`, $h$ comes from S1 on $g$, and $d$ may carry an unfactored part.
- *When leftovers arise* (a fact): only through composite pieces, or a nontrivial unfactored part. When every piece of $\Theta$ is prime, $r_k = 1$ for every S1 on a value with $r_z = 1$. So no fold happens and every $r$ stays 1.
- *Why folding, not splitting* (C5): v0.3 §16.8 row 3 split the piece in every matrix of the step and in the live temporaries. That would rewrite matrices in the middle of a step. A fold rewrites nothing, and the next S1 on that temporary runs the new gcd on the whole value, as Control does. It is the simplest correct option, and settles the first draft's open point 4. Under the draw, leftovers need a composite piece (25, 35, 49, …).

**The rule, case by case:** v0.5 §18.5's two tables (multiplication and addition), row for row, with Packed-coprime's terms:
- "product" means this section's product rule, and "quotient by an S1 gcd" its quotient rule;
- "exp" means $\lvert \Theta \rvert$ exponent operations;
- each quotient also makes its test: $\lvert \Theta \rvert$ comparisons and one remainder. Its "1 div" is the exact division $r_z / r_k$, always made when the test passes.

For example:
- `den` in mul is $F(d \cdot d)$, the product rule;
- `cross_ad` is $F(b / h)$ with $h = \gcd(c, b)$ by S1, the quotient rule;
- `general` in add with $g > 1$ is the product of $F(b / g)$ and $F(d / h)$, two quotients and one product.

The cases with no factor work (`zero`, `one_x`, `one_y`, `int_both`, `both`; add's `zero` through `int_y`) copy $F$ or give $(0, \ldots, 0;\ 1)$, as there.

**Factor work is counted apart from the core** (C8, as v0.5 C6). It covers:
- pass 1's and reconciliation's gcds, comparisons and `divide_out` steps (remainders, exact divisions, multiplications), and `enter`'s;
- splits and their entries re-expressed; `remap`'s passes and entries;
- S1's `divide_out` steps, the products into $\pi$, the division $z / \pi$ and the product $\pi \cdot r_k$;
- every exponent operation, comparison, multiplication, remainder and exact division of the factor rule, case by case; and the folds.

The core's counters (multiplications, additions, exact divisions) stay Control's. S1's new gcd on $u'$ and $\zeta$ is counted as a gcd, reported per model (§19.13). The base's gcds (pass 1, splits, reconciliation) are counted as factor work, by kind.

### 19.10 Workloads, overflow and the base's limit (items 1g, 1i and 3; C7)

- **Workloads exactly as v0.4 §17.9, for every model:** the smooth draw over the primes up to 7, capped at 32767, with a random sign on the numerator; the same order of random calls and the same seeds. Every model reads the same numbers.
- **64 bits**, as every model.
- **Overflow** as v0.4 §17.10: the mark in the result's cells, with the counters "new" and "passed on", the same way in every model, and the job goes on. **The mark is never factored** (§19.4, the guard).

**$\kappa = 4$** (item 3). Why 4:
- every denominator of every matrix is 7-smooth, or the mark's 0, which is never factored. Inputs are 7-smooth by the draw. Every result denominator divides a product of its operands' denominators: add's $(b/g)(d/h)$ divides $bd$, and mul's $(b/g_2)(d/g_1)$ divides $bd$;
- every piece of a working base, seeded pieces included, divides some denominator or a piece of an operand. So every piece is 7-smooth and at least 2;
- the pieces of a working base are pairwise coprime, so each owns a different, nonempty set of the primes 2, 3, 5 and 7;
- **so a base, $\Theta$ and every working base hold at most 4 pieces** (the survey, §1 item 2). "Base full" cannot occur under this draw.

With item 11 the argument is unchanged: the pieces are 2, 3, and pairwise coprime products of 5s and 7s. So there are at most two pieces beyond 2 and 3.

$\kappa$ costs one byte per piece slot in every entry. A smaller $\kappa$ could fail under this draw; a larger one would cost bytes and be reachable by no job.
- **$\kappa = 4$ is tuned to the draw.** This is a record of the model's limit, as the 64-bit word is, and settles the first draft's open point 9.
- In general a base has no small bound: one 64-bit value has at most 15 distinct primes, and a matrix's denominators together may have more.
- A workload with larger primes would need a larger $\kappa$ and a re-measure of the entry size. That is out of this round's scope (RULES, Optimization approach 2: solve the problem in front of us).

**Base full** (coordinator's call C7, settling the first draft's open point 6, by the author's ruling). It is the error when `enter` would place a piece beyond $\kappa$ in the working base, seeded pieces included (§19.4): in pass 1, in a split, or in reconciliation's $\Theta$. It is **an error that stops the operation, like overflow before v0.4:**
- **stopped:** the operation stops and returns the error "base full", the form v0.1 §0 item 8 gave an overflow. The job stops, and no later step of it runs;
- **recorded:** the driver records the job, the model, the step and the matrix, outside the clock;
- **reported:** with the round's results, as a failed job. The job's other models still run, and it has no verdict.
- It is not v0.4 §17.10's per-cell mark: a base belongs to the whole matrix, so no cell can carry it.
- Under this draw it cannot occur, so if it happens it is a bug, as an overflow after the gates was (v0.1 §11). It is tested only by a constructed matrix outside the draw (§19.13).

**Facts** (not decisions):
- absorb, `enter`, split, `remap`, S1, S2, and the quotient and product rules make only remainders, exact divisions, gcds, and products that divide a value Control computes or a stored denominator;
- every exponent formed is an exponent of a value below $2^{63}$, so at most 62, and it fits its byte;
- so none can overflow, and the marked cells are Control's;
- before the cap's rejection, a drawn value is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth with probability about 0.69 (v0.5 §18.9). That bears on Packed-23's S2. Packed-coprime's S2 needs only that the remaining pieces be prime: 2, 3, or known prime (5 and 7, under this draw).
