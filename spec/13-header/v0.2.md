# Signed Numbers in One Word: Specification v0.2

Nov 1, 2026 · Rescoped by the author to the designs in [`../../book/notes-designs-a-b.md`](../../book/notes-designs-a-b.md): one word per magnitude, the first problem only (below zero). Overflow, the second problem, is out of scope, and so is everything built for it in v0.1 (size classes, pools, multi-word routines, gcd). v0.1 stays as the record of the larger plan. Three rounds: Lean on correctness, Lean on everything else it can prove, then timed trials. Small calls are marked **(coordinator's call)**.

## 1. Scope

A magnitude is one unsigned 64-bit word. Operands are chosen so that no result overflows, in every build. Numbers lie on the axes: integers, and $m \cdot i^d$ for complex numbers; a sum across axes of two nonzero numbers (an expression) is an error.

**Operations:** add, subtract, negate, multiply, exact division ($b \mid a$, $b \ne 0$, by the machine's 64-by-64-bit divide). The case check is part of add, not an operation of its own. B also has collapse.

## 2. Control

The machine's signed 64-bit integers, two's complement, with its own add, subtract, negate, multiply and signed divide. Complex numbers on the axes: a pair (real, imaginary) of them, with the usual rules (multiply by four products, exact division of a pair by a number on an axis) **(coordinator's call)**.

## 3. Design A

A number is an **8-bit header** and a **word**, in two parallel lists. Header bits 6–7 are the direction $d$ (mod 4, for $1, i, -1, -i$); bits 0–5 are 0 (the class code is always 0 here), so no rule masks the header with $D = \texttt{0xC0}$ (the author's change of Nov 1, 2026, after round 2 found every such mask redundant; rounds 1 and 2 used it).

- **Add**, the notes' tree, with `t = h1 xor h2`: $t = 0$, $m_1 + m_2$ in direction `h1`; $t = \texttt{0x80}$, $m_1 - m_2$ and its borrow $b$, negated when $b = 1$, direction `h1 + (b shl 7)`; otherwise a zero operand takes the other's direction and the add runs as same-direction, and two nonzero operands are an error. No other zero test.
- **Subtract:** add with `h2 xor 0x80`. **Negate:** `h xor 0x80`.
- **Multiply:** $m_1 m_2$, direction `h1 + h2`.
- **Exact division:** $m_1 / m_2$, direction `h1 - h2`.
- Header arithmetic is on bytes, mod 256.
- A zero keeps whatever direction its operation gives.

## 4. Design B

Integers: a pair $(P, N)$, value $P - N$, two words. Complex numbers: four words $(m_0, m_1, m_2, m_3)$ along $1, i, -1, -i$. No header.

- **Add:** part by part. **Negate:** swap $P$ and $N$ (complex: turn every part by 2). **Subtract:** add the negation, by crossed parts.
- **Multiply:** integers $(P_1P_2 + N_1N_2,\ P_1N_2 + N_1P_2)$; complex, sixteen products into four parts by $d_1 + d_2$.
- **Collapse:** $\lvert P - N \rvert$ from the subtraction and its borrow, as in A's add; it goes to $P$ when the borrow is 0, else to $N$, and the other part is 0. Complex: each axis separately.
- **Exact division:** collapse both, divide the magnitudes, A's direction rule.

## 4a. Design C

Added Nov 1, 2026 by the author (notes, Design C). A number is one word $w = 2v + a$: the axis $a$ in bit 0 (0 real, 1 imaginary) and a signed value $v$ in bits 1–63, two's complement, so $-2^{62} \le v < 2^{62}$; value $v \cdot i^a$. Zero is $w = 0$ or $w = 1$, keeping the axis its operation gives. `sar` is the arithmetic shift right.

- **Add**, with `t = (w1 xor w2) and 1`: $t = 0$, `w1 + (w2 and not 1)`; otherwise, if `w2 < 2` (unsigned) the result is `w1`, else if `w1 < 2` it is `w2`, else an error (an expression).
- **Subtract:** $t = 0$, `w1 - (w2 and not 1)`; otherwise `w1` if `w2 < 2`, the negation of `w2` if `w1 < 2`, else an error.
- **Negate:** `((w and 1) shl 1) - w`.
- **Multiply:** `p = (w1 sar 1) * (w2 and not 1)`, negated when `w1 and w2 and 1` is 1, then OR `(w1 xor w2) and 1`.
- **Exact division:** `q = (w1 and not 1) / (w2 and not 1)`, signed, which is $2v_1 / 2v_2 = v_1 / v_2$; negated when $a_2 = 1$ and $a_1 = 0$; then `(q shl 1) + ((w1 xor w2) and 1)`, which x86-64 does in one `lea` and ARM64 in one shifted `add`. The divisor is even, so never $-1$, and the signed divide never traps. (The first rule, `(w1 and not 1) / (w2 sar 1)`, one instruction shorter, divided $-2^{63}$ by $-1$ for $(-2^{62}) / (-i)$, an in-range result, and x86-64's `idiv` traps on that; ARM64 and RISC-V do not. Found in round 1, fixed at the author's request.)
- **Edge case:** negation of $v = -2^{62}$ overflows and returns the word unchanged, as Control's negation of $-2^{63}$ does.

## 5. Round 1: correctness (done for A and B)

Proved in Lean 4, core only ([`lean/`](lean/)): for magnitudes below $N = 2^n$, one word, each rule of §3 and §4 gives the true result, under the no-overflow hypothesis where one is needed; any zero equals any zero; B's collapse is the unique pair with one part zero; B's four-slot form agrees with Gaussian integers. Design C: each rule of §4a on 64-bit words gives the true result (encoding and decoding inverse, zero on both axes, the expression error exactly for two nonzero values on different axes), under the no-overflow hypothesis. The word-level proofs in the same folder belong to the larger plan. The Python tests of v0.1 are retired. Rerun for A without the mask (`lean/Header/HeaderByte.lean`): each header rule of §3 on bytes gives the header of round 1's result, `addByte_eq` for add, and likewise subtract, negate, multiply and divide. Round 2 is not rerun; its programs and counts keep the mask, one step more per add, subtract, multiply and divide path, and one more for `t`.

## 6. Round 2: what Lean can prove beyond correctness

Control, A, A's branch-free row (§7), B and C as portable pseudocode (algorithm design rule 6), then x86-64 machine code.

- **(a) Counts, proved in Lean:** the pseudocode as a small instruction set, each step one instruction on x86-64, ARM64 and RISC-V (RV64IM). For every path through every operation: instructions, branches, loads and stores, exactly, with a proof that the paths cover every input. A table maps each step to its instructions on the three families, checked against the disassembly. Cycle predictions from uops.info's latencies and throughputs, as claims about that model.
- **(b) Branch inventory:** every branch, its condition, and the inputs that take it, proved from the same programs; when each is predictable is written beside, since that depends on the data a program meets, not the program.

## 7. Round 3: timed trials

Spec 12's method, against Control, on a quiet machine: every operation on every build except C (the author's call: C goes through rounds 1 and 2 only, for now), 1024 operations per row, under each sign mix (all positive, 1/16 negative, random) and, for add and subtract, each magnitude-order mix ($\lvert a \rvert \ge \lvert b \rvert$ always, 15/16, half). Integers, then complex numbers on the axes (each operand on a random axis, a nonzero pair across axes replaced by one with a zero) **(coordinator's call)**. B's operands enter collapsed. One branch-free row, for A: the direction and borrow branches replaced by masks (`0 - b`, AND, XOR); no conditional moves (RV64I lacks them). Time beyond what the counts predict is branch cost, and a large or consistent residual is a finding. Bytes per value beside: Control 8 (16 complex), A 9, B 16 (32 complex).
