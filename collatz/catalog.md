# Collatz catalog

Compiled 6 November 2026. This catalog is the reference record of everything we have found to be true and relevant to Collatz maps on N, Z, Z[i], the Eisenstein integers Z[ω] and general rings of integers, from all the studies, with the literature results that bear on them. Each entry records the statement, how we know it is true, and the credit due to whoever found it first, where we know it. Negative results that are established facts are entries; failed approaches and process notes stay in the research files. The catalog does not track papers: papers are drafted from an outline and filled in from the catalog. It replaces `collatz/papers/collatz-inventory.md` (403 items mapped to an old outline) and adds the results of the files that postdate it. Goldbach and Weilert work are kept elsewhere. Updated the same afternoon with the notes FD, CBP, CB, RT, MON, GT, RQ (source key below) and the abstract Lean layer.

## 0. How to read an entry

Each entry has a short id, the statement, its status, prior art and credit, and the source.

Status labels. Lean: proved in Lean 4 (core, toolchain v4.35.0, axioms at most `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`), with the theorem names. Paper: proved on paper, not formalized, with the location of the proof. Computer-assisted: a proof that rests on stated program runs. Computed to X: an observation checked for every start up to the bound X. Heuristic. Literature: taken from a cited source. Open. Proposal: a definition put forward and awaiting the author's approval; nothing else in the catalog uses it. Paper, unchecked: an argument written down in the catalog itself and not yet checked by a second reading. Superseded entries, and conjectures found false, are in Section 18.

Source key. Files in `collatz/research/`: TH `theorems.md`, FD `fundamental-domain.md` (logs `logs/fd_*`, `logs/fdint.txt`), CBP `cycle-bounds-prior-art.md`, CB `cycle-bounds.md` (logs `logs/cyclebound*`, `logs/certarith_selftest.txt`), RT `rings-test.md` (logs `logs/rings_*`), MON `monks-slides.md`, GT `general-theory.md`, RQ `real-quadratic.md` (logs `logs/realq_*`), GP `general-proofs.md` (checks in `genproofs.py`, log `logs/genproofs.txt`), QUO `quotient.md`, M3 `modulus3-search.md`, IAP `invariants-prior-art.md`, LEI `leigh1986.md`, MW `matthews-watts1985.md`, RPA `rotation-prior-art.md`, SC `score.md`, ST `symmetry-test.md`, IC `integer-census.md`, FAM `families.md`, CONV `convergents.md`, COL `collatz.md`, LIT `literature.md`, SLIT `symmetric-literature.md`, LAG `lagarias2016.md`, OE `oeis.md`, LEAN `lean/` (files in `lean/GaussianCollatz/`), LOG `logs/`. Older drafts, cited as sources only: D1 `collatz/papers/gaussian-collatz.md`, D2 `collatz/papers/gaussian-collatz-2.md`. INV is the superseded inventory, cited only where a figure was computed for it and is written up nowhere else.

Vocabulary. A map is (α, k, d): multiplier α, modulus π^k, digit table d. A Collatz map is a map in which every nonzero orbit reaches a trivial cycle, a cycle that contains a unit; a unit cycle consists only of units. A map commutes with the units if T(uz) = uT(z) for every unit u, and respects associates if T(uz) is an associate of T(z) (Section 7). Rotated and mirrored maps are defined in Section 6. The window is q^k < N(α) < q^(k+1/(q-1)), which for q = 2 is 2^k < N(α) < 2^(k+1). The drift is the mean change of log2 N per odd step under the equidistribution assumption. The score is 1/M - 1/N with M = N(π^k) and N = N(α). A fundamental-domain map is a map on R/U given by a set F of representatives and a digit read from the class of the representative (A21); RT and RQ call these Collatz-type maps and call maps of our form Barina-type maps, names the catalog does not use. Older names ("level", "expression", "rule", "symmetric", "unit-equivariant", "Collatz-like", "minimal" as the ordering principle) are retired; Lean identifiers keep some of them (`Expr`, `Expr.IsRule`, `Symmetric`).

Throughout π = 1+i in Z[i], U is the unit group, and for Z[i], π^3 = -2+2i, π^4 = -4, π^5 = -4-4i.

## 1. Framework and definitions

### 1.1 Definitions and basic lemmas

F1. In Z[i], a+bi is even (divisible by π) exactly when a+b is even; (a+bi)/(1+i) = ((a+b)+(b-a)i)/2; dividing by π halves the norm; since 2 = -iπ^2, the exponent of π in an integer is twice its exponent of 2; conjugation preserves parity and π^k divides x iff it divides x̄. Status: paper. Source: TH 1; D2 3.1.

F2. Definition of a map (α, k, d) over R: α odd, k ≥ 0, d a function on odd z depending only on z mod π^k with π^k | αz + d(z); digit set D; T(z) = z/π on even z and αz + d(z) on odd z; accelerated S(z) = w/π^v(w) with w = αz + d(z), S(z) = 0 if w = 0. Status: definition; for Z[i] in Lean (`Expr`, `Expr.T`, `Expr.S`; in Lean S is 0 on even inputs). Source: TH Def 1.1.

F3. Lemma: π | xy implies π | x or π | y; for odd α, π^k | αx implies π^k | x; for k ≥ 1 every digit is odd and D has exactly one element in each odd class, so d(z) is the element of D in the class of -αz and a map is a choice of one odd element per odd class. Status: Lean for Z[i] (`pi_dvd_mul`, `pow_dvd_of_odd_mul`, `Expr.d_odd`, `Expr.d_inj`); paper for general R (GP 9, Lemma 9.1; GT 3, Lemma 3.1, which shows the proof needs only an integral domain and a prime π with finite residue field and ∩π^nR = 0, and adds that the odd classes mod π^k are the group (R/π^k)^×). Source: TH Lemma 1.2; GP 9; GT 2 item 1, 3.

F4. The congruence d(z) ≡ -αz (mod π^k) depends only on the class of z, and z ↦ -αz permutes the q^(k-1)(q-1) odd classes. Status: paper. Source: D1 2; D2 3.1.

F5. Definitions of periodic point, cycle, trivial cycle, unit cycle, trivial map and Collatz map; cycles taken literally as sets. Status: definition; Lean for Z[i] (`Periodic`, `Reaches`, `OnTrivialCycle`, `Trivial`, `Expr.IsRule`). Source: TH Def 1.3; ST 1 (the author's criterion: every nonzero orbit reaches a trivial cycle, cycles literal for non-commuting maps).

F6. Two cycles that meet are equal: if x is periodic with period p and y = f^j(x), then f^(j(p-1))(y) = x. Status: Lean (`reaches_back`). Source: TH Lemma 1.4(a).

F7. Triviality can be read off T or S alike: a nonzero even element reaches its odd part under T, only even elements lie between consecutive odd terms, and units are odd. Status: paper. Source: TH Remark 1.5.

F8. A map commutes with the units iff d(uz) = u d(z) iff D is closed under multiplication by units (k ≥ 1); then S(uz) = uS(z) and S^n(uz) = uS^n(z). Status: Lean (`Expr.Symmetric`, `Expr.S_commutes`, `iter_unit_mul`). Source: TH Lemma 1.4(b), (c).

F9. For every map, z ↦ uz conjugates (α, D) to (α, u^(-1)D) (digit table z ↦ u^(-1)d(uz)); the four unit images of a digit set always give identical cycle counts and basins. Status: paper; computed agreement in every run (ST 2; M3 3 item 3). Credit: the REU associates proposition at modulus 1+i, and on Z the passage from 3x+1 on the negatives to 3x-1, are this conjugacy (RPA 3.1, 3.9).

F10. A map that commutes with the units acts on classes of associates: every odd Gaussian integer is a unit times one with a > 0, b ≥ 0, so one representative per class suffices for a check and cycles are counted up to units; a conjecture of Collatz type for such a map speaks about all of Z[i] \ {0}, with no "half" as for 3x+1 on Z. Status: paper; Lean (`exists_unit_primary`). Source: D1 5.1; D2 1.

F11. An f-invariant set of non-units containing an odd element rules out triviality. Status: Lean (`not_trivial_of_invariant`). Source: LEAN Dynamics.lean.

F12. Invariants of the rings used: N (units 1, π = 2, q = 2), Z (±1, 2, 2, e = 1, κ = 1, line), Z[i] (four units, 1+i, q = 2, ramified, e = 2, κ = 2, square lattice, conjugation moves π: π̄ = -iπ), Z[ω] (six units, 2, q = 4, inert, e = 1, κ = 1, triangular lattice, conjugation fixes π). Status: paper. Source: IC 2.3, 7.

F19. Proposed definition: a map T on R is a Collatz map if every nonzero orbit reaches a unit and the orbit of every unit returns to a unit; equivalently, every nonzero orbit visits the units infinitely often. For finite U it is equivalent to F5: an orbit that visits the finite set U infinitely often visits some unit twice, so it is eventually periodic on a cycle through a unit; conversely an orbit that reaches a trivial cycle visits its unit infinitely often. For infinite U it is weaker than F5: the walks u ↦ uε of a commuting lift with T(1) = ε (O14) satisfy it and have no periodic unit. It is weaker also than the quotient form of RQ 4.5 (every orbit enters U and stays there), which it contains as the case T(U) ⊆ U. Status: proposal, awaiting the author's approval; F5 is unchanged and every other entry uses F5. Consequence for maps that respect associates: A18. Source: stated by the author, 6 November 2026; RQ 4.2, 4.5.

### 1.2 Background from the literature

F13. History: Collatz says he invented the problem before 1952 and passed it to Hasse in 1952; Coxeter gave the first published statement in 1971 with prizes of 50 and 100 dollars; Erdős published nothing on 3x+1, called it "hopeless" in conversation, and the "mathematics is not ready" remark is not in Lagarias's account; Erdős's only prize in Lagarias 2016 concerns the density of ⟨2x+1, 3x+1, 6x+1 : 1⟩. Status: literature (Lagarias 2016, p. 772; Collatz 1986; Coxeter 1971). Source: LAG 1.2, 1.5, 4.

F14. Lagarias 2016 restates 3x+1 through the affine semigroup ⟨2x, (2x-1)/3⟩: the integers in the orbit of 4 form the backward tree, a non-integer never returns, and the orbit is not discrete, so Erdős's density bound does not apply; the x+1 problem, with free inverse semigroup ⟨2x, 2x-1⟩, is the "solved cousin". Klarner and Rado introduced orbits ⟨R : A⟩ (Pacific J. Math. 53 (1974)). Status: literature. Source: LAG 1.3, 1.5, 2.

F15. Conway (1972) proved a generalization of 3x+1 undecidable, so maps of this kind are studied one at a time; Conway 2013 on unsettleable problems. Status: literature (via Lagarias 2016). Source: LAG 1.5.

F16. Frameworks that contain our maps: Kohl's RCWA (rcwa maps of Z^2 with 2×2 matrix coefficients include each step of a Gaussian map; RCWA itself implements only Z, Z^2, semilocalizations Z_(π) and GF(q)[x]); Kionke's lattice maps (tame cones give divergent orbits of positive density; on Z[√2] at least half diverge; integer matrix multipliers proposed as an open extension); Siegel's Hydra maps on O_K (no Z[i] example); Siegel and O'Dwyer (almost bounded orbits on a set of logarithmic density one under (p-1)^2 ρ_H^d < 1, no Z[i] or Z[ω] example). Status: literature, partly unverified (RCWA manual chapter 2, Siegel and O'Dwyer's MH1 not read). Source: LIT 2.5, 2.8, 2.9; IAP 2.4.

F17. Literature search result: as far as the search reached, no earlier work takes the ring as a parameter, studies maps on Z[i] dividing by 1+i at moduli (1+i)^k with k ≥ 2, or uses the criterion of commuting with the units. Gaps: Lagarias's bibliography I from M on, Garg's formulas, the REU paper, Matthews's web pages, Hasse, Möller, Wirsching, Venturini, most of The Ultimate Challenge. Status: literature negative. Source: LIT 3; SLIT 5; IAP 1, 4.

F18. Works checked and found to concern Z only or to contain no map commuting with -1 (Tremblay, Carnielli, Goodwin, Hohertz-Kalantari, Rozier, Gilbert, Bruschi, Rorabaugh, Edgington, Briscese-Calogero, Goyal, Shrimali-Antu-Jones, Barina-Maat, Bouhamidi, Ye, Siegel 1909.09733, Behajaina-Paran, Diarte-Carot, Hamada, Lesieutre, Karami and others). Status: literature negative. Source: LIT 2.13; SLIT 3.5; IAP 4; RPA 3.11.

## 2. The window and drift

W1. Below the window: if N(α) < q^k, every orbit of T is eventually periodic (ρ = |α|/|π|^k < 1, every orbit enters the disc |z| ≤ R_0 = δ/(|π|^k(1-ρ)) and stays). Status: paper, not in Lean; for Z[i] in D2, and for every order in Q or an imaginary quadratic field, any prime π and any table in GT 6.3, Theorem 6.3. With several archimedean places the norm form is false and the per-place form W16 replaces it. Credit: on Z it follows from Leigh's Lemma 3, (61), unconditionally (LEI 3.1); MW 1985 does not contain it (MW 3.3). These maps play the part of the x+1 problem. Source: D2 Prop 3; IAP 3.1.

W2. Worst case above the lower end: an odd step with exactly k divisions multiplies the norm by N(α)/q^k > 1. Status: paper. Source: D2 3.4.

W3. In density over odd z (Haar measure on the π-adics), v - k is geometric with P(v - k ≥ j) = q^(-j), mean 1/(q-1); the mean change of log2 N per odd step is log2 N(α) - (k + 1/(q-1)) log2 q, negative exactly inside the window. For q = 2: mean 1, variance 2. Status: paper for the density statement, heuristic for orbits. Source: IC 2.4; D2 3.4.

W4. Lemma: write the map at modulus π^k as T' with z ↦ z/π on the q^(k-1) even classes and z ↦ (αz + d)/π^k on the odd ones; its Markov matrix on classes mod π^k (fractions of classes mod π^(2k)) is doubly stochastic and irreducible, so the stationary weights are uniform, q^(-k); the weighted product of norm ratios is (1/q)^(1/q)(N(α)/q^k)^((q-1)/q), below 1 exactly when N(α) < q^(k+1/(q-1)). Status: paper for general R (GP 4, Lemma 4.1 and the product); the Z argument is in MW 3.2. Computed: the matrix built in exact fractions is doubly stochastic and irreducible with entries q^(-k) and 1/q for Z[i] at (1+i)^2 to (1+i)^4, Z[ω] at moduli 2, 4, 8 and Z at moduli 2 to 16, and the products agree with the formula (GP 4; LOG genproofs.txt). The earlier check recorded in MW 3.4 lists 5+3i among its multipliers; 5+3i is even (norm 34) and no multiplier, and GP 4 uses 4+3i in its place. Credit: MW 1985 Lemma 3.2 gives doubly stochastic Q only when every m_i is prime to the modulus, which fails on even classes. GT 6.1: the proof needs only an integral domain and a prime with finite residue field, so it holds in every ring tested in RT and RQ, including the split ring and Z[√2]. Source: MW 3.2, 3.4; GP 4; GT 6.1.

W5. The window, Definition: q^k < N(α) < q^(k+1/(q-1)); for q = 2, 2^k < N(α) < 2^(k+1); for 3x+1, 2 < 3 < 4 at modulus 2; for 7x ± 1, 4 < 7 < 8 at modulus 4. Prior art: on Z the upper end at every k is the dividing line of Matthews and Watts 1985, Conjectures 1 and 2 (p. 30), which apply because the encoded map satisfies their condition C with β = 1 and m = d (all trajectories eventually periodic below, almost all divergent above; positive multipliers only); Leigh 1986, Conjecture 1, Corollary (iii), (4), is the one-sided form for nonzero multipliers with |m_i/d|; at k = 1 the upper end is Heppner's threshold d^(d/(d-1)) for Hasse's maps (Arch. Math. 31 (1978) 317-320, via Lagarias bibliography I entry 78; paper not seen), with Hasse's case m = d+1 inside; Crandall's conjecture that qx+1 diverges for q ≥ 5 is the case q = 2, k = 1; Siegel and O'Dwyer's heuristic ρ_H < 1 gives the same upper end if the operator norm of multiplication by α is |α| (our computation). The norm form and the exponent 1/(q-1) were not found in print. Status: definition, with the literature identification. Source: IAP 2.1, 3.1; LEI 3.1; MW 3.1 to 3.4, 4.1.

W6. With the unaccelerated encoding (m_i = 2^(k-1) on even classes, 2^k m on odd ones) the weights are not uniform (odd classes total 1/(k+2)); the product is (m/2^(k+1))^(1/(k+2)) with the same threshold, while the unweighted product gives the wrong answer (1.22 > 1 for 3x+1). So the weighting of MW 1985 makes the criterion independent of how the map is written. Status: computed exactly, with argument. Source: MW 3.2.

W7. Weighted products, first and second encoding: 3x+1 (k = 1) 0.866, 0.909; 5x+1 1.118, 1.077; 7x ± 1 (k = 2) 0.935, 0.967; 9x ± 1 1.061, 1.030; 21x+d (k = 4) 0.810, 0.932; 29x+d 0.952, 0.984; 31x+d 0.984, 0.995; 33x+d 1.016, 1.005; in every case the eigenvalue 1 of Q(d) is simple. Status: computed exactly. Source: MW 3.2.

W8. MW 1985 Theorem 1.1(b), applied with the first encoding, gives the drift per odd step, log2(m/2^k) - 1; their Lemma 2.1 shows the digits drop out of Q(m) when gcd(m_i, m) = 1, so for our maps the weights do not depend on the digit table (also visible in Leigh, whose chain depends only on the m_i and d). Status: literature plus derivation. Source: MW 3.3, 4.6; LEI 3.6.

W9. "Divergent" in MW and Leigh means "not eventually periodic", which equals "unbounded" only when there are finitely many elements of bounded size: true for Z, Z[i], Z[ω], false for Z[√2] with the absolute value; Leigh's Lemma 3 and our W1 both need it. Status: remark. Source: LEI 3.1; MW 3.4.

W10. The windows for different k are disjoint, so in one ring the norm of a multiplier in the window determines its modulus, and ordering by modulus then norm is ordering by norm. Status: paper. Source: D2 3.5.

W11. Which moduli carry multipliers: π^k does iff (q^k, q^(k+1/(q-1))) contains the norm of an odd element. In Z for every k ≥ 1; in Z[i] exactly for k ≥ 2 (odd norms are 1 mod 4; witnesses 2^j + i for k = 2j, (2^j+1) + 2^j i for k = 2j+1); in Z[ω] exactly for k ≥ 2 (modulus 2 needs 5, not a norm; witness 2^k - ω of norm 4^k + 2^k + 1). Status: paper (GP 2, Proposition 2.1); checked exhaustively in Z for k ≤ 11, in Z[i] for k ≤ 14 (every odd norm in a window is 1 mod 4, and the witness lies in the window) and in Z[ω] for k ≤ 8 (LOG genproofs.txt); the multipliers are tabulated up to (1+i)^7 in W12 and at Eisenstein moduli 2, 4, 8 in IC 7. Credit: not found as a principle; the REU slides and an uncitable blog post touch it (IAP 3.2). Source: GP 2; IAP 3.2.

W12. Odd α in Z[i] has N(α) ≡ 1 (mod 4). Multipliers in the window up to units: moduli 1 and 1+i none; (1+i)^2: norm 5 (2 ± i); (1+i)^3: 9, 13 (3, 3 ± 2i); (1+i)^4: 17, 25, 29 (4 ± i, 5, 4 ± 3i, 5 ± 2i); (1+i)^5: 37, 41, 45, 49, 53, 61 (11 multipliers); (1+i)^6: 65 to 125, twelve norms (28 multipliers); (1+i)^7: 137, 145, 149, 153, 157, 169, 173, 181, 185, 193, 197, 205, 221, 225, 229, 233, 241, 245 (46 multipliers). Status: computed exactly. Source: D2 4.4; COL 2, 5 item 1; TH Lemma 5.3.

W13. In Z[ω] the naive window (4^k, 4^(k+1)) would admit 23 multipliers up to units at modulus 4 instead of the five in (16, 25.40); all but those five expand on average. Status: computed. Source: IC 2.4.

W14. Drift definition: log2(N/M) - log2 q/(q-1) bits per odd step, which for q = 2 is log2 N(α) - (k+1); for 3x+1 on N, -0.415; for 7x ± 1, -0.193; for the 5+2i map, -0.142 (= ln(29/32) = -0.098 in natural log). Inside the window the drift lies in (-log2 q/(q-1), 0). Status: definition and arithmetic. Source: SC 1; D2 3.4, 6.4; IC 5.

W15. Score definition 1/M - 1/N = (N-M)/(NM); inside the window it lies in (0, 1/M - q^(-k-1/(q-1))), for q = 2 in (0, 1/(2M)); below the window it is negative; within one modulus score and drift order maps alike, across moduli the score favours the smaller modulus; compare within one ring and one class. Prior art for such a score was not searched. Status: definition. Source: SC 1, 2 (q = 2); GP 7 (general q).

W16. Below the window with several archimedean places: if |π|_v > 1 and |α|_v < |π|_v^k at every archimedean place v, every orbit of T and S is eventually periodic (the argument of W1 place by place; R is a lattice in the product of the completions). The norm form fails: in Z[√2] with π = √2, k = 2, α = 3 + 2√2 (a unit, |N(α)| = 1 < 4) and smallest digits, the per-place drifts are +1.04 and -4.04 bits, and 182 of 200 odd starts with |a|, |b| ≤ 30 passed 10^40. In Z[√3], where no generator of the prime above 2 is balanced (|σ_2(1+√3)| = 0.73 < 1), the theorem never applies and even α = 1 diverges. ## 6. Rotated and mirrored maps

## 7. Maps on R/U: the quotient by the units

Throughout this section R is an integral domain with finite unit group U (Z[i] in Lean), [z] is the class Uz of z, and R/U is the set of classes of nonzero elements (with [0] = {0} kept where convenient). Lean names are in `lean/GaussianCollatz/Quotient.lean`; the integer ones are in the namespace `IntCollatz`.

A1. Setting: associates x ~ y (y = ux, u a unit) is an equivalence relation; the class of z ≠ 0 has exactly |U| elements; [1] = U; [z] ↦ |z| identifies Z/{±1} with N, and Z[i]/U with the quarter plane a > 0, b ≥ 0, the fundamental domain of the drop-below-start checks. Norm and parity are constant on classes. A map T respects associates (sends associates to associates) if T(uz) ~ T(z) for all units u and all z; commuting with the units implies it. Status: Lean (`Assoc`, `assocSetoid`, `Cls`, `cls`, `cls_eq_iff`, `cls_unit_mul`, `cls_one_iff`, `RespectsAssoc`, `respects_iff`, `Commutes.respects`; `exists_unit_primary` in Soundness.lean for the quarter plane). Source: QUO 2.

A2. Descent: there is a map T̄ on R/U with T̄[z] = [T(z)] for every z iff T respects associates; it is then unique, and T̄^n[z] = [T^n(z)]. Status: Lean (`descend`, `descend_cls`, `descent_iff`, `descend_unique`, `descend_iter`, `qiter`; abstract versions for any finite group acting on a set in Abstract.lean, with the same names in the namespace `Abstract`); paper for any group, finite or not (GT 8, Theorem 8.1). Credit: the universal property of a quotient, standard (QUO 10 item 6). Source: QUO Prop 2.2(1); GT 8.

A3. Respecting associates is strictly weaker than commuting with the units, but gives no new induced maps: z ↦ z^2 respects associates on Z[i] and does not commute (T(i) = -1 ≠ i); on Z, 3|z| + 1 on odd z and z/2 on even z respects associates and T(-1) = 4 ≠ -4; if T(0) = 0, every map induced by a map that respects associates is also induced by a map that commutes with the units (put T'(uf) = uT(f) on one representative f per class). Status: Lean for z^2 and for the commuting replacement (`sq_respects`, `sq_not_commutes`, `exists_commuting_same_descend`); paper for the integer example. Source: QUO Prop 2.2(3), (4).

A4. Transfer theorem: let T respect associates and X be a set of nonzero elements closed under units with 1 ∈ X (all nonzero elements for T, the odd elements for S). Then T is a Collatz map on X (every z ∈ X has an iterate that is periodic with a trivial cycle) iff T̄ is a Collatz map on X/U in Collatz's own sense: every [z] reaches [1], and [1] is periodic. So for maps that commute with the units the Collatz property is a statement about R/U, as the Collatz conjecture is about N. The finiteness of U is used once, in a pigeonhole step, and is needed: the unit reached need not itself be periodic (the twisted map of A10 sends -1 to 4 on the cycle {1, 4, 2}). Equivalently (QUO Remark 3.3), "the only cycle of T̄ on X/U is that of [1] and no orbit diverges" is the Collatz property. Status: Lean (`transfer` for any predicate P with P(1), not needing closure under units; `Expr.rule_iff_quotient` for S on odd elements; `transfer_nonzero` for T on nonzero elements; `pigeon`, `CollatzOn`, `QCollatzOn`, `trivial_iff_collatzOn`, `iter_iter`); Lean for any finite group acting on a set (`Abstract.transfer_orbit`, `Abstract.transfer`), from which the Z[i] statement is rederived (`transfer_of_abstract`); paper in that setting (GT 8, Theorem 8.2). For infinite U see A16, and for the proposed definition F19 see A18. Credit: an instance of standard equivariant dynamics (a relative periodic point is periodic; Field 2007 ch. 2; ChaosBook ch. 11; RPA 3.12); not found stated for Collatz maps on R/U (QUO 10). Source: QUO Thm 3.2, Remark 3.3.

A5. Cycle lifts for maps that respect associates: if [z] has least period p under T̄ and g = T^p, then g maps the class Uz into itself; y ∈ Uz is T-periodic iff g-periodic, with least T-period p times its least g-period, and every T-period is a multiple of p; the cycles of T over the cycle of [z] correspond bijectively to the g-cycles in Uz, with |C| = p|C ∩ Uz|; there are between 1 and |U| of them. Nothing more holds in general: on Z[i] the map with 1 → -i → -1 → 1, i ↦ -i and z ↦ z on non-units respects associates, [1] is fixed, and the only cycle over it has length 3, which divides no unit order, with i not periodic. Status: Lean (`lift_cycle`, `qperiod_dvd`, `lift_period_dvd`, `qiter_mul`, `qiter_add`; the example `ex3`, `ex3_respects`, `ex3_cycle`; abstract `Abstract.lift_cycle`, `Abstract.qperiod_dvd`, `Abstract.lift_period_dvd`, with `lift_cycle_of_abstract` for Z[i]); paper for any finite group and any orbit (GT 8, Theorem 8.4). Credit: the divisibility of periods is the semiconjugacy fact "semiconjugacies map cyclic points of order k to cyclic points of order d for some d dividing k" in K. G. Monks's slides (MON 3.2 item 3). Source: QUO Thm 4.1; GT 8.

A6. Cycle lifts and stabilizer for maps that commute with the units: with T^p(z) = εz, every point of Uz is periodic with least period p·ord(ε); the cycles over [z] are the translates vC of the cycle C of z, and vC = C iff v ∈ ⟨ε⟩; so there are exactly |U|/ord(ε) of them, each of length p·ord(ε), and the stabilizer of C is ⟨ε⟩. A7. Trivial cycles and unit cycles are the lifts of [1]: a cycle contains a unit iff it lies over the cycle of [1]. With p the least period of [1] and ε = T^p(1): T1 is A5 at z = 1 (the trivial cycles correspond to the cycles of T^p on U); T2 is A6 at z = 1, and the stabilizer U ∩ C_1 of T2 is ⟨T^p(1)⟩, which T2 only showed to be a subgroup; T3 is the case p = 1 (all elements of C_1 are units iff T(1) is a unit); E_2 (T7) does not respect associates (A9) but acts on U as the example of A5. A8. A9. Consequences of A8: (1) no map at modulus π^k respects associates when some unit u ≠ 1 is ≡ 1 (mod π^k) (by U1): on Z at moduli 1 and 2, so no map 3x + d does; on Z[i] at k ≤ 2, so E_2 does not. A10. Twisted maps with class-dependent multipliers exist: if T(z) = α_c z + d(c) on odd z in the class c (a generalized map in the sense of Matthews and Watts), T respects associates iff for every odd class c and unit u there is a unit ε with α_{uc}u = εα_c and d(uc) = εd(c); if the α_c are associates of one α, α_c = s(c)α, then T = s·T' on odd elements with T' the map with multiplier α and table s(c)^(-1)d(c), T respects associates iff T' commutes with the units, and then T̄ = T̄', so T and T' are Collatz maps together. A11. A12. Mirror on R/U: conjugation induces an involution σ̄ of R/U fixing [1] and preserving parity and norm; if S_α respects associates, so does S_ᾱ, and S̄_ᾱ = σ̄ S̄_α σ̄. A13. The integers. N = (Z \ {0})/{±1}. Collatz's 3x+1 does not respect associates (C(5) = 16, C(-5) = -14), so it defines no map on Z/{±1}; no map of our form at modulus 2 respects associates (1 ≡ -1 mod 2 forces d(-1) = d(1), respecting forces d(-1) = -d(1), so d(1) = 0, which is even). ### 7.1 General groups, infinite unit groups and the proposed definition

A16. Transfer for an infinite group: let T respect associates, [1] be periodic under the induced map with least period p, and g = T^p, which maps U into U. Then T is a Collatz map on X (F5) iff the induced map is a Collatz map on X/U in Collatz's sense and every g-orbit on U is eventually periodic (RQ Theorem 4.1). For T commuting with U and T^p(1) = ε this holds iff ε^m ∈ G_e for some m ≥ 1, on a free orbit iff ε has finite order (GT Theorem 8.3); in a real quadratic ring iff T^p(1) = ±1. The pigeonhole step of A4 is exactly what fails: in Z[√2] the lift of the first cone map has S(1) = 1 + √2 and is not a Collatz map on R although its induced map is one on R/U (O10). Status: paper (GT 8, Theorem 8.3; RQ 4.1, 4.2); not in Lean (the abstract layer assumes a finite group). Source: GT 2 item 8, 8; RQ 4.1, 4.2; RT 9.4.

A17. Choice of lift: every Collatz map F on R/U has a lift that commutes with U and is a Collatz map on R: T(uf) = uμ(f)L_0(f) for a commuting lift L_0 and μ : F → U has w = w_0 Π μ(f_i) over the cycle of [1], so μ(1) = w_0^(-1) and μ = 1 elsewhere gives w = 1. For a fundamental-domain map, (uα, ud) is the same map on F and its lift is L_u(z) = L(uz), so L_u^p(1) = u^p L^p(1); in a real quadratic ring with w = L^p(1) = ±ε^j, some rotation lifts to a Collatz map on R iff p | j (rotation by ε^(-j/p)). Lifts that respect associates without commuting can mix fixed units and walks and can have any number of trivial cycles. So the failure of A4 for infinite U is a property of a lift, not of the map on R/U. Status: paper (RQ 4.3, Corollaries 4.3, 4.4). Source: RQ 4.3.

A18. Consequence of the proposed definition F19: for a map T that respects associates, T is a Collatz map on R in the sense of F19 iff the induced map sends every nonzero class to [1] (every class reaches [1]) and [1] is periodic, for any unit group, with no condition on the orbits of units. Argument: by A2, T̄^n[z] = [T^n(z)] and [T^n(z)] = [1] iff T^n(z) is a unit. If T is a Collatz map in the sense of F19, every nonzero orbit reaches a unit, so every class reaches [1], and the orbit of 1 returns to a unit, T^n(1) ∈ U for some n ≥ 1, so T̄^n[1] = [1]. Conversely, if every class reaches [1], every nonzero orbit reaches a unit; and if [1] has period p, then for every unit u, [T^p(u)] = T̄^p[u] = T̄^p[1] = [1], so T^p(u) is a unit and the orbit of every unit returns to a unit. Only descent is used, which holds for any group (A2). Compare A4 and A16: under F5 the same statement needs U finite or the condition of A16 on the unit orbits. Status: paper, unchecked. Source: stated by the author, 6 November 2026; argument written for this catalog from QUO 2.2 and RQ 4.5.

A19. Monks and Yazinski 2004: T (x/2, (3x+1)/2) has exactly one nontrivial autoconjugacy, the involution Ω = Φ∘V∘Φ^(-1) of Z_2 that complements parity vectors (Φ^(-1) the parity-vector conjugacy of Lagarias, V(x) = -1 - x Hedlund's automorphism of the shift); with x ~ y iff x = y or x = Ω(y), the induced map Ψ([x]) = [T(x)] on Z_2/~ and ξ = x/2 on even x, Ω(x) on odd x, the Collatz conjecture holds iff the ξ-orbit of every positive integer contains 1 iff the Ψ-orbit of the class of every positive integer contains [1] ("parity neutral"). Self-conjugate cycles (Ω(C) = C) are positive and {1, 2} is the only self-conjugate integer cycle, results with no counterpart in our work. The relation is not x ~ -x: T does not commute with -1, Ω is not multiplication by a unit, exchanges parity and does not preserve Z (Ω(3) = -4/9). This is the earlier instance of the move of A4 (descent and transfer for one group of order 2); ours uses the unit group, which is larger in general, simpler, and present in every ring, together with the results that depend on that choice (A8, A13, A14, Z[i]/U). Self-conjugate cycles are the ⟨Ω⟩ case of the stabilized cycles of A6. M. Monks 2009 and the SlidesV4 deck add a two-to-one endomorphism R of T whose fibres are the Ω-pairs, so the quotient of T by Ω is conjugate to T again (our inference from the slides). Status: literature (survey deck "The 3x+1 Problem", pages 7 to 13, read as page images; the paper itself, Discrete Math. 275 (2004) 219 to 236, unread); the equivalence sketch is ours (MON 2.4). Credit: Monks and Yazinski 2004, to be credited in full as the earlier quotient restatement on N. Source: MON 1, 2, 4, 5.

A20. Single trivial cycle by rotation: for a map S that commutes with the units with U finite (hence cyclic), p the period of [1] and ε = S^p(1), the rotation by u has |U|/ord(u^p ε) trivial cycles (A11), so some rotation has exactly one trivial cycle, a cycle through all the units, iff u^p ε generates U for some u. This holds always when p = 1 (take u = gε^(-1) for a generator g) or gcd(p, |U|) = 1 (u ↦ u^p is then a bijection of U). It can fail only when gcd(p, |U|) > 1: u^p ε then ranges over the coset εU^p of the proper subgroup U^p, which may contain no generator (in Z[i] this is the case p even and ε = ±1). The 5+2i map has p = 1 and its rotations by i and -i have one trivial cycle (R11). Whether a Collatz map with a failing case occurs is open (Q15). Status: paper (from A11). Source: QUO Cor 6.1; stated by the author, 6 November 2026.

### 7.2 Fundamental-domain maps

A21. Definition: a fundamental domain is a set F of nonzero elements with exactly one element in each class of associates (or in each odd class, for maps acting on odd classes); rep_F(w) is the element of F associate to w. A fundamental-domain map (F, α, k, d) has a digit table on the odd classes mod π^k that meet F, with π^k | αf + d(f), and S_F(f) = rep_F((αf + d(f))/π^v) on odd f ∈ F (T_F likewise); it is a Collatz map if every odd f ∈ F reaches rep_F(1) and rep_F(1) is periodic, which is Collatz's sense on R/U (A4). Every such map is a map on R/U. Domains used: in Z[i] the quadrant Q (a > 0, b ≥ 0), the mirrored quadrant R' = iQ̄, the cone C (-45° < arg ≤ 45°) and the primary elements P (z ≡ 1 mod 2+2i); in Z the positives and n ≡ 1 mod 4; in other rings half-planes, sectors and arithmetic domains (RT 2.1, 2.3), and in real quadratic rings the cone E = {σ_1(z) > 0, 1 ≤ |σ_1(z)/σ_2(z)| < ε^2} (RQ 2). Status: definition. Source: FD 2.1; RT 2.1; RQ 2.

A22. Symmetries of the family, for any fundamental domain F: (α, d) and (uα, ud) are the same map on F, so associates of a multiplier give nothing new; (F, α, d) and (uF, α, d^u) with d^u(z) = u d(u^(-1)z) induce the same map on R/U; conjugation gives σ̄ S̄_{F,α,d} σ̄ = S̄_{σF, ᾱ, σdσ}, so on C (closed under conjugation on odd elements) the family is closed under the mirror while on Q conjugate multipliers give different families. Distinct tables give distinct maps on F. The first statement uses nothing about U and holds in Z[√2] for the units ε^n. Status: paper (FD Proposition 2.3); computed (`fdbasics.py`: 885,600 comparisons for the first, 147,600 for the second, no difference; the R' runs reproduce the Q counts with conjugate multipliers; all 1560 Collatz maps on C come in mirror pairs; LOG fdbasics.txt, fdsummary.txt). Source: FD 2.2; RT 9.5.

A23. 3x+1 on N is the fundamental-domain map with F the positives, modulus 2, α = 3 and digit 1; it is the quotient of the commuting map L(z) = 3z + sgn(z), whose digit sgn(z) is a function of z mod 2^j for no j. No map of our form, at any modulus and with any multiplier, induces 3x+1 on N, and on the positives no fundamental-domain map at modulus 2 is induced by a map of our form (the positives satisfy (A) and (B) of A24, and a commuting table at modulus 2 would need d(1) = 0). On the other natural fundamental domain of the odd integers, n ≡ 1 mod 4, the same formula is the commuting map 3x ± 1 at modulus 4, below the window, a Collatz map for the trivial reason of A10; so on the 2-adic domain the Collatz formula is solved and the Collatz problem lives on the archimedean one. Status: paper (FD 3, Corollary 4.3, 4.3); computed for odd n ≤ 2·10^5 (`fdbasics.py`; LOG fdint.txt). Credit: not found (FD 10). Source: FD 3, 4.2, 4.3.

A24. Which fundamental-domain maps come from maps of our form: put D(uf) = u d(f) and L(z) = odd part of αz + D(z), a commuting map with L̄ = S̄_F. Conditions: (A) every f ∈ F has at least two elements of F congruent to it mod π^J for every J; (B) for every J, odd class mod π^J and unit u, uF meets the class. Under (A), if a map (α', k', d') of our form induces S̄_F, then α' = εα and d' = εD for a unit ε, so D is a residue-class table and the inducing map is a rotation of (α, k', D) (FD Theorem 4.1). Under (A) and (B) (quadrants, cones, the positives of Z) S_F is induced by a map of our form iff its table commutes, and then by (α, k, d) itself (FD Theorem 4.2). So the commuting maps of our form are the special case of the family in which D is a residue-class table, and below the threshold of U1 every fundamental-domain map on a quadrant or a cone is new. Status: paper (FD 4.1, 4.2; uses A8). Source: FD 4.

A25. Arithmetic fundamental domains: with m = κ + 1 and H a set of representatives of the cosets of the image of U in (R/π^m)^×, P = {z odd : z mod π^m ∈ H} is a fundamental domain for U on odd elements; the commuting extension of a table on P is a residue-class table mod π^max(k,m); for k ≥ m restriction and extension are inverse bijections between commuting tables of maps of our form and tables on P, and the two maps are Collatz maps together. In Z[i] this is the primary domain (m = 3): on P every fundamental-domain map at (1+i)^k, k ≥ 3, is a commuting map of our form, so by G26 none with N(α) < 29 inside the window is a Collatz map, and the first is the 5+2i map; at nominal modulus (1+i)^2 the maps are commuting maps at (1+i)^3 (the Collatz ones are below the window, G48) or grow. For k < m the exponent on P is not geometric and the window moves (on Z[√-2] at π the mean v is 2.50 or 1.50 against 2.00 on a half-plane). No arithmetic domain exists for an infinite U. Computed: P reproduced the counts of commuting maps of our form exactly in all seven comparisons (Z[√-2] at π^3, π^4; Z[θ] at θ^2, θ^3, θ^4; Z[φ] at 4; Z[ω] at 4), and in Z[i] at (1+i)^2, (1+i)^3, (1+i)^4 (2 of 44, 0 of 33, 2 of 14). ## 8. Smallest digits and ties

D1. If every odd class mod π^k has a unique element of least norm, the table of these elements commutes with the units. Status: paper; in general for any size invariant under U, with the count of commuting smallest tables as a product over orbits (GT 12, Proposition 12.1). It does not apply with an infinite U, where no Euclidean size is invariant (in Z[√2] digits were ordered by a^2 + 2b^2). Source: ST Lemma 1; GT 12.

D2. Least-norm elements of a class lie in the closed Voronoi cell of π^k R at 0, and ties occur only on its boundary. In Z[i]: odd k = 2j+1, cell |x| + |y| ≤ 2^j, boundary points even for j ≥ 1, so no ties (except at modulus 1+i); even k = 2j, cell |x|, |y| ≤ 2^(j-1), 2^j tied classes with two least elements each, 2^(2^j) smallest-digit tables, 2^(2^(j-2)) of them commuting (j ≥ 2). Status: paper. Credit: least-norm remainders and Voronoi ties are standard (Euclidean algorithm); the tie counts were not found; on Z the precedent is Matthews's L_d (least absolute residues, odd for odd d, tie at ±d/2 broken toward +d/2 for even d, so not odd). ## 17. Open questions

Q1. The 5+2i problem (H7). A second cycle with n odd steps and exponent V satisfies Π(α + d_j/z_j) = ε(1+i)^V, which for large z_j forces 2^V ≈ 29^n and arg(5+2i)^n ≈ Vπ/4 mod π/2; the angle condition has no analogue for 3x+1; Steiner and Simons-de Weger bounds might be adapted to both conditions with |ε(1+i)^V - α^n|. The interval step with both conditions gives at least 1901 odd steps (V10); further routes in Q16. Source: D1 13 item 1; D2 9 item 1; CB 3.

Q2. Is any map in the window a Collatz map? Every positive result is a finite check: 7x ± 1, the 251 integer maps, the five (1+i)^2 maps, the 305 at (1+i)^3, the 5+2i map and the 30 other commuting single-cycle maps at (1+i)^6, (1+i)^7, 5+2ω. Q3. Finiteness of cycles for the M12 maps and α = 11 at (1+i)^6: needs an effective lower bound for |επ^V - α^n|/|π|^V, a linear form in two logarithms (real α) or a logarithm and an angle; with a Baker-type bound M9 would make each check finite. Source: FAM 5; D2 9 item 3.

Q4. Does α = 3 admit a Collatz map at modulus (1+i)^3 (G21)? Source: M3 7, 8.

Q5. Is there a Collatz map on Z with multiplier -3 at modulus 2 (Z4)? Source: ST 6.

Q7.
