### 18.2 Why (what rounds m3 and m4 found; not decisions)

- **Every gcd of the gcd-first arithmetic has a denominator, or a divisor of one, as an argument** (v0.3 §16.0).
- **Full factoring of numerators is what made Pack-primes slow:** trial division was 97–98% of its mul, sop and pos instructions, on 43–50-bit product numerators. Product denominators were smooth, and their factoring cost was negligible (m3-cost-breakdown, answer 3 and weak point 1).
- **Exponent-list merges saved nothing:** Pack-primes' merge path cost 192M instructions on mul at $n = 64$, against Control's whole binary-gcd bill of 151M (m3-cost-breakdown, answer 3 and weak point 8). Item 4b rules out exponent merges.
- **Pack-coprime's eager base refinement** was 84–92% of every operation (m3-cost-breakdown, weak point 2). Item 3 finds factors over fixed primes only, so there is no base to refine.
- **v0.3 stored the factors in a second structure** per matrix, (f), with $W + 1$ offsets of 32 bits. Item 2 puts them in the list's entries instead.
- **Why 2 and 3** (item 16, the author's rationale): Packed-23 is a proof of concept.
  - 2 and 3 account for about 71% of the drawn numbers' **distinct prime divisors**. Each prime $p$ divides a drawn value with probability $1/p$, and $(1/2 + 1/3)/(1/2 + 1/3 + 1/5 + 1/7) \approx 0.71$, before the cap's rejection.
  - Counted with multiplicity, the share is about 0.78: $E[\nu_p] = 1/(p - 1)$, and $(1 + 1/2)/(1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/6) \approx 0.78$.
  - The rest, 5 and 7, exercises the unfactored part path.
  - The draw over the primes up to 7 also gives far less overflow at $n = 64$. v0.4 §17.10's simulation finds at most about 0.05% of mul's cells, 0.39% of sop's and 0.93% of pos's marked at $n = 64$, and none in add.
- **The inner sums of mul were about 70% of Control's gcd work in multiplication, on m3's draw** (v0.3 §16.6 item 4). Item 11 brings them within reach of S1.

### 18.3 The packed list's entries (items 2, 5 and 10; 1c; C4)

**Entry $k$ of the stored list** (after the rewrite of §18.4) has five fixed fields. They are laid out in this order (coordinator's call C4):

| # | Field | Symbol | Width (C4) | Byte offset in the entry | Holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | flag | $\phi_k$ | 1 byte | 0 | 1 if the entry's factors are present (§18.4), 0 otherwise |
| 2 | whole number | $v_k$ | $w$ bits, the list's narrowed width | 1 | the value, as in Packed |
| 3 | exponent of 2 | $e_{2,k}$ | 1 byte | $1 + w/8$ | $\nu_2(v_k)$ when $\phi_k = 1$; 0 otherwise |
| 4 | exponent of 3 | $e_{3,k}$ | 1 byte | $2 + w/8$ | $\nu_3(v_k)$ when $\phi_k = 1$; 0 otherwise |
| 5 | unfactored part | $r_k$ | $w$ bits | $3 + w/8$ | when $\phi_k = 1$, $v_k / (2^{e_{2,k}} 3^{e_{3,k}})$, the unfactored part left after dividing out 2 and 3, which holds every other prime factor, so $\gcd(r_k, 6) = 1$; 0 otherwise |

- **Size:** no padding and no alignment (C4), so an entry is $E(w) = 2w/8 + 3$ bytes (§18.8). Entry $k$ starts at byte $k\,E(w)$, and its whole number at byte $k\,E(w) + 1$.
- **Flag:** $\phi_k = 0$: only $v_k$ is used. $\phi_k = 1$: the exponents and $r_k$ are used (item 2). $v_k$ is kept in every entry.
- **Unused fields:** an entry with $\phi_k = 0$ holds 0 in its exponent and unfactored part fields (C4), so its bytes are fixed for the model check.
- $\nu_p$ is the exponent of $p$ in $v$ (the $p$-adic valuation).
- **What the fields' facts give** (not decisions):
  - for $v < 2^{63}$: $e_2 \le 62$, $e_3 \le 39$. So each exponent fits one byte, the author's example (item 5);
  - $r_k \le v_k$, so the unfactored part fits at the whole number's width $w$.
- **During the build** (item 10), (l) is exactly Packed's: $2rc$ whole numbers of 64 bits, with nothing else. All of v0.4 §17.3–17.5 applies as written:
  - the probe compares $\text{l}[k] = v$;
  - a new value is appended at position $W$;
  - the widest value is tracked.
- **At the pack** (v0.4 §17.3, unchanged): (h) is freed; the list is narrowed to $w$ and shrunk to $W$ entries; the fields are narrowed. Then comes §18.4's pass, which changes the list's layout.
- **Reads by handle** read $v_k$ at width $w$, sign-extended, as Packed reads (l), and the other fields at their widths.

### 18.4 Finding the factors (items 3, 10 and 12; C1, C3, C8)

**When** (item 10): in one pass at the end of each matrix's build, right after the pack. Each matrix is factored before the next build begins.
- For an input: inside its last `enter` call, after the pack.
- For an intermediate or a result: at the end of its `madd` or `mmul` call, after the pack.

**The pass, in this order:**
1. **Grow** (C8). The list's allocation grows from $W\,w/8$ to $W\,E(w)$ bytes. This is an **accounting event**: the list's accounted bytes rise to $W\,E(w)$ at that call, as v0.2 §15.6 counts a shrinking `realloc`.
   - **No second list** holds for the accounted bytes: the model has one list at every moment.
   - The heap may differ. A growing `realloc` may move the block, and then old and new coexist inside that call. The heap peak (v0.2 §15.6) is reported as measured, with any such copy in it.
2. **Rewrite in place** (coordinator's call C1). One backward pass over the entries, $k = W - 1, \ldots, 0$:
   - read entry $k$'s whole number from byte $k\,w/8$; then write entry $k$ at byte $k\,E(w)$, its whole number at byte $k\,E(w) + 1$, and its other fields as 0;
   - *why nothing is lost:* entry $k$ is read before it is written. Every entry $j < k$, not yet read, ends at byte $(j + 1)\,w/8 \le k\,w/8 \le k\,E(w)$, so the write of entry $k$ never reaches it.
3. **Walk the cells' denominator fields** (C8), in cell order (row-major). For each, take its handle $k$:
   - if $\phi_k = 0$ and $v_k \ne 0$, factor entry $k$ (below) and set $\phi_k = 1$;
   - otherwise go on.

   So each whole number used as a denominator is factored once, at its first meeting. An entry used only as a numerator keeps $\phi_k = 0$.

**Factoring entry $k$:** the shared routine **divide out** (C3), with no limit. It is the same routine as S1's step 1 (§18.5), which runs it with the limit $e_p(z)$:

```text
function divide_out(m, p, limit)             m >= 0; p is 2 or 3
    let i = 0, q = 1
    while i < limit and m mod p = 0          one remainder per test
        m <- m / p                           an exact division
        q <- q * p                           the power found, p^i
        i <- i + 1
    return (m, i, q)
end function
```

- Here: $(m, e_{2,k}, \cdot) = \text{divide\_out}(v_k, 2, \infty)$, then $(r_k, e_{3,k}, \cdot) = \text{divide\_out}(m, 3, \infty)$.
- **Nothing more:** no trial division beyond 3, no full factoring, no primality test, no table.

**The mark's guard** (item 12): an entry whose whole number is 0, the overflow mark's denominator (v0.4 §17.10 rule 1), is never factored. Its $\phi$ stays 0. *Why:* $0 \bmod 2 = 0$ always, so an unlimited loop would never stop.

**Free factors:** a numerator equal to a denominator of the same matrix is the same entry of (l), so it has the denominator's factors with no extra work.

**Timing** (item 10):
- the grow, the rewrite, the walk and the factoring are part of measure 1 for an input, and of measures 2–5 for an intermediate or result, as a user entering or computing the matrix would pay;
- they are never part of the workload's generation.

**Cost** (a fact): one read per cell's denominator field. Each entry factored costs at most $e_2 + e_3 + 2$ remainders and $e_2 + e_3$ exact divisions, all factor work (§18.5).

### 18.5 Reduction (items 4, 11 and 15; C2, C5, C6, C7)

**Notation.** For a denominator $z$ whose factors are at hand (a stored entry with $\phi = 1$, or a temporary's carried factors), $F(z) = (e_2(z), e_3(z), r_z)$.

**S1, reduction by the denominator's factors: the model's general path (item 4a).** It gives $k = \gcd(u, z)$ for a value $u$ and a factored denominator $z$.
- *Where:* every gcd of add and mul with a factored denominator as an argument (C5; the table below).
- *Path:* it works on $\lvert u \rvert$, as the new gcd works on magnitudes.
  1. $(u_1, i_2, q_2) = \text{divide\_out}(\lvert u \rvert, 2, e_2(z))$, then $(u', i_3, q_3) = \text{divide\_out}(u_1, 3, e_3(z))$ (§18.4's routine).
  2. $r_k = \gcd(u', r_z)$ by the new gcd of v0.4 §17.7 (item 15), unless S2 applies.
  3. $k = (q_2 q_3)\, r_k$: two multiplications. $F(k) = (i_2, i_3, r_k)$ is known with it.
- *Exact:*
  - since $2^{e_2(z)} 3^{e_3(z)}$ and $r_z$ are coprime, $\gcd(u, z) = \gcd(u, 2^{e_2(z)} 3^{e_3(z)}) \cdot \gcd(u, r_z) = 2^{i_2} 3^{i_3} \gcd(u, r_z)$, with $i_p = \min(\nu_p(u), e_p(z))$;
  - since $\gcd(r_z, 6) = 1$, $\gcd(u, r_z) = \gcd(u', r_z)$;
  - every division is exact, and $q_2$, $q_3$, $q_2 q_3$ and $k$ divide $z$, so no step can overflow;
  - for $u = 0$ the loops stop at $e_p(z)$ steps, and $k = z$, as Control's $\gcd(0, z) = z$.
- *What it costs:*
  - up to $e_2(z) + e_3(z) + 2$ remainders and $e_2(z) + e_3(z)$ exact divisions;
  - the products $q_2 q_3$ and $(q_2 q_3)\, r_k$;
  - then one new gcd on $u'$ and $r_z$. Here $u'$ may be up to 63 bits, and $r_z$ is 1 or small whenever $z$ is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth or nearly so. With sizes that far apart, the new gcd's 6a case reduces $u'$ by one remainder first (v0.4 §17.7).
- *Why it can be faster:* the binary gcd's work grows with the operands' bit lengths, and the 2s and 3s of $z$ no longer enter it.

**Special case S2, the unfactored part is 1 (item 15): the model's one special case.**
- *Condition:* inside S1, $r_z = 1$, that is, $z$ is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth.
- *Path:* no gcd call. $r_k = 1$ and $k = q_2 q_3$, from S1's step 1 alone.
- *Exact:* $\gcd(u', 1) = 1$ for every $u'$.
- *Why it is faster:* it saves the gcd call, on every reduction against a $\{2, 3\}$-smooth denominator.
- *Where it arises:* under v0.4's draw, a denominator is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth unless 5 or 7 divides it (§18.9).
- *Note:* this special case is for this build. It may be short-lived, depending on how unfactored parts are handled later (for example in the variants of §18.12).

**Which path each gcd takes** (item 4b; coordinator's call C5). A gcd with a factored denominator as an argument uses S1; every other gcd uses the new gcd on the values. No gcd merges exponents. Control's cases, their order and its values are unchanged (tc3's arithmetic, v0.4 §17.1); only the way each gcd's value is obtained changes.

| Where | Control's gcd | Packed-23 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| entry of an input (v0.1 §8) | $\gcd(a, b)$ | the new gcd on the values: no factors exist until after the pack (item 10) |
| add, `den` ($b = d$) | $h = \gcd(t, d)$, $t = s_x a + s_y c$ | S1 with $F(d)$ |
| add, `num`, `general` | $g = \gcd(b, d)$ | S1, $u = d$, with $F(b)$; it gives $F(g)$ |
| add, `num` ($g > 1$) | $h = \gcd(t, g)$, $t = s_x d' + s_y b'$ | S1 with $F(g)$ |
| add, `general` ($g > 1$) | $h = \gcd(t, g)$, $t = s_x a d' + s_y c b'$ | S1 with $F(g)$ |
| mul, `cross_ad` ($a = d$), `int_y` | $h = \gcd(c, b)$ | S1 with $F(b)$ |
| mul, `cross_cb` ($c = b$), `int_x` | $h = \gcd(a, d)$ | S1 with $F(d)$ |
| mul, `general` | $g_1 = \gcd(a, d)$, $g_2 = \gcd(c, b)$ | S1 with $F(d)$, and S1 with $F(b)$ |
| mul's inner sums, `add(s, p)` | as add | as add, with $s$'s and $p$'s carried factors |
| add steps of sop and pos; add jobs | as add | as add, with the stored operands' entries |

- **$F(g)$:** formed only when $g > 1$. With $g = 1$, Control's branch makes no $h$, and nothing reads $F(g)$.
- **Where the factors come from:**
  - a stored operand's denominator: its entry, which has $\phi = 1$ by §18.4;
  - in mul, $A$'s from the row buffer, which holds each decoded cell's denominator factors (coordinator's call C2), and $B$'s by handle;
  - a temporary's: its carried factors.
- **Read counters** (v0.4 §17.6's rule). The factor fields are read with their denominator's entry, by handle for $B$ and from the row buffer for $A$. They add no read count. So every read counter (cross reads, K-Q3 reads) equals what Packed's by-handle reads give.
- **The mark:** an operand that is the mark is caught by v0.4 §17.10 rule 2's test $d = 0$ first, so S1 never sees the mark's denominator.

**Temporaries' factors (item 11).** In mul, the product $p$ and the running sum $s$ each hold their value and their denominator's factor fields: a flag, $e_2$, $e_3$ and the unfactored part. These are data, part of the temporary's value, not a structure (item 14). They never hold their numerator's factors.
- **Where the rule applies** (C7): only to mul's product and running sum (item 11), and only to a temporary that a later addition of the same cell reads:
  - every product, when $n \ge 2$;
  - every running sum but the cell's last.
- **Where it does not apply:** $F$ is not formed for the results of add jobs, the add steps of sop and pos, a cell's last $s$ (which is stored, and whose denominator is factored by §18.4's pass on the result), or the one product of a cell at $n = 1$.
- A temporary's flag is always 1: a temporary is never the mark (v0.4 §17.10 rule 2).
- $F$ is formed after the case's value. A cell that overflows stops and is stored as the mark (v0.4 §17.10 rule 4), and no factors are formed for it.

**The rule, case by case** (coordinator's call C7). $x = s_x a/b$ and $y = s_y c/d$ are the operands, and $F$ of the result's denominator is given in the same order as Control forms that denominator. So every unfactored part product and quotient divides a value Control computes, and no intermediate exceeds it. Two forms recur:
- **product:** $F(z_1 z_2) = (e_p(z_1) + e_p(z_2),\ r_{z_1} \cdot r_{z_2})$;
- **quotient by an S1 gcd:** $F(z / k) = (e_p(z) - e_p(k),\ r_z / r_k)$.

The last column counts each case's factor work. "Exp" means one exponent addition or subtraction per prime, two in all; "mul" is a multiplication; "div" an exact division.

*Multiplication* (Control's order):

| Case | Control's denominator | $F$ of it | Factor work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zero` | 1 | $(0, 0, 1)$ | none |
| `one_x` | $d$ | $F(d)$ | none (a copy) |
| `one_y` | $b$ | $F(b)$ | none |
| `int_both` | 1 | $(0, 0, 1)$ | none |
| `both` ($a = d$, $c = b$) | 1 | $(0, 0, 1)$ | none |
| `den` ($b = d$) | $d \cdot d$ | $(2 e_p(d),\ r_d \cdot r_d)$ | exp, 1 mul |
| `num` ($a = c$) | $b \cdot d$ | $(e_p(b) + e_p(d),\ r_b \cdot r_d)$ | exp, 1 mul |
| `cross_ad` | $b / h$, $h = \gcd(c, b)$ | $(e_p(b) - e_p(h),\ r_b / r_h)$ | S1; exp, 1 div |
| `cross_cb` | $d / h$, $h = \gcd(a, d)$ | $(e_p(d) - e_p(h),\ r_d / r_h)$ | S1; exp, 1 div |
| `int_x` | $d / h$, $h = \gcd(a, d)$ | $(e_p(d) - e_p(h),\ r_d / r_h)$ | S1; exp, 1 div |
| `int_y` | $b / h$, $h = \gcd(c, b)$ | $(e_p(b) - e_p(h),\ r_b / r_h)$ | S1; exp, 1 div |
| `general` | $(b / g_2) \cdot (d / g_1)$ | $(e_p(b) - e_p(g_2) + e_p(d) - e_p(g_1),\ (r_b / r_{g_2}) \cdot (r_d / r_{g_1}))$ | two S1; 3 exp, 2 div, 1 mul |

*Addition* (Control's order; in mul's inner sums both operands are temporaries):

| Case | Control's denominator | $F$ of it | Factor work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `zero` | 1 | $(0, 0, 1)$ | none |
| `zero_x` | $d$ | $F(d)$ | none |
| `zero_y` | $b$ | $F(b)$ | none |
| `int_both` | 1 | $(0, 0, 1)$ | none |
| `int_x` | $d$ | $F(d)$ | none |
| `int_y` | $b$ | $F(b)$ | none |
| `cross` ($g = 1$ known) | $b \cdot d$ | $(e_p(b) + e_p(d),\ r_b \cdot r_d)$ | exp, 1 mul |
| `den` ($b = d$) | $d / h$, $h = \gcd(t, d)$ | $(e_p(d) - e_p(h),\ r_d / r_h)$ | S1; exp, 1 div |
| `num`, `general`, $g = 1$ | $b \cdot d$ | $(e_p(b) + e_p(d),\ r_b \cdot r_d)$ | S1 for $g$; exp, 1 mul |
| `num`, `general`, $g > 1$ | $(b / g) \cdot (d / h)$, $h = \gcd(t, g)$ | $((e_p(b) - e_p(g)) + (e_p(d) - e_p(h)),\ (r_b / r_g) \cdot (r_d / r_h))$ | two S1; 3 exp, 2 div, 1 mul |

- *Why the last row holds:* Control's denominator is $b' \cdot (d/h)$ with $b' = b/g$. Its factors are $b'$'s, $(e_p(b) - e_p(g),\ r_b / r_g)$, times $(d/h)$'s, $(e_p(d) - e_p(h),\ r_d / r_h)$. $F(g)$ comes from S1 on $\gcd(b, d)$, and $F(h)$ from S1 on $\gcd(t, g)$. Since $h \mid g \mid d$, $r_h \mid r_d$.
- *Divisions:* every division in the tables is exact. $r_k$ divides $r_z$ by S1.

**Factor work is counted apart from the core** (coordinator's call C6, as v0.3 §16.5). It covers:
- S1's remainders, exact divisions and the products $q_2 q_3$ and $(q_2 q_3)\, r_k$;
- §18.4's remainders and exact divisions;
- every exponent operation, multiplication and exact division in the tables above, case by case.

The core's counters (multiplications, additions, exact divisions) stay Control's. The new gcd's calls on unfactored parts are counted as gcds, reported per model (§18.13).

### 18.6 The dot sum (item 1h)

As v0.4 §17.1 item 11 and §17.6, for Control, Packed and Packed-23 alike:
- each product cell is summed left to right, starting with the first product: $s = \text{product}(0)$, then $s = \text{add}(s, \text{product}(t))$ for $t = 1, \ldots, n - 1$;
- there is no $0/1$ start and no `zero_x` addition, so each cell makes $n - 1$ additions;
- overflow stops the cell, and the terms after it are not computed (v0.4 §17.10 rule 4).

In Packed-23, $s$ starts with the first product's carried factors (item 11). The cell is stored as Packed stores it. Its denominator's factors come from §18.4's pass on the result, not from the temporary (item 10).

### 18.7 Structures (RULES, Algorithm design 4)

**The cells are the matrix itself** and are not counted (v0.4 §17.8).

| Model | Stored, per matrix | Temporary, per operation | Local, per function |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Control** | none beyond its cells (the pairs) | none | none |
| **Packed** | (l) the packed list (v0.4 §17.8) | (h) the index table, from the build's `create` to its pack | mul's row buffer, freed before the pack, counted in the accounted peak |
| **Packed-23** | **(l) the packed list** (§18.3): during the build, $2rc$ whole numbers of 64 bits, as Packed's; after the pack, $W$ five-field entries, rewritten in place (C1). **No second structure** (item 2) | **(h) the index table**, as Packed's | **mul's row buffer**, as Packed's, each decoded cell also holding its denominator's flag, exponents and unfactored part (C2). **The only local** (item 14) |

- One (h) exists at a time, as v0.4 §17.8. §18.4's pass runs after (h) is freed and rewrites (l) in place. So no replacement list exists in the accounted bytes; the heap is as §18.4 says.
- **Data, not structures** (item 14). A data type is not a data structure. Packed-23's data types are:
  - a whole number;
  - a rational: a numerator and a denominator;
  - a denominator's factor fields: a flag, two exponents and an unfactored part.

  Mul's product and running sum each carry their denominator's factor fields as part of their value, as they carry their numerator and denominator. So they add no structure, and mul's function has one local, the row buffer.
- **Not structures** (follows from the items; v0.3 §16.3 called such things scratch): the counters, powers and quotients of `divide_out`, S1 and C7, and $F(g)$ and $F(h)$. These are a few machine words, in local variables, like a temporary's value in mul's dot sum in every model.
- **Nothing else exists in Packed-23:** no second list, no table of factors, no prime table, no cache, no index from factors to entries.
- **The test build's audit copies are allowed**, as v0.4 §17.8.
- The driver's arrays are as v0.1 §3.

### 18.8 Entry size and memory (item 5; C1, C2, C4, C8)

**Bytes per stored entry** (C4), $E(w) = 2 w / 8 + 3$, against Packed's $w / 8$:

| $w$ | Packed | Packed-23 | Ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 1 | 5 | 5.0 |
| 16 | 2 | 7 | 3.5 |
| 32 | 4 | 11 | 2.75 |
| 64 | 8 | 19 | 2.375 |

**During a build** (item 10), Packed-23 holds exactly Packed's structures: cells $4n^2$, (h) $4n^2$, and (l) at $2rc \times 8 = 16n^2$ on the sweep. So **an input's build peak is Packed's, 1.5 times Control's $16n^2$.** The five-field entries appear only after (h) is freed, and the input's whole $W\,E(w)$ is below that peak.

**During an operation**, the accounted peak is the larger of two moments:
- **the result's build:** Packed's figure, plus the inputs' larger stored lists, $(E(w) - w/8)\,W$ more bytes per input, plus the larger row buffer in mul;
- **the result's rewrite** (C1): the inputs, the result's packed cells, and its list at $W\,E(w)$ after the grow (C8).

**The row buffer** is counted in the accounted peak while it exists (v0.4 §17.8), during the result's build in mul. Each of its $n$ decoded cells also holds its denominator's flag, two exponents and 64-bit unfactored part (C2): at least 11 bytes more per cell than Packed's. At 24 bytes per decoded cell in Packed (v0.4 §17.6), that is 35 bytes per cell, 2,240 bytes at $n = 64$.

*A bound* (a fact): a result with $W$ near $2rc$ at $w = 64$ has a stored list of up to $38n^2$ bytes. With its cells at $4n^2$, the rewrite moment is then up to about $42n^2$ plus the inputs, against Control's $48n^2$.

*Example* (non-normative): m3's mul workload, $n = 64$, job 10, from DECISIONS-m4's figures (m3's draw, not this round's). The inputs have $W \approx 1{,}009$ at $w = 16$; the result has $W = 4{,}189$ at $w = 64$. Control's peak is 196,608 bytes. Packed's is 135,110 (0.687), without its row buffer, which that build did not account.

| Moment | Packed-23, bytes | × Control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the result's build: inputs $2 \times 23{,}447$, cells 16,384, (h) 16,384, (l) 65,536, row buffer 2,240 | about 147,400 | **0.75** (the peak) |
| the result's rewrite: inputs 46,894, cells 16,384, list $4{,}189 \times 19 = 79{,}591$ | about 142,900 | 0.73 |

**Once packed** (measure 6, stable bytes of one input):
- Under v0.4's draw, every input value is at most 32767, so $w \le 16$, and its entries are at most 7 bytes against Packed's 2.
- Every value is 7-smooth, and there are 497 such values up to 32767. So $W \le \min(2n^2, 497)$.
- Upper bounds, at $w = 16$ and that $W$:

| $n$ | Control | Packed | Packed-23 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | 4,096 | $\le$ 2,018 | $\le$ 4,503 (up to 1.10 times Control's) |
| 32 | 16,384 | $\le$ 5,090 | $\le$ 7,575 |
| 64 | 65,536 | $\le$ 17,378 | $\le$ 19,863 |

The cells are $4n^2$ at 16-bit handles, the case wherever $W > 128$; the rest is $W\,E(16)$. The actual $W$ is known only once the inputs are generated.

**Entries with $\phi = 0$** carry their exponent and unfactored part fields as zeros (item 2: fixed fields; C4).

### 18.9 Workloads and overflow (item 6)

- **Workloads exactly as v0.4 §17.9, for every model:** numerators and denominators drawn the same way, from the primes up to 7: 2, 3, 5 and 7. Each prime $p$ divides with probability $1/p$ and repeats with probability $1/p$, capped at 32767, with a random sign on the numerator. The order of random calls and the seeds are as v0.4 §17.9. Every model reads the same numbers.
- **64 bits**, as every model (v0.4).
- **Overflow** as v0.4 §17.10: the mark in the result's cells, with the counters "new" and "passed on", the same way in every model, with the job going on. The set of marked cells must be identical in every model (v0.4 §17.10 rule 6).
- **The mark is never factored** (item 12, §18.4).
- **Facts** (not decisions):
  - finding factors (§18.4), S1, S2 and the factor rule (§18.5) make only remainders, exact divisions, and products that divide a value Control computes. So none can overflow, and the marked cells are Control's;
  - a denominator of this draw is 7-smooth, so its unfactored part is a product of 5s and 7s, and is 1 when the denominator is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth;
  - before the cap's rejection, a drawn value is $\{2, 3\}$-smooth with probability $(1 - 1/5)(1 - 1/7) \approx 0.69$.

### 18.13 Correctness, counters and tests (C6, C9; v0.4 §17.12 adapted)

**Against Control, per operation** (coordinator's call C6):

**Counters reported for Packed-23** (counting build), per matrix, operation and step:
- S1 calls and S2 hits;
- the factor work of §18.5, by kind (remainders, exact divisions, multiplications, exponent operations) and by case;
- the new gcd's calls and work on unfactored parts;
- per matrix: entries factored, cells walked, and the list's bytes before and after the grow.

**Against the model** (the simulator):

**Audits** (test build):

- every entry with $\phi = 0$ holds 0 in its unused fields;
- 0 is never factored;
- (l) has $W$ entries of $E(w)$ bytes, $w$ the narrowest width that holds the widest whole number;

### 18.16 Instructions to builders and reviewers (coordinator's call C10; replacing v0.4 §17.14's for Packed-23)

> **Build Packed-23 as Packed (v0.4), unchanged in its build, pack, gcd, dot sum and overflow recording, and add only what follows.**

>   - rewrite the list in place in one backward pass, reading each entry before writing it, with unused fields 0;

> - Add no other structure, cache, table or shortcut.
