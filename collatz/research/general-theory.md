# General theory: the ring-level claims, with hypotheses

Working notes, 6 November 2026. Nothing here is in the papers or the catalog. This file audits every invariant the catalog states across rings (Section 2), states each with explicit hypotheses, and supplies the proofs that were missing (Sections 3 to 14). Section 15 describes the abstract Lean layer, which proves the group-action theorems once for all rings and derives the earlier Z[i] theorems from them. Sources are cited with the keys of `catalog.md` Section 0 (TH, GP, QUO, IC `integer-census.md`, ST `symmetry-test.md`, FD `fundamental-domain.md`, RT `rings-test.md`, IAP, FAM, D2) and catalog ids (W5, T1, ...).

## 1. Settings and hypotheses

The hypotheses below are referred to by their labels. Each theorem names the ones it uses and no others.

(D) $R$ is an integral domain.

(P) $\pi \in R$ is a prime element, $P = \pi R$, the residue field $R/P$ is finite of size $q$, and $\bigcap_n \pi^n R = 0$ (true when $R$ is Noetherian, by Krull's intersection theorem). An element is odd if $\pi \nmid z$, even otherwise. For $z \ne 0$, $v(z)$ is the largest $v$ with $\pi^v \mid z$, finite by the last condition, and the odd part of $z$ is $z/\pi^{v(z)}$.

(Dd) $R$ is a Dedekind domain and $P$ a nonzero prime ideal (not necessarily principal), with $v_P$ its valuation. Used only in Section 4.

(G) $U$ is a subgroup of the unit group $R^\times$. (Gf): $U$ is finite. When nothing is said, $U = R^\times$.

(N) $N : R \to \mathbb{N}$ is multiplicative, $N(x) = 0$ only for $x = 0$, $N(u) = 1$ for $u \in U$, and $N(\pi) = q \ge 2$. For an order $R$ in a number field $K$ one takes $N(z) = |R/zR| = |N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(z)|$, which has these properties and also $N(z) = 1$ exactly for units.

(O) $R$ is an order in a number field $K$ of degree $n$, with archimedean places $v$, absolute values $|\cdot|_v$ (the usual one on $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ through an embedding), local degrees $n_v \in \{1, 2\}$, so that $\prod_v |x|_v^{n_v} = |N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(x)|$.

(A1) (O) with a single archimedean place: $K = \mathbb{Q}$ or $K$ imaginary quadratic. Then $R$ is a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb{C}$, $N(z) = |z|^n$, $|\pi| = q^{1/n} > 1$, and $U$ is finite.

(S) $\sigma$ is a ring automorphism of $R$, of any order. It fixes $P$ exactly when $\sigma(\pi) = \eta\pi$ for a unit $\eta$, the factor $\eta = \sigma(\pi)/\pi$.

Maps. A map $(\alpha, k, d)$ is as in F2 with $R$, $\pi$ in place of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, $1+i$: $\alpha$ odd, $k \ge 0$, $d$ a function on odd elements depending only on the class modulo $\pi^k$ with $\pi^k \mid \alpha z + d(z)$, $T(z) = z/\pi$ on even $z$ and $\alpha z + d(z)$ on odd $z$, and $S(z)$ the odd part of $\alpha z + d(z)$ ($S(z) = 0$ if that is $0$). These are the Barina-type maps of RT. When the generator matters it is written $S^{\pi}_{\alpha, d}$. A cycle is trivial if it meets $U$; $f$ is a Collatz map on a set $X$ if every $z \in X$ has an iterate on a trivial cycle (QUO Definition 3.1). The map commutes with $U$ if $f(uz) = uf(z)$, and respects associates if $f(uz) \in Uf(z)$, for $u \in U$.

The abstract setting of Sections 7, 8 and 15: a set $X$, a group $G$ acting on $X$, a point $e \in X$, and $f : X \to X$. The rings are the case $X = R$, $G = U$ acting by multiplication, $e = 1$, so that $Ge = U$. The orbit $Ge$ is free when $ge = e$ only for $g = 1$, which holds in a domain for $e = 1$.

## 2. The audit

Each item gives the claim with its catalog ids, the general hypothesis under which it is true, where a general proof stands, which rings have only a ring-specific proof, what `rings-test.md` found, and what this file adds. "Lean" means a proof in `lean/GaussianCollatz/`; before this file every Lean proof was for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (and a few integer facts).

1. Digits and the existence of maps (F3, F4). Every digit is odd, $D$ has one element in each odd class, $d(z)$ is the element of $D$ in the class of $-\alpha z$, and every odd $\alpha$ has a map at every modulus. Hypotheses (D), (P), $k \ge 1$. General proof: GP 9, Lemma 9.1, and GP Proposition 2.1 (first sentence); GP states the setting as $\mathbb{Z}$ or imaginary quadratic, but the proof uses only (D) and (P). Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: no exception. Added: Lemma 3.1 (counting classes and inverting odd elements modulo $\pi^k$, used throughout).

2. The existence threshold (U1, U2). A map commuting with $U$ exists at $P^k$ for some (equivalently every) odd $\alpha$ iff $k > \kappa = \max_{u \ne 1} v_P(u - 1)$ iff $U$ acts freely on the odd classes. Hypotheses (Dd), (Gf). General proof: IC Theorem 1. The thresholds were computed ring by ring (IC 2.3: $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, and $\kappa = e$ for imaginary quadratic fields with $U = \{\pm 1\}$). Infinite $U$: no modulus admits a map commuting with all of $U$ (IC 2.3 item 5, general, needs $R/P^k$ finite). Rings-test: confirmed in all seven rings, including the negative form for $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$. Lean only for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ at $k \le 2$ (`prop1`) and the units modulo $\pi^3$. Added: Proposition 4.2, $\kappa = e(P/2)$ for every prime above 2 in every ring of integers with $-1 \in U$ (so commuting maps start at $P^{e+1}$ in every number ring), and the value for primes above odd $p$.

3. Which moduli carry multipliers (W11, W10). The modulus $\pi^k$ carries a multiplier in the window iff $(q^k, q^{k+1/(q-1)})$ contains the norm of an odd element. Hypotheses (D), (P), (N). General proof: GP Proposition 2.1, first sentence (the definition of the window plus item 1). Ring-specific: $\mathbb{Z}$ (every $k \ge 1$), $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ (exactly $k \ge 2$) in GP 2; $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$ ($\pi^2$ empty, since odd norms are $1, 3 \bmod 8$) in RT 5.2; $\theta$ empty in $\mathbb{Z}[\theta]$ (3 is inert) in RT 6.2; the others computed. Rings-test refinement for the split ring: odd elements can have even norm, so the principle must be read with norms of odd elements, as GP states it, and not with odd integers (W12's "odd norms are $1 \bmod 4$" is a fact about $\mathbb{Z}[i]$). Missing: any statement that moduli eventually carry multipliers. Added: Theorem 5.2, in every order of a number field all moduli with $(q^{1/(n(q-1))} - 1)q^{k/n} > 2$ carry a rational-integer multiplier, so only finitely many moduli are empty.

4. The window and the exponent $1/(q-1)$ (W3, W4, W5, W14). Two parts. The exact part: for every odd $\alpha$ and table, the Markov matrix on classes modulo $\pi^k$ is doubly stochastic and irreducible, and $v - k$ is geometric with mean $1/(q-1)$ in density. Hypotheses (D), (P). General proof: GP Lemma 4.1 and IC 2.4 (GP states a narrower setting; the proof uses Lemma 3.1 only). The heuristic part, that the sign of $\log_2 N(\alpha) - (k + 1/(q-1))\log_2 q$ decides convergence, needs that the size of an element is one absolute value, (A1). Rings-test: the exponent $1/3$ confirmed directly in $\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$ ($q = 4$); in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ the $|N|$ window is the wrong criterion for residue-class maps on $R$ (a multiplier expanding in one embedding diverges in it), while on $R/U$ with the cone the $|N|$ window works. Added: Section 6.2, the drift per archimedean place, whose weighted sum is the norm drift; with several places the per-place drifts are the heuristic criterion for maps on $R$.

5. Below the window (W1). If $N(\alpha) < q^k$, every orbit is eventually periodic. Proof only for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (D2 Proposition 3) and, on $\mathbb{Z}$, Leigh's Lemma 3; not in Lean. Hypothesis needed: (A1). Missing: the general proof. Added: Theorem 6.3 under (A1), Theorem 6.4 under (O) with the per-place condition $|\alpha|_v < |\pi|_v^k$ (and $|\pi|_v > 1$) at every archimedean place, and an exception to the norm form: in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ the map with $\alpha = 3 + 2\sqrt 2$ at modulus $2$ has $|N(\alpha)| = 1 < 4$ and 182 of 200 starts escape (Section 6.4, `ringbelow.py`).

6. The trivial-cycle bound and its divisor form (T1, T2, A7). Any map has at most $|U|$ trivial cycles; for a map commuting with $U$ their number is $|U|/|H|$ with $H = \langle T^p(1)\rangle$ the stabilizer. Hypotheses: for the bound, only that $U$ is a finite set; for the divisor form, (G), (Gf) and commuting. General proof: ST Theorem 2 (stated for any unit group), QUO 6.3. Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ only (`trivial_cycles_le_four`, `trivial_cycle_count` as three explicit cases). Rings-test: every literal run in rings with finite $U$ had at most $|U|$ trivial cycles and every commuting case matched $|U|/\mathrm{ord}(\varepsilon)$; with infinite $U$ ($\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$) maps have 4 to 12 trivial cycles, so the bound has no content there. Added: Section 7 in the setting of a finite group acting on a set (any $f$; the count is $[G : \langle\varepsilon\rangle G_e]$, which is $|G|/\mathrm{ord}(\varepsilon)$ on a free orbit), the form for a finite subgroup of an infinite $U$, and the abstract Lean proof with the Z[i] statements as corollaries.

7. The unit-cycle theorem (T3). For $f$ commuting with $U$ and $w = f(1)$: if $w \in U$ the unit cycles are the cosets of $\langle w\rangle$, otherwise there is none. Hypotheses (G), commuting, and (Gf) for the count. General proof: GP Theorem 9.2 (it uses the norm to see that $uw$ is not a unit; a group argument suffices). Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: no exception. Added: Theorem 7.3 for any group of units, finite or not: if $w$ has infinite order no unit is periodic at all, which is what the lifts with $S(1) = \varepsilon$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ show (RT 9.4).

8. Descent and transfer (A2, A4). Descent holds for any group action (the universal property of the orbit space). Transfer, a map respecting associates is Collatz on $R$ iff the induced map is Collatz on $R/U$ in Collatz's sense, needs (Gf) for the direction from $R/U$ to $R$. General proof: QUO Proposition 2.2 and Theorem 3.2, written for a domain with finite $U$. Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: the converse direction fails in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ (lifts with $S(1) = \varepsilon$). Added: Theorem 8.2 for a finite group acting on any set, Theorem 8.3 (the exact statement for infinite groups: transfer holds iff, in addition, $\varepsilon = f^p(e)$ has finite order modulo the stabilizer of $e$), and the abstract Lean proof.

9. Cycle lifts and the stabilizer (A5, A6). Over a cycle of $[z]$ of least period $p$ there are between 1 and $|U|$ cycles, of lengths $p r$; for commuting maps exactly $|U|/\mathrm{ord}(\varepsilon)$, of length $p\,\mathrm{ord}(\varepsilon)$, with stabilizer $\langle\varepsilon\rangle$. Hypotheses (D), (Gf) (and freeness of the orbit, automatic in a domain). General proof: QUO Theorems 4.1 and 4.2. Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: every count matched. Added: Theorems 8.4 and 8.5 for any finite group and any orbit, and the abstract Lean proof.

10. Respecting associates iff commuting, for residue-class maps (A8, A9). Hypotheses: (D), (P), (G), a norm as in (N), and the unboundedness property (B) of Section 9 (for every class and unit there are points with $N(w(z))$ and $N(w(uz))$ large). General proof: none. QUO Theorem 5.1 is for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, with the remark that the proof extends verbatim to $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$ and imaginary quadratic orders; its Lemma 5.0' uses the complex conjugate, and RT 9.3 asserts the extension to $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ with $|N|$ without proof. Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: no non-commuting pair passed in any ring, including the split ring and $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$. Added: Theorem 9.1 with the minimal hypotheses (the proof needs neither finiteness of $U$ nor that $N = 1$ only on units, nor cancellation of $\pi$), Lemma 9.2 proving (B) for every order in every number field, and an abstract Lean proof over any commutative ring (Section 15).

11. Twisted maps with class-dependent multipliers (A10). Hypotheses (D), (P), (Gf). General proof: QUO Proposition 5.3 (uses only that classes are infinite, $U$ finite, and cancellation). Rings-test: not tested. Nothing added beyond recording the hypotheses.

12. Rotated maps (R2, R5, A11). One step $S_{u\alpha}(z) = S_\alpha(uz)$ for every table; for commuting tables $S_{u\alpha} = uS_\alpha$ and the rotation is a Collatz map iff the original is. Hypotheses (D), (P), (G); (Gf) for the Collatz statement. General proof: GP 3, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.2(1). Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Rings-test: one-step identity without mismatch everywhere; rotations of non-commuting tables differ in verdict in most groups in every ring. Added: the abstract form (two maps agreeing up to the group induce the same map on the orbit space, Theorem 8.6) in Lean, with the Z[i] rotation theorem as a corollary.

13. Mirrored maps and automorphisms (R3, R6, R7, R8, A12, R10). One step $S_{\sigma\alpha}(z) = \eta^v\sigma(S_\alpha(\sigma z))$ with $\eta = \sigma(\pi)/\pi$; for commuting tables the iterates carry $\eta^{V_n}$ and the mirror is Collatz iff the original is; for $\eta = 1$ the mirror is conjugate to the map for every table. Hypotheses (D), (P), (S) with $\sigma(P) = P$, (G); (Gf) for the Collatz statements of the commuting case. General proof: GP 3, but only for $\sigma$ of order at most 2, and with the invariance of the norm argued for imaginary quadratic rings only. Not covered anywhere in general: automorphisms that move $P$ (the split ring; RT 6.5 proves the conjugacy for $\mathbb{Z}[\theta]$ only). Rings-test: $\eta = 1$ ($\mathbb{Z}[\phi]$, $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, $\mathbb{Z}$) always agrees, $\eta = -1$ ($\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$) loses the relation for non-commuting tables, so fixing the ideal is not enough, as GP 3.2 predicts; in the split ring the mirror is a map of the other family. Added: Section 10, for automorphisms of any order, with the transport identity $\sigma S^{\pi}_{\alpha,d}\sigma^{-1} = S^{\sigma\pi}_{\sigma\alpha,\sigma d\sigma^{-1}}$ (any $\sigma$, any table, which is the split-ring conjugacy), the change-of-generator identity $S^{\eta\pi} = \eta^{-v}S^{\pi}$, the norm invariance for every order, and the family size $|U|\cdot|\mathrm{Aut}_P(R)|$.

14. Forced fixed points (K1, K2, and the Collatz-type version RT 10.4). If $(\varepsilon\pi^v - \alpha)z \in D$ with $v \ge k$, then $S(z) = \varepsilon z$; for a non-unit $z$, with $\varepsilon = 1$ or a commuting table, the map is not Collatz; if $\varepsilon\pi^v - \alpha$ is a unit every digit of a Collatz map is a unit. Hypotheses (D), (P), (G), any $U$. General proof: ST Proposition 3 and Corollary 3a (paper, for "the unit group"), RT 10.4 for the fundamental-domain version; TH Lemma 5.1 and Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$; IC Lemma 3 for $\mathbb{Z}$. Rings-test: no forced commuting or forced literal map passed in any ring; a multiplier forced only with $\varepsilon \ne 1$ can pass with a non-commuting table, as the lemma allows. Added: Section 11 restates both with $U$ arbitrary (for $\varepsilon$ of infinite order the orbit of $z$ is not even periodic, and the conclusion stands).

15. Smallest digits and Voronoi ties (D1, D2, D3, D8). If every odd class has a unique least element, the least-digit table commutes; least elements lie in the closed Voronoi cell and ties lie on its boundary. Hypotheses: a size function invariant under $U$ (for D1), a lattice with a Euclidean size (for D2): (A1) with $N$, or (O) with the trace form for D2 alone. General proof: ST Lemma 1 and Proposition 1, written for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ (and D6 for $\mathbb{Z}$). The tie counts are ring-specific (D2, D3 for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$; D8 for $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$; RT for the others, computed). Rings-test: ties occur as predicted; none in the split ring; in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ no size is invariant under $U$ (digits were ordered by $a^2 + 2b^2$), so D1 does not apply. Added: Section 12 with the hypotheses.

16. Arithmetic fundamental domains reproduce the Barina-type counts (RT 2.3, 10.6). For $k \ge \kappa + 1$ the Collatz-type maps on $P = \{z : z \bmod \pi^{\kappa+1} \in H\}$ are exactly the commuting residue-class maps. Hypotheses (D), (P), (Gf) and $k \ge \kappa + 1$. Proof: FD Proposition 4.4 for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$; RT 2.3 sketches the general case ("generalizes verbatim"). Rings-test: exact agreement in all seven comparisons; below $\kappa + 1$ the counts differ; $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$ has no arithmetic domain. Added: Theorem 13.1 with a full proof, which turns the seven agreements into instances of a theorem, and the reason for the difference below $\kappa + 1$.

17. Cycle conditions (M2, RT 11). A cycle with $n$ odd steps, exponent $V$ and $S^n(z_0) = \varepsilon z_0$ satisfies $z_0(\varepsilon\pi^V - \alpha^n) = \sum_j \alpha^{n-1-j}\pi^{s_j}d_j$, so large cycles need $\alpha^n$ close to $\varepsilon\pi^V$; this gives one size condition per archimedean place plus an angle (complex place) or sign (real place) condition. Hypotheses: (D), (P) for the identity, (O) for the conditions. Proof: FAM 3 for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$; RT 11 tabulates the conditions ring by ring without a general statement. Rings-test: the extra condition in the split ring (irrational $\arg\theta$) is proved there for $\mathbb{Z}[\theta]$. Added: Section 14, the product identity $\prod_j(1 + d_j/(\alpha z_j)) = \varepsilon\pi^V/\alpha^n$, the per-place bound, the count of conditions with and without units, and a criterion valid in every imaginary quadratic ring: for a rational multiplier the angle condition is a congruence on $V$ exactly when $P$ is fixed by complex conjugation, and a second real condition when $2$ splits.

18. Drop-below-start soundness (V1, V2). Hypotheses: a size $N : R \to \mathbb{N}$ with $N(z/\pi) < N(z)$ for even $z \ne 0$; for V1 also a $U$-invariant size, commuting, and a fundamental domain. The proof (strong induction on $N$) is ring-free; Lean for $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ only; RT 9.1 uses it on $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]/U$ with $|N|$. Added: Proposition 8.7 (statement and the one-line proof, for the record).

Summary of the audit. Items 1, 2, 4 (exact part), 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12 and 14 had general proofs on paper, some stated in narrower settings than their proofs need. Items 3 (eventual existence), 5, 10, 13 (beyond order 2 and beyond the fixed prime), 16 and 17 lacked a general proof; they are supplied in Sections 5, 6, 9, 10, 13 and 14. Item 4 (heuristic part) and item 5 have genuine exceptions in a ring with two archimedean places, $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$, where the norm form fails and the per-place form is the correct one; items 6, 7 and 8 have exceptions there because $U$ is infinite, and Sections 7 and 8 give the exact weaker statements. The split ring $\mathbb{Z}[\theta]$ is an exception to no theorem, but it shows that "odd" is relative to the chosen prime (norms of odd elements can be even) and that an automorphism can move $P$, which Section 10 covers.
