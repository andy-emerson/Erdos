# Spec 13: the complement layout in the harness (build `cpl`)

Nov 2, 2026. Phase A: the complement layout ([complement-research.md](complement-research.md)) built into the spec 13 Nim harness, checked, and its compiled loops counted. **No timings were run.** Nothing is committed.

## What was built

The layout is one 64-bit word:
- bit 63 is the sign start s;
- bit 62 is the axis a (G = 2^62);
- bits 0–61 are the count c.

The value is (c − 2^62·s)·i^a. Each axis holds −2^62 … 2^62 − 1.

[`src/designcpl.nim`](src/designcpl.nim) uses the research's formulas exactly, as the author chose them:

| Operation | Formula | Research count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| add | "Branch-free, no selects" full add. Bridge b = z1 + (w2 OR G), Z1 = 0 − (z1 = 0), src = w1 XOR (t AND Z1), r = b XOR ((b XOR src) AND G). Expression flag e = ((t AND NOT Z1) shr 62) AND (z2 ≠ 0), ORed into a sticky `err`, as in `abf`/`alg` | 11 + 5 = **16** (+1 for `err \|= e`) |
| sub | negate w2 (4 steps), then the full add | 4 + 16 = **20** (+1 for `err`) |
| neg | z = w AND G; d = z − w; r = (d AND NOT G) OR z | **4** |
| mul | sign-extend both (4 + 4); m = ((w1 AND w2) shl 1) sar 63; p = ((x1 XOR m) − m)·x2; r = p XOR ((p XOR w1 XOR w2) AND G) | **18** |
| div | the same, with m = ((w2 AND NOT w1) shl 1) sar 63 applied to q = x1 ÷ₛ x2 | **18** |

`opaque` (from `pseudo.nim`) is used once, on `Word(z1 == 0)` in the add. It stops gcc from turning the zero-test mask into a `cmov` or a branch. No other barrier was needed.

**Wiring:**
- **`src/bench.nim`:** build `cpl` (`-d:bld=cpl`) keeps one list of words per operand and one for the results, like Control's value builds. Integers and complex numbers run the same loop functions (`loop_int_*`), as in A.
  - The loader encodes (d, m) as a word. It exits 2 if an operand is outside −2^62 … 2^62 − 1.
  - `value` decodes the word, so a zero on either axis checksums as 0.
  - `dump` prints the word in hex.
- **Bytes per value:** 8 (8 for complex).
- **`src/build.sh`:** builds `cpl` with the other builds and writes `build/asm/cpl.s`.
- **`test/asmcheck.py`:** `cpl` is a no-branch row (`NOBRANCH = abf, amul, alg, cpl`).
- **`test/check.py`:**
  - decodes the word and compares values, so either zero is accepted;
  - adds a `cpl` case set;
  - adds `cpl` to the error cases (exit 3);
  - checks the cpl timing rows;
  - checks that an operand of +2^62 is refused (exit 2);
  - checks bytes per value (8, 8).
- **`test/run.py`:** see the next section.

## Range: a separate, capped operand set

`cpl` holds less than the other builds: 63 bits per axis, against Control's 64-bit int64 and A's and B's 64-bit magnitudes. The shared timing rows draw add/sub/neg magnitudes up to 62 bits and products up to 63 bits, so their sums and products overflow `cpl`.

**Choice: a capped copy of the rows, used only by `cpl`.**
- `gen.py`'s `draw`/`rows`/`generate` take `cap` (default 0). `cap = 1` takes one bit off every bound:
  - add, sub and neg draw bit lengths 1..61, so a sum is below 2^62;
  - mul and div draw L1 in 1..61 and L2 in 1..62 − L1, so every product, dividend and quotient is below 2^62.
- The capped rows have the same names, seeds and sign/order/axis mixes, and the same complex zero replacement. There are 54 of them, since collapse belongs to B. They are written to `build/work/cpl/`.
- Every operand and exact result in them was checked to be within −2^62 … 2^62 − 1. The largest is 62 bits.
- **The other builds' operands are unchanged.** The 72 default rows regenerated byte-identical to the existing `build/work/*.bin`, since the `cap = 0` path is the old code.
- `run.py` times `cpl` on the capped copy of each row. It also times Control on the same capped copy, as the cell `control-cpl`, and pairs `cpl` with `control-cpl`, so the comparison is on identical operands. Collapse rows have no `cpl` cell.
  - This was dry-run with the timing replaced by `sum`: all cells' checksums agree, including `cpl` and `control-cpl`. The fake output was deleted.
- `check.py` runs the capped rows in `cpl` and in Control: 108 extra checksums.

Why not a single cap for all builds: that would change the operands the other builds see, which was ruled out. A per-pair skip in `cpl` would make its checksum differ from the row's.

## Checks

- **`sh src/build.sh`:** all 8 builds compile; `asmcheck: PASS`. The `cpl` loops have no conditional branch of the operation's own, no `cmov` and no vector instructions. The add and sub loops have `sete`/`setne`.
- **`python3 test/check.py`:** **PASS**. That is 774,843 results, 192 error cases and 504 checksums.
  - The `cpl` set, for each kind and operation (neg, add, sub, mul, div), has edge cases and 4,000 random cases. The edge cases are every direction pair over {0, 1, 2, 3, 2^32 ± 1, 2^32, 2^61 ± 1, 2^61, 2^62 − 2, 2^62 − 1, 2^62 (as −2^62 only)}.
  - The set is run in `cpl` and in Control, whose answers are the same values.
  - Zeros come in both forms (0 and G), from directions 0/2 and 1/3.
  - The 32 error cases (a complex add or subtract of two nonzero numbers across axes, alone and after five valid pairs) exit 3 in `cpl`.
- **The check catches broken copies of `cpl`.** Three were built and then reverted:
  - `src = w1` (no zero fix): 453 failures;

## Instruction counts (gcc 13.3.0, `build/asm/cpl.s`, x86-64 baseline: no BMI, so no `andn`)

Each element loop carries the loop's own 6 instructions: two loads (one for neg), one store, `add` index, `cmp`, `jne`. The operation is the rest.

| Operation | Loop | Operation | Of which `mov` copies | Operation without copies | Research | Difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| neg | 11 | 6 | 2 | **4** | 4 (x86: "+1 `mov`") | 0, and one more copy than the research's x86 note |
| add | 30 | 24 | 3 | **21** | 16 (+1 `err`) | +4 |
| sub | 36 | 30 | 5 | **25** | 20 (+1 `err`) | +4 |
| mul | 29 | 23 | 5 | **18** | 18 | 0 |
| div | 31 | 25 | 5 | **20** | 18 | +2 |

For comparison, the add loop is 30 instructions in `cpl`, 38 in `alg` and 49 in `abf`. For mul the loops are 29, 10 and 10; for div 31, 11 and 11; for neg 11, 8 and 8.

**The differences:**
- **`mov` copies** (all operations): x86-64 has two-operand forms, so each value used twice after a destructive operation needs a copy.
  - In neg, both w (used by `and` and `sub`) and z (used by `sub` and `or`) are reused, so there are 2 copies, not the research's 1. Two are needed without three-operand forms.
  - The research's counts are operators, not register moves.
- **add, +4 and +1:**
  - `err |= e` is 1 (`or`), the sticky flag, outside the research's 16.
  - The zero test `z1 = 0` is `test; sete; movzbl`, 3 instructions where the research counts `seqz` as 1: +2.
  - `z2 ≠ 0` is `test w2, NOT G; setne; movzbl`. The `test` absorbs z2's AND, so 3 instructions where the research counts 2: +1.
  - With no `andn`, gcc forms NOT Z1 as `lea −1(x)` from the 0/1 value and then `and`, 2 where the research counts `andn` as 1: +1.
  - The rest map one to one:
    - t: `xor`;
    - z1: `and`;
    - the bridge: `or`, `add`;
    - Z1: `neg`;
    - src and fix: `and`, `xor`, `xor`, `and`, `xor`, 5 = 2 + 3;
    - e: `shr`, `and`.
- **sub:** the negate's 4 (`and`, `sub`, `and`, `or`), then the add's 21. Same +4 as add, with 2 more copies.
- **mul:** exactly the research's 18:
  - each sign extension is 4 (`shr`, `xor`, `and`, `xor`);
  - m is `and`, `add` (as shl 1), `sar`;
  - the conditional negation is `xor`, `sub`;
  - then `imul`;
  - the fix is 4.
- **div, +2:**
  - **`not`:** gcc has no `andn`, so m's w2 AND NOT w1 costs `not` + `and`. Note that the research's "(3, or 2 with `andn`)" for this m does not add up by its own operators: `not`, `and`, `shl`, `sar` is 4, or 3 with `andn`. Its total of 18 holds only with `andn`.
  - **`cqto`:** x86 `idiv` needs rdx:rax sign-extended. It is the same cost Control's signed divide pays.
  - **No trap or overflow check:** none was emitted, since `-d:danger` is used and the operands are ≥ −2^62.

## cpl2: the round 2 (M1) multiply and divide

The author asked for the research's cheaper generic (RV64-style) forms ("Round 2: optimization", M1, in `complement-research.md`; checker `test/complement/m1.py`, read but not edited). They are built as a second variant: `cMul2` and `cDiv2` in `src/designcpl.nim`, in a new build `cpl2`. `cpl2` is `cpl` with only multiply and divide replaced. `cpl` is unchanged.

- **Multiply, 17 (the "generic, scaled" form):**
  - L = w shl 1 and D = w + (L sar 1) = 2v, 3 each;
  - x2 = D2 sar 1;
  - m = (L1 AND L2) sar 63, all ones when both are imaginary;
  - P = ((D1 XOR m) − m)·x2 = ±2p, and p = P sar 1;
  - then the fix.

  On overflow it gives the 63-bit wrap on axis a1 XOR a2, as add does. Round 1's multiply does not.
- **Divide, 15 (16 without `andn`):**
  - q = D1 ÷ D2 = trunc(v1/v2). D2 is even, so the machine's divide never meets −2^63 / −1;
  - m = (L2 AND NOT L1) sar 63;
  - (q XOR m) − m, then the fix.

**Wiring:**
- `bench.nim` adds `-d:bld=cpl2`.
- `build.sh` builds it.
- `asmcheck.py` adds it as a no-branch row.
- `check.py` runs `cpl2`:
  - in the `cpl` set;
  - in the error cases (exit 3);
  - on the capped cpl timing rows;
  - on the +2^62 refusal (exit 2);
  - for bytes per value (8, 8).
- `run.py` times `cpl2` on the capped rows and pairs it with `control-cpl`, like `cpl`.

**Checks:**
- `build.sh` ends `asmcheck: PASS`. `cpl2`'s multiply and divide loops have no branch, no `cmov`, no `setcc` and no vector instruction. No `opaque` was needed.
- `check.py` passes: 820,748 results, 224 error cases and 558 checksums.
- Two broken copies of `cpl2` were caught, then reverted:
  - multiply without the final `sar 1`: 1,728 failures;
  - divide with the mask's operands swapped: 581 failures.
- The check's division cases are exact only, as before, and its products stay in range. So the multiply's wrap and the truncating divide are not tested here. They are in the research's `m1.py`, and in Lean in milestone B.

**Compiled counts** (gcc 13.3.0, baseline x86-64 without BMI; each loop includes the loop's own 6 instructions):

| | Loop | Operation | `mov` copies | Operation without copies | Research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| cpl mul (round 1) | 29 | 23 | 5 | 18 | 18 |
| **cpl2 mul (M1)** | **24** | 18 | 1 | **17** | 17 |
| cpl div (round 1) | 31 | 25 | 5 | 20 | 18 (+1 `not`, +1 `cqto`) |
| **cpl2 div (M1)** | **24** | 18 | 1 | **17** | 15 (+1 `not`, +1 `cqto`) |

- **Multiply: exactly the research's 17.**
  - gcc writes each `shl 1` as `lea (w,w)`, so it keeps w and costs no copy.
  - The fix computes `w1 XOR w2` first, then XORs in p: the same 4 instructions in another order.
  - Against `cpl` the loop is 5 shorter: one operation and four copies. Round 1's sign extension, `w XOR ((w XOR (w shr 1)) AND G)`, needs w twice after a destructive operation; the `lea` form does not.
- **Divide: 17 = the research's 15, plus `not` (no `andn` without BMI1; the research's 16), plus `cqto`** (x86 `idiv` needs rdx:rax).
  - Against `cpl` the loop is 7 shorter: three operations and four copies.
- **The add, sub and neg loops of `cpl2` are byte-for-byte the same operations as `cpl`'s:** 30, 36 and 11.
- On the research's basis (operation + 3 for loads and the store), `cpl2` is 20 for multiply and 18 for divide (17 + 3), against the research's 20 and 18 (with `andn`, 15 + 3) or 19 (without).

## Lean, round 1 (`lean/Header/Complement.lean`)

Phase B: the full add, subtract, negate, multiply and exact division, all five formulas as built. They are proved for every input in core Lean, with no `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide` or `bv_decide`.

**What is proved.** The formulas are written exactly as built, on natural numbers mod 2^64:
- `cadd`: the bridge `b = z1 + (w2 OR G)`, `Z1 = 0 − (z1 = 0)`, `src`, the fix, and `e = ((t AND NOT Z1) shr 62) AND (z2 ≠ 0)`;
- `cneg`: the 4-step negate;
- `csub`: `cneg w2`, then `cadd`;
- `cmul`, `cdiv`: the 18-step forms. Sign extension, the mask `m`, `(x XOR m) − m` on the first factor (multiply) or on the quotient (divide), the machine's product or truncating signed divide (`Isa.wdiv`), then the fix.

They are compared with round 1's `A.add`, `A.sub`, `A.neg`, `A.mul` and `A.div` (`lean/Header/DesignA.lean`) through an encoding `cenc ⟨m, d⟩`, the word of the signed value ±m on axis `d mod 2`:
- **`cadd_ref`:** for numbers that fit (±m in −2^62 … 2^62 − 1), when `A.add` gives `r` and `r` fits, the formula gives exactly `cenc r` with flag 0. When `A.add` gives an expression, the flag is 1.
- **`csub_ref`:** the same against `A.sub`.
- **`cneg_ref`:** `cneg (cenc x) = cenc (A.neg x)` whenever `−x` fits.
- **`cmul_ref`:** `cmul (cenc x) (cenc y) = cenc (A.mul x y)` whenever the product fits.
- **`cdiv_ref`:** `cdiv (cenc x) (cenc y) = cenc (A.div x y)` whenever `y.m > 0` and the quotient fits.
- **`cenc_cdec`:** every 64-bit word is `cenc` of a number that fits, so nothing is left out.

**Hypotheses and exceptions, stated explicitly:**
- **No overflow:** the operands and round 1's result fit (`fits`). The magnitudes of round 1's add are on a 2^64 clock, which never wraps here.
- **Either zero (`cenc_zero`):** round 1's `⟨0, d⟩` and `⟨0, d + 2⟩` (0 and −0) are the same word, 0 or G by the axis. This is the only identification, and with it the results are exact word equalities. No "any direction for a zero" clause is needed, unlike `LargerRow` and the packed checkers. The result's zero is on `w2`'s axis when `w1` is a zero, else on `w1`'s, which is what round 1 gives under `cenc`.
- **Negate of −2^62 (`cneg_min`):** it has no negation in range, and the formula returns −2^62, the 63-bit wrap, as int64's `neg` does.
- **Multiply and divide.**
  - Multiply needs the product to fit. On overflow the formula is not the 63-bit wrap (the research says the same), and the theorem says nothing about it.
  - Divide needs a nonzero divisor, since the machine's `idiv` traps on zero, and a quotient that fits; only −2^62 / ±1 toward +2^62 does not.
  - Divisibility is **not** needed. Truncating division commutes with the signs, as round 1's `m1 / m2` does, so `cdiv` is round 1's division for every pair, not only exact ones.
  - The sign-extended words are at least −2^62, so the machine divide never meets −2^63 / −1. This is what `Isa.wdiv_ofInt`'s `Fits` hypotheses check.
- **Subtract has no such exception.** Subtracting −2^62 is right whenever the difference fits: the negation wraps, and the add's wrap undoes it (`cadd_min`). The harness's `cpl` check set already contains such pairs (−1 − (−2^62) = 2^62 − 1).

**Method.** A word is written by its parts, `P h c = 2^62·h + c` (the two top bits and the count).
- AND, OR, XOR, add, subtract and shift act on the parts (`P_and` … `P_shr`, from `Packed.lean`'s `and_parts`, `xor_parts`, `or_parts`).
- So each formula is evaluated once, symbolically, on parts (`cadd_parts`, `cneg_parts`).
- The comparison with round 1 is then a case split on the directions (16 pairs), the zero tests and the order of the magnitudes, closed by `omega`.
- Subtract reuses `cneg_ref` and `cadd_ref`, plus `cadd_min` for the −2^62 case. Proving it directly, with the negate's parts nested inside the add's, ran past 1M heartbeats.
- Multiply and divide go through the signed reading. Sign extension of `cenc x` is `Isa.ofInt (±m)` (`sext_cenc`), so the round 2 lemmas `Isa.wmul_ofInt` and `Isa.wdiv_ofInt` give the product and quotient of the signed values. The masked negation is `condneg_ofInt`, and the sign rule (`i·i = −1`, `v/(iu) = −i v/u`) is 16 direction cases with `simp`. The fix maps the signed result's word back to `cenc` (`fix_cenc`).

**Round 2 (M1): `cpl2`'s multiply and divide** (`cmul2`, `cdiv2`, the generic forms as built in `cMul2`/`cDiv2`):
- **`cmul2_wrap`, the overflow behaviour:** for all numbers that fit, with no hypothesis on the product, the result is the word of the **63-bit wrap** of round 1's product on its axis (`wrap63`, `cencI`). This is the same wrap as add's. Round 1's `cmul` has no such statement, since its overflow is not the wrap.
- **`cmul2_ref`:** when the product fits, the word of `A.mul x y` exactly.
- **`cdiv2_ref`:** when the quotient fits, the word of `A.div x y` exactly. Divisibility is not needed, and the divisor `2v2` is even, so the machine's divide never meets −2^63 / −1. A zero divisor is the machine's trap; the model's `Int.tdiv` gives 0 there, as round 1's `m1 / 0` does, so no hypothesis is needed, but the theorem says nothing about the machine on a zero divisor.
- **`cdiv2_ovf`, the one quotient that does not fit:** +2^62, from −2^62 / ±1 with the sign rule. It gives the zero of its axis, not the wrap, as the research says.
- **Method.** `two_v` proves research identity I1 (`w + ((w shl 1) sar 1)` is the word of `2v`), and `half_wrap` proves that the final `sar 1` of the word of `2t` is the word of `wrap63 t`. Isa's `wmul_ofInt`/`wdiv_ofInt` and core's `Int.mul_tdiv_mul_of_pos` (`2v1 ÷ 2v2 = v1 ÷ v2`) do the rest. `sar63_and` reads the masks from the top bits of `L1`, `L2`.

**Build.** `lake build` passes with no errors and no warnings. `Header.Complement` takes about 22 s, with a peak of about 1.2 GB. It is imported in `lean/Header.lean` and listed in `lean/README.md`. `#print axioms`: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound` (or fewer) for every theorem. No heartbeat or recursion limit is raised.

## Round 3: counts (`lean/Header/ProgCpl.lean`, `Counts.lean`)

Counts and branches only, under spec v0.2 §8's round 3. The programs are **not** proved correct here (that is round 2 for this layout), and nothing was timed.

**Programs.** The five formulas of `src/designcpl.nim`, step for step, in the instruction set of [results-r2.md](results-r2.md) §1 (`PCpl.add`, `sub`, `neg`, `mul`, `div`). One list of words, as Control, B and C: registers 1, 2, 3 hold the addresses of `x`, `y`, `z`.
- **Constants in registers.** `G = 2^62` is in register 4 and `NOT G` in register 5. Neither is an immediate on x86-64 (imm32) or RV64 (imm12); ARM64 encodes both. They are loop invariants, set once, as gcc does (`movabs` before the loop in `cpl.s`), so the caller passes them like the addresses, and the counts do not include them. `−1` is an immediate everywhere (`xori r, r, −1`: x86-64 `not`, ARM64 `mvn`, RV64 `not`).
- **No `andn`.** RV64IM, baseline x86-64 and the instruction set have none, so `t AND NOT Z1` (add) and `w2 AND NOT w1` (divide) are `xori −1` and `and`: one step more than the research counts in each. This is also what gcc emits.
- **The flag.** The add leaves `e` in register 21, computed fresh, as A's branch-free rows do. The build ORs it into a sticky `err`: one `or` more, outside the count.

```text
add  (sub: the 4 negate steps on w2 first, into r11)
 0: ld r10, 0(r1)          ; w1
 1: ld r11, 0(r2)          ; w2
    [sub] and r22, r11, r4 ; z = w2 and G
    [sub] sub r11, r22, r11
    [sub] and r11, r11, r5
    [sub] or  r11, r11, r22 ; -w2
 .. xor  r12, r10, r11     ; t
 .. and  r13, r10, r5      ; z1 = w1 and NG
 .. or   r14, r11, r4
 .. add  r14, r13, r14     ; b = z1 + (w2 or G)
 .. sltiu r15, r13, 1
 .. neg  r15, r15          ; Z1 = 0 - (z1 = 0)
 .. and  r16, r12, r15
 .. xor  r16, r10, r16     ; src
 .. xor  r17, r14, r16
 .. and  r17, r17, r4
 .. xor  r17, r14, r17     ; r = b xor ((b xor src) and G)
 .. and  r18, r11, r5      ; z2
 .. xori r19, r15, -1      ; not Z1
 .. and  r19, r12, r19
 .. srli r19, r19, 62
 .. sltu r20, zero, r18    ; z2 != 0
 .. and  r21, r19, r20     ; e
 .. sd   r17, 0(r3)

neg: ld r10; and r12, r10, r4; sub r13, r12, r10; and r13, r13, r5; or r13, r13, r12; sd r13

mul: ld r10; ld r11
     srli r12, r10, 1; xor r12, r12, r10; and r12, r12, r4; xor r12, r12, r10   ; x1
     srli r13, r11, 1; xor r13, r13, r11; and r13, r13, r4; xor r13, r13, r11   ; x2
     and r14, r10, r11; slli r14, r14, 1; srai r14, r14, 63                     ; m
     xor r15, r12, r14; sub r15, r15, r14; mul r15, r15, r13                    ; p
     xor r16, r15, r10; xor r16, r16, r11; and r16, r16, r4; xor r16, r15, r16  ; fix
     sd r16

div: as mul, with m = xori r14, r10, -1; and r14, r11, r14; slli r14, r14, 1; srai r14, r14, 63
     and q = div r15, r12, r13; xor r15, r15, r14; sub r15, r15, r14; then the fix
```

- **No branch.** `Reg.cpl_no_branch` proves the five programs have no conditional branch and no jump. `all_forward` and `all_loads_first` now cover them too.
- **Steps without the loads and the store (RV64IM):** add 17, sub 21, neg 4, mul 18, div 19. Against the research: add 16 and div 18 count `andn` as one step; neg 4 and mul 18 are equal.
- **ARM64:** each step is one instruction except `sltiu` (`cmp; cset`) and `sltu` (`cmp; cset`) in the add, so add and sub are 2 more. The research's ARM64 hand counts use `bfi`, `bfxil`, `bic` and `csel`, which are not in the instruction set, so they are not counted here.
- **x86-64,** by §1's rules: `sltiu` and `sltu` are 3 each (`test; sete; movzx`, `test; setne; movzx`), the divide is `cqo; idiv`, and a `mov` comes before an operation whose first source is still needed.

**One change to the x86-64 rule.** In `costAt`, a result could take over the register of a source not read again on the path. For `G` that is wrong: the negate's `z = w AND G` would otherwise be counted as overwriting `G`, which the loop still needs. `Counts.lean` now has `callerReg`: registers 1–9 (addresses and constants) are the caller's, and are never taken over. No program writes one. This changes only the cpl negate (x86-64 7 → 8). `table_eq` proves every earlier row unchanged.

- **neg:** equal. Both copy `w` (needed by `sub`) and `z` (needed by `or`).
- **add, model 1 more.**
  - **Fold, gcc −1.** gcc folds `z2 = w2 AND NG` into the zero test (`test w2, NG; setne; movzbl`). The model counts `and` and then the 3-instruction `sltu`.
  - **Sticky flag, gcc +1.** gcc pays 1 for the sticky `or` of `err`.
  - **Order, gcc −1.** gcc forms `NOT Z1` before `src` as `lea −1(seqz)`, from the 0/1 value, so `Z1` is dead at `t AND Z1`. The program keeps the source's order (`src`, then `NOT Z1` by `xori`), so `Z1` is still live there, and the model counts one more copy. `lea −1` and `xori −1` are one instruction each.
  - The other non-copy instructions map one to one: `xor` t, `and` z1, `or`+`add` the bridge, `test; sete; movzbl` + `neg` for Z1, `and; xor` src, `xor; and; xor` the fix, `and; shr; and` for e.
- **sub:** the same three differences, after the negate's 4 non-copy instructions and 2 copies, which match.
- **mul, gcc 2 more copies.** The non-copy instructions match one to one: two sign extensions of 4, `and; add; sar` for `m` (gcc's `add r, r` is the model's `slli 1`, one instruction), `xor; sub; imul`, and the 4-step fix. gcc's extra two `mov`s are its register choices: `x1 − m` moved into `rax`, and the fix starting from a copy of `w2`. In the model the fix takes `w1`'s register, since `w1` is not needed after it.
- **div, gcc 2 more copies.** The non-copy instructions match: the sign extensions, `not; and; add; sar` for `m`, `cqto; idiv`, `xor; sub`, the fix. The model's divide is 2 instructions, with `rax` assumed free (as in results-r2.md §7, item 9). gcc moves the quotient out of `rax` and starts the fix from a copy of `w2`.
- **No conditional branch, `cmov`, `adc` or `sbb`** appears in any of the five loops (asmcheck PASS, above). This agrees with `cpl_no_branch`.

## Open points

- The counts are for baseline x86-64. With `-mbmi`, `andn` would remove 1 from add, sub and div, but the harness's flags are spec 12's and were not changed.
- The research's ARM64 and RV64 counts were not assembled here. Round 3 above counts the programs on all three families by results-r2.md §1's rules. Nothing was assembled for ARM64 or RV64.
- Overflow (out-of-range results) is not tested in the harness. The rows and the check stay in range. The research's own check covers the wrap.
