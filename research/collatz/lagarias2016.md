# Lagarias 2016: Erdős, Klarner, and the 3x+1 problem

Notes written 4 November 2026 for the draft `papers/gaussian-collatz.md`. Source: the PDF of 24 JSTOR screenshots supplied by the user, all read. Page numbers below are the journal page numbers printed on each page.

The citation, confirmed from the first page and the running footers: Jeffrey C. Lagarias, "Erdős, Klarner, and the 3x + 1 Problem", *American Mathematical Monthly* 123, no. 8 (October 2016), 753-776, doi:10.4169/amer.math.monthly.123.08.753. MSC: Primary 11B05, Secondary 11B75, 20M20. The issue number 8 is taken from the DOI ("123.08") and the footer "October 2016"; the pages run from 753 (title) to 776 (end of references and author note).

## 1. What the paper says

### 1.1 Scope

The abstract (p. 753): "This paper describes work of Erdős, Klarner, and Rado on semigroups of integer affine maps and on sets of integers they generate. It gives the history of problems they studied, some solutions, and new unsolved problems that arose from them." Only the last section (Section 10, pp. 772-775) is about 3x+1 itself. The rest is about orbits of semigroups of integer affine maps x ↦ mx + b, which arose from self-orthogonal Latin squares (Section 2, pp. 753-755).

### 1.2 Erdős's statements and prizes

The paper does not contain any "mathematics is not ready" remark. I read every page and it is not there. The only remark by Erdős about 3x+1 that the paper reports is on p. 775: "However he did not publish any result on the 3x + 1 problem, which he characterized in conversation as “hopeless.” The upper bound on orbit size given in Theorem 3 seems to be the closest he ever came to working on problems like the 3x + 1 problem." The "not ready" saying is usually traced to Lagarias's 1985 Monthly survey (the paper's reference [39]). I have not checked that against the 1985 paper [unverified]. The draft does not use the quotation, so nothing in it needs correcting.

The paper reports no prize from Erdős for 3x+1. His prize was for a different problem, the positive density problem (p. 766): "Erdős Positive Density Problem. Let R = {f2(x) = 2x + 1, f3(x) = 3x + 1, f6(x) = 6x + 1} and set A = {1}. Does the set S = ⟨R : A⟩ have a positive density? More precisely, does S have a positive lower asymptotic density d(S) > 0?" Then: "Erdős offered a prize of £10 for its solution in 1972. The problem was answered in the negative soon afterwards by Joan R. Crampin and Anthony J. W. Hilton (see [31, p.140], also [25]). They collected £5 each from Erdős." Footnote 3 on p. 766 qualifies the attribution: "A. J. W. Hilton's [25] recollection is that this problem may have been formulated by Klarner, and that Erdős liked it and offered a prize for its solution." Figure 2 on p. 766 reproduces Erdős's £5 cheque to Hilton.

The paper does report a 3x+1 prize from someone else, Coxeter (p. 772): "However it was first stated in the literature (to my knowledge) in 1971 by H. S. M. Coxeter [14], in the published version of a lecture he gave in Australia in 1970. Coxeter offered fifty dollars for a proof, and one hundred dollars for a counterexample." Footnote 4: "Presumably Canadian dollars, but maybe Australian dollars!"

Erdős's mathematical contribution is the density bound. Klarner and Rado report it on p. 759, quoting [37, Theorem 8]: "P Erdős has kindly communicated to us the essentials of a result which shows that for certain sets R of unary linear operations and certain sets A the set ⟨R : A⟩ has density zero and is therefore neither a per-set or a near per-set. This applies for example, to ⟨2x + 1, 3x + 1 : 1⟩." Lagarias states it as Theorem 3 (Erdős) on p. 759. Take affine maps m_i x + b_i with real coefficients, m_i ≥ 1 and b_i ≥ 0, and suppose some σ > 0 has α := Σ 1/m_i^σ < 1. Then for every T ≥ 1, |⟨R : A⟩^# ∩ [0, T]| ≤ (1/(1 - α)) (Σ_{a ∈ A, 0 ≤ a ≤ T} 1/a^σ) T^σ. Corollary 1 (p. 761) applies this to {2x+1, 3x+1}. With τ ≈ 0.78788 the root of 1/2^τ + 1/3^τ = 1, the orbit of 1 has O(T^{τ+ε}) elements up to T, so density zero.

### 1.3 Klarner and Rado

Klarner visited Reading in 1970-1971, where Rado was professor (p. 755). In Lagarias's words: "They formulated the problem as the study of orbits of semigroups S of affine functions f : N → N of the form f(x) = βx + α, with α, β nonnegative integers." The orbit ⟨R : A⟩ is "the smallest set of integers that contains A and is closed under the operations of substituting any of its values into any function f_i" (p. 755). Their results appeared as a Stanford report in 1972 [35] and in Pacific J. Math. in 1974 [37]. For several variables they proved, under some conditions, that "if an orbit contains an infinite arithmetic progression then it is a finite union of arithmetic progressions ([37, Theorem 4])" (p. 755).

The Klarner-Rado sequence is S_KR = ⟨2x + 1, 3x + 1 : 1⟩ = 1, 3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 19, ... (p. 755). Footnote 1 on p. 756 quotes Klarner and Rado: "For example, we have studied the set S = ⟨2x + 1, 3x + 1 : 1⟩ which seems to be fairly complicated." Klarner's three questions (p. 757) ask for its size, whether it contains an infinite arithmetic progression, and whether its complement can be covered by infinite arithmetic progressions. All three are answered (p. 757): "(1) S has natural density zero. (2) S does not contain an infinite arithmetic progression. (3) The complement of S can be partitioned into a countable union of infinite arithmetic progressions." The answers are due, respectively, to Erdős, to a corollary of Erdős's bound, and to Coppersmith (1975) [13].

The later results on the Klarner-Rado sequence and related semigroups are these:

- Fredman's thesis improves Erdős's bound to C_1 T^τ, and Fredman and Knuth give an asymptotic formula c T^τ + o(T^τ) (p. 762).
- Theorem 4 (p. 762): the semigroup ⟨2x+1, 3x+1⟩ is free, and every integer occurs at most once on each level of the tree. Corollary 2 (p. 765) then gives multiplicity m_KR(n) ≤ log_2 n + 1.
- Crampin and Hilton's solution of the prize problem (Theorem 6, p. 767) rests on a relation: "The key observation underlying their solution is that the semigroup of affine maps S(R) is not a free semigroup; it has the nontrivial relation f2 ∘ f2 ∘ f3(x) = f6 ∘ f2(x) = 12x + 7" (p. 766). They prove the bound O(T^{τ_1+ε}) with τ_1 ≈ 0.900526.
- Coppersmith's criterion (Theorem 7, p. 768) is stated in terms of "feedback elements", integer fixed points of semigroup elements. Theorem 8 (p. 769) gives the complement of every orbit ⟨2x + 1, 3x + 1 : a⟩ as a finite disjoint union of infinite arithmetic progressions.
- Theorem 9 (p. 770) is a "bad" example: for a ≥ 2 the complement of ⟨2x, 2x + 1 : a⟩ cannot be covered by a finite set of infinite arithmetic progressions.
- Klarner's freeness criterion of 1982 (Theorem 10, p. 771) has the necessary condition 1/m_1 + ... + 1/m_k ≤ 1 (p. 770), with equality as "the critical case". Theorem 11 (p. 771) lists six free semigroups with multipliers (2, 3, 6).

### 1.4 Problems posed

There are three open problems in the paper.

- Question, p. 766: "Does the Klarner–Rado sequence have bounded multiplicities? That is, does m_KR(n) = O(1) hold? If not, can the upper bound in Corollary 2 be improved?" A reviewer computed m_KR(7238266879) = 5 (p. 766).
- Klarner's Question, p. 772: "Do any of the orbits of 0 of the six sets of affine transformations above, given by ⟨α1, α2, α3 : 0⟩, have positive density?" Lagarias adds: "Klarner's question has not been answered for any of these six sequences." Guy put one of the six, ⟨2x, 3x + 2, 6x + 3⟩, in his 1983 Monthly paper and in *Unsolved Problems in Number Theory* as Problem E36 (p. 772).
- Freeness of semigroups of integer affine maps, encoded as 2 × 2 matrices: "It remains an open problem and they describe it as “very challenging.”" (p. 771, citing Cassaigne, Harju and Karhumäki).

Section 10 also suggests looking at the rational part of the inverse orbit, posed as a question rather than a problem (p. 775): "To really understand this problem, should we not understand these rational numbers as well?"

### 1.5 The 3x+1 problem as an affine semigroup orbit (Section 10)

Lagarias inverts T(x) = (3x+1)/2 or x/2 to the semigroup S_3 = ⟨2x, (2x - 1)/3⟩ (p. 773). The warm-up is the x+1 problem, "its simpler cousin, the x + 1 problem, a solved problem" (p. 753), whose inverse semigroup ⟨2x, 2x - 1⟩ has orbit of 1 equal to N \ {0} (p. 773). On S_3 he notes (p. 774): "once an iterate becomes noninteger, it stays noninteger forever after, under application of the semigroup maps", because the power of 3 in the denominator never decreases. Hence (p. 774): "It follows that the set of integer values of an orbit that starts at an integer a forms a connected subtree of the backwards iteration tree." He then states: "3x + 1 Conjecture (Affine Semigroup Form). The semigroup orbit ⟨2x, (2x − 1)/3 : 4⟩ contains every positive integer except 1 and 2." Two things separate S_3 from the Klarner-Erdős setting (p. 774): it has negative coefficients, so positive fixed points are possible, and it has non-integer rational coefficients. Also, "each semigroup orbit ⟨2x, (2x − 1)/3 : a⟩ is not discrete for any real value of a" (p. 775), so Erdős's Theorem 3 does not apply.

Historical points in this section:

- Collatz says he invented the problem before 1952 and circulated it to Hasse in 1952 [10] (p. 772).
- Coxeter was the first to publish it, in 1971 [14] (p. 772).
- Conway "in 1972 showed that a generalization of the problem is undecidable" [11]. "His work was done about the same time as the work of Klarner, Rado, and Erdős, so one may say that iteration problems of this sort were “in the air” around this time" (pp. 772-773).
- The paper also points to "Conway's more recent views on its unsettleability" [12, Appendix A] (p. 773).

## 2. Generalizations that relate to the Gaussian family

The paper says nothing about complex numbers, Gaussian integers, rings of algebraic integers, number fields or p-adic extensions. It has nothing on lattice maps with matrix multipliers either. Section 10 treats only the classical 3x+1 and x+1 maps on Z. What it does have is the following.

The first is multivariable orbits (p. 755): "More generally, they considered orbits of affine maps f_i : N^n → N^n of the form f_i(x1, x2, ..., xn) = β_{i,1}x1 + ··· + β_{i,n}xn + α_i, in which all β_{i,j} and α_i are nonnegative integers." The printed "N^n → N^n" appears to be a slip for N^n → N, since the formula has one output. These maps are operations of several arguments that generate sets of integers, for example ⟨2x + 3y : 1⟩, which "consists of all integers congruent to 1 or 5 modulo 12" (p. 755). They are not maps on Z^n, so they are not a precedent for the plane dynamics in the draft. Hoffman's 1976 thesis and the Hoffman-Klarner papers [26, 27, 28] continue this multivariable line (p. 770).

The second is real coefficients (p. 759): "One can further extend the definitions above to real-valued functions and sets. ... For integers m_i, b_i the discreteness property is built in. For the general case of real numbers, some restrictions on the values {(m_i, b_i) : i ∈ I} are needed to get discrete orbits; the conditions that all b_i ≥ 0, all m_i > 1 and that for each T > 0 there are finitely many m_i ≤ T are sufficient for discreteness. For affine functions with rational coefficients appearing in connection with the 3x + 1 problem treated in Section 10 orbits are not discrete." Theorem 3 is stated for real coefficients.

The third is matrix encodings and undecidability (p. 771): "Composition of such maps can be encoded as multiplication of integer matrices. In 1991 Klarner, along with Birget and Satterfield [33], showed the undecidability of such questions in general by a reduction of the Post correspondence problem; the reduction used 3 × 3 matrices. The affine maps f(x) = ax + b above can be encoded as 2 × 2 matrices [a b; 0 1] with nonnegative entries, and are not covered by this result." This bears on the draft. An affine map z ↦ az + b of Z[i] is a 2 × 2 upper triangular matrix over Z[i]. As an affine map of Z^2 it is a 3 × 3 integer matrix, the size at which Klarner, Birget and Satterfield prove freeness undecidable for general integer matrices. So the inverse branches of the draft's rules sit between the open 2 × 2 case over Z and the undecidable 3 × 3 case. Neither result settles freeness for them. This is my own observation, not the paper's.

The fourth is multiplicative structure. Klarner's 1981 decision procedure (p. 762) applies to "semigroups having the property all the multipliers a_i of the affine functions generating the semigroup are powers of a fixed element m > 1". The orbits ⟨2x, 2x + 1 : a⟩ are 2-automatic (p. 770, citing Klarner [32] and Allouche-Shallit [1]). Neither result is stated outside Z.

The fifth is the affine semigroup form of Section 10, which carries over to the draft's rules. The paper does not say so; the check below is mine. Use the form of a rule with all k divisions in one step, z ↦ (αz + d(z))/(1+i)^k on odd z and z ↦ z/(1+i) on even z. Its inverse branches are w ↦ (1+i)w and w ↦ ((1+i)^k w - d)/α, one for each digit d ∈ D. If (1+i)^k w - d is divisible by α, its quotient z satisfies αz + d = (1+i)^k w, which forces z to be odd and d to be the digit of z's class. So the integral part of the semigroup orbit is exactly the backward tree. For H, with (1+i)^4 = -4, the branches are (1+i)w and (-4w - d)/(5+2i). Lagarias's stays-nonintegral property holds here too, with the prime 5+2i in place of 3. If w has 5+2i to the power -j in its denominator with j ≥ 1, so does -4w - d, because 4 and d are prime to 5+2i, and dividing by 5+2i raises the power to j+1. Multiplying by 1+i leaves it unchanged. Conjecture 1 can therefore be restated in Lagarias's form: the integral part of ⟨(1+i)w, (-4w - d)/(5+2i), d ∈ D : {1, i}⟩ is Z[i] \ {0}. The paper gives the analogue of Proposition 2 in the draft for x+1: the inverse semigroup ⟨2x, 2x - 1⟩ is free and its orbit of 1 is all of N \ {0}. As on Z, the Gaussian orbits are not discrete. The odd inverse branch multiplies the norm by 16/29 < 1, so Erdős's Theorem 3, which needs every multiplier to be at least 1, does not apply.

## 3. References in the paper that the literature search missed and that bear on the draft

The literature search (`research/collatz/literature.md`) mentions none of Lagarias 2016, Conway, Coxeter, Klarner, Coppersmith or the matrix-semigroup papers. Of the paper's 43 references, these bear on the draft. Citations are copied from the paper's list (pp. 775-776).

1. J. C. Lagarias, Erdős, Klarner, and the 3x + 1 problem, *Amer. Math. Monthly* 123 no. 8 (2016) 753-776. This is the source of the affine semigroup form of the conjecture (p. 774), which carries over to H (Section 2 above). It is also a recent survey by the author of the draft's references 3, 4 and 13.
2. J. H. Conway, Unpredictable iterations, in Proc. 1972 Number Theory Conference, Univ. of Colorado, Boulder, CO, 1972, 49-52. [Reprinted in [40].] This proves that a generalization of 3x+1 is undecidable. It bears on the draft's open question in Section 7 and open problem 6. Rules whose multiplier or level depends on the class are residue-class-wise affine maps, and Conway's class of maps contains undecidable instances. So any positive result about such rules has to use the specific rule. The draft's Section 11 cites Matthews and Kohl for that framework but not Conway, who started it.
3. J. H. Conway, On unsettleable arithmetical problems, *Amer. Math. Monthly* 120 no. 3 (2013) 192-198. Conway's later view that 3x+1 may be unsettleable [12, Appendix A]. It fits the draft's remark that a single-cycle rule restricting to 3x+1 would at best give "computational evidence and a reduction".
4. H. S. M. Coxeter, Cyclic Sequences and Frieze Patterns, (The Fourth Felix Behrend Memorial Lecture), *Vinculum* 8 (1971) 4-7. [Reprinted in [40].] The first published statement of the problem, according to Lagarias (p. 772). It matters only if the introduction gives history.
5. L. Collatz, On the motivation and origin of the 3x + 1 problem (Chinese), *J. Qufu Normal Univ., Natural Sci. Ed.* 12 no. 3 (1986) 9-11 (English translation in [40, 241-247]). Collatz's own account. It matters only for history.
6. D. A. Klarner, R. Rado, Arithmetic properties of certain recursively defined sets, *Pacific J. Math.* 53 no. 2 (1974) 445-463. This is the origin of the affine semigroup orbit ⟨R : A⟩, the language in which Lagarias restates 3x+1 and in which H's backward tree can be described.
7. D. A. Klarner, J.-C. Birget, W. Satterfield, On the undecidability of the freeness of integer matrix semigroups, *Int. J. Algebra Comput.* 1 no. 2 (1991) 223-226. Undecidability of freeness for 3 × 3 integer matrices, the size at which affine maps of Z^2 live. It is relevant if the draft discusses the inverse semigroup of H or whether its backward tree has repeated nodes.
8. J. Cassaigne, T. Harju, J. Karhumäki, On the undecidability of freeness of matrix semigroups, *Int. J. Algebra Comput.* 9 no. 3-4 (1999) 295-305. Section 4 treats the open 2 × 2 upper triangular case, which is the shape of a Gaussian affine map over Z[i]. Same use as reference 7.
9. D. Coppersmith, The complement of certain recursively defined sets, *J. Combin. Theory Ser. A* 18 no. 3 (1975) 243-251. Its "feedback elements" (integer fixed points of semigroup elements) and its covering criterion apply to integer affine semigroups. They would be the starting point if anyone asks whether the complement of an orbit of an inverse semigroup of the draft's rules is a union of residue classes modulo powers of 1+i. That is a stretch for the current draft, so I mention it only for completeness.

The paper's reference [39] confirms the draft's reference 13, which the draft marks as from memory: "J. C. Lagarias, The 3x + 1 problem and its generalizations, *Amer. Math. Monthly* 92 (1985) 3-23. [Reprinted with corrections in [40].]" That entry can lose its [unverified] mark. The paper also lists [40] as "J. C. Lagarias (Editor), *The Ultimate Challenge: The 3x + 1 Problem*. American Mathematical Society, Providence, RI, 2010", which matches the book details in the draft's reference 5.

The remaining references do not bear on the draft. They cover Latin squares (Euler, Tarry, Bose-Shrikande-Parker, Sade, Crampin-Hilton, Brayton-Coppersmith-Hoffman, Klyve-Stemkowski), Erdős-Graham and Erdős on Rado, Fredman and Fredman-Knuth, Guy, Hoffman and Hoffman-Klarner, Klarner's other papers, Klarner-Post, Allouche-Shallit, Cassaigne-Nicolas, Chvátal-Klarner-Knuth, and Hilton's private communications. All of them concern integer affine semigroups on N or combinatorics.

## 4. How the introduction or related work could use it

The introduction could mention, in one or two sentences, that 3x+1 has an affine semigroup form (Lagarias 2016, p. 774). The backward tree is the integral part of the orbit of the inverse maps ⟨2x, (2x - 1)/3⟩, and a value that leaves the integers never returns. For H the same holds with the inverse maps (1+i)w and (-4w - d)/(5+2i), with 5+2i in the role of 3, so Conjecture 1 has an exact affine semigroup restatement. Lagarias's x+1 warm-up, a solved cousin whose inverse orbit is all of N \ {0}, is a natural one-line comparison for Proposition 2 and for the easy real-line map of G_3 in Section 6. In related work, Conway's 1972 undecidability theorem should go next to Matthews and Kohl. It started the residue-class-wise framework, and it is the reason the open question of Section 7 can only be answered rule by rule. The matrix-semigroup freeness results (Klarner, Birget and Satterfield; Cassaigne, Harju and Karhumäki) can be mentioned in a sentence if the draft says anything about the inverse semigroup. The paper should not be used for an Erdős quotation about 3x+1: the only one it has is "hopeless" (p. 775), reported second-hand from conversation. Its only Erdős prize, £10, was for the density of ⟨2x+1, 3x+1, 6x+1 : 1⟩, not for 3x+1. Any history paragraph that wants Coxeter's prize should cite Coxeter 1971 as reported on p. 772.
