# Q72: Gaussian-Unit Rational Format Specification v0.1

Oct 26, 2026 · Andrew Emerson

This spec defines a data type rather than a language feature: a family of fixed-width binary formats for exact complex rationals on one axis, specified the way a standards body specifies a floating-point format. Its central member, Q72, is a one-byte Gaussian unit (G8) paired with an ordinary unsigned 64-bit integer (U64).

## Purpose and scope

In scope: the G8, the use of an unmodified U64 as a numerator and a denominator, the pair they form, canonical encodings, arithmetic semantics, the ladder of widths, and a vector form for arrays.

Out of scope: rounding (a conforming implementation never rounds; see Exactness), syntax and language bindings, transcendental functions, the internal representation of arbitrary-precision values, and I/O formats.

## Notation

For a positive integer $n$, $\text{bits}(n)$ is its length in binary, $\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1$, and $\text{bits}(0) = 0$. A word of $w$ bits is a U$w$; its bits are numbered $w-1$ (most significant) down to 0.

## G8: the Gaussian unit

A **G8** is one byte.

| Bit | Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | sign | the value is multiplied by $-1$ |
| 6 | imaginary | the value is multiplied by $i$ |
| 5–0 | slash $s$ | where a paired U64 divides into numerator and denominator |

Read together as a 2-bit number, the sign and imaginary bits are the **turn** $t$, the number of quarter turns: $t = 0$ is $1$, $1$ is $i$, $2$ is $-1$, $3$ is $-i$. Because these four units form a cyclic group, multiplying units is adding turns modulo 4: $i \cdot i$ is $01 + 01 = 10$, which is $-1$. Negation adds 2 (flips bit 7); conjugation negates the turn; the reciprocal of $i^t$ is $i^{-t}$. Setting the sign bit alone gives $-1$, the imaginary bit alone gives $i$, both give $-i$, so the bits also read independently.

A G8 alone is a unit and is never zero; its slash only has meaning in a pair, so the canonical byte of a lone G8 has slash 0.

## U64

The **U64** is the standard unsigned 64-bit integer, unmodified, with its standard arithmetic. On its own it is a natural number. In a pair, the slash of the G8 tells how to read its bits.

## Q72: the pair

A **Q72** is a G8 and a U64, written $(g, u)$. With $t$ and $s$ from $g$:

$$
p = \left\lfloor \frac{u}{2^{63-s}} \right\rfloor, \qquad q = \left(u \bmod 2^{63-s}\right) + 1, \qquad x = i^t \cdot \frac{p}{q}.
$$

The numerator $p$ is the top $s+1$ bits of $u$, read from the most significant bit. The remaining $63-s$ bits store the **denominator minus one**, so a field of zeros means $q = 1$ and no pattern has a zero denominator. At slash 63 there is no field: the whole word is the numerator, and the value is an integer.

Examples: $1/2$ has $p = 1$, $q = 2$, slash 0, and $u = 2^{63} + 1$. The integer $10$ has $p = 1010_2$; slash 3 puts it in the top four bits with a zero field, so $u = 10 \cdot 2^{60}$; slash 63 gives $u = 10$, a non-canonical encoding of the same value. $-3i/4$ has turn 3, $p = 11_2$, slash 1, and $u = 3 \cdot 2^{62} + 3$.

**Every pattern is a value.** All $2^{72}$ bit patterns decode, with no reserved codes, infinities or not-a-number values.

**Zero.** When the numerator bits are all zero, $p = 0$ and the value is zero, whatever the G8 and the denominator field. Zero comes from the U64, never from the G8; a G8 is always a unit. Zero has no direction: $0 \cdot i = 0$, and $-0$ is $0$.

**Range.** A nonzero magnitude $p/q$ in lowest terms is representable exactly when

$$
\text{bits}(p) + \text{bits}(q - 1) \le 64.
$$

The largest magnitude is the integer $2^{64} - 1$; the smallest positive magnitude is $1/2^{63}$. Integers up to $2^{64} - 1$, and every fraction whose numerator and denominator minus one together need at most 64 bits, are representable.

### Canonical form

The canonical encoding of a nonzero value has $p$ and $q$ coprime and $s = \text{bits}(p) - 1$, so the numerator's leading 1 is bit 63 of the U64. The canonical encoding of zero is $g = 0$, $u = 0$: all bits clear. Every value has exactly one canonical encoding; operations return canonical results; equality of canonical encodings is equality of values, so comparing two Q72s for equality is comparing their bytes and their words.

Non-canonical patterns (a numerator with leading zeros, a fraction not in lowest terms, a zero with nonzero bits) are valid inputs and decode to their values.

## Arithmetic semantics

Every operation is exact. Results are the mathematically exact value, in canonical form.

- **Multiplication** adds turns and multiplies magnitudes. Division multiplies by the reciprocal $i^{-t} q/p$.
- **Division by zero** is an error. There is no infinite or undefined result to return.
- **Addition and subtraction** are defined between values on the same axis: two reals, or two imaginaries. A real plus an imaginary is a complex number with both parts, which is two Q72s (see Complex values). A zero is on every axis.
- **Comparison.** Real values are totally ordered. Imaginary values are compared for equality only.
- **Conjugation** negates the turn; **negation** adds 2 to the turn; both leave the U64 unchanged.

### Exactness

A result that does not fit in a Q72 is promoted to the next width of the ladder, and from the top of the ladder to arbitrary precision. A conforming implementation never rounds a result, and has no rounding mode or inexact flag. A program that wants an approximation asks for one explicitly, and the approximation is a new value produced by an explicit function, outside this spec.

### Complex values

A complex value with both parts nonzero is a pair of Q72s, real part first: $(a, b)$ means $a + b$ where $a$ is real and $b$ imaginary. Its operations follow from the parts: $(a, b)(c, d) = (ac + bd, ad + bc)$, where every product of two imaginaries is real and lands in the first part.

## The ladder

The same layout scales with the word. A G with a $w$-bit word holds a sign bit, an imaginary bit, and a slash of $\log_2 w$ bits.

| Format | G | Word | Total | Largest integer | Smallest positive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q24 | G8 (slash 4 bits used; 2 reserved, zero) | U16 | 24 | $2^{16}-1$ | $2^{-15}$ |
| Q40 | G8 (slash 5 bits used; 1 reserved, zero) | U32 | 40 | $2^{32}-1$ | $2^{-31}$ |
| Q72 | G8 | U64 | 72 | $2^{64}-1$ | $2^{-63}$ |
| Q144 | G16 (slash 7 bits used; 7 reserved, zero) | U128 | 144 | $2^{128}-1$ | $2^{-127}$ |

The sign and imaginary bits are always the top two bits of the G. For Q24 and Q40, the slash occupies the low bits of the byte and the reserved bits between are zero in canonical form; a decoder ignores them. Beyond Q144, values are arbitrary-precision exact rationals with the same turn, whose representation this spec does not fix.

Promotion is by value: a result is stored in the narrowest format that holds it, and an implementation may keep a column in one format and promote the whole column.

## The vector form

Arrays of Q72 values can be stored element by element, each with its own G8 and U64. For arithmetic on columns of data, this spec also defines a **vector form**: one shared denominator $d$ for the whole vector, and per element a turn and a U64 magnitude $m_i$, so element $i$ is $i^{t_i} m_i / d$.

A vector of Q72 values converts to the vector form over the least common multiple of their denominators, and back by reducing each element. The two are different layouts of the same values; equality, ordering and results do not depend on which is used.

In the vector form, addition of two vectors over one denominator is integer addition with no gcd, multiplication is integer multiplication over $d^2$, and sums are integer sums: the arithmetic of fixed point, with overflow detected rather than assumed away. Magnitudes that overflow 64 bits widen the whole vector to 128-bit magnitudes, then to arbitrary precision; after arithmetic, a common factor of the denominator and all magnitudes is divided out, and the vector narrows when every magnitude fits.

The vector form is what makes exact matrix arithmetic on measured data fast: a matrix of decimals in this form is an integer matrix over one denominator, so its product is an integer matrix product, which can be computed exactly on floating-point hardware (see Evidence).

## Determinism and platform requirements

Results are bit-identical on every platform: arithmetic is exact and canonical form is unique. Implementations need only unsigned 64-bit integer arithmetic and a 64×64→128-bit multiply.

## Prior art

Q72's magnitude is a **floating-slash rational**, the format Matula and Kornerup defined and analysed ("Finite Precision Rational Arithmetic: Slash Number Systems", *IEEE Trans. Computers*, 1985; *Finite Precision Number Systems and Arithmetic*, Cambridge, 2010). Their formats store a numerator and denominator whose boundary moves, round by best rational approximation, and are arranged in multi-tier precision hierarchies; Q72's ladder is such a hierarchy. Their Integrated Rational Arithmetic Unit (1981) computes all four operations on floating-slash operands with one Euclidean-algorithm unit. Related representations include Kornerup and Matula's LCF continued-fraction encoding (1995) and Hehner and Horspool's quote notation (1979).

Kahan argued that pure-imaginary values deserve their own type, so that arithmetic mixing real and imaginary values does not invent a zero real part; C99's `_Imaginary` type (Annex G) follows him. Q72 puts the axis of every value into the type itself.

For exact computation, the standards are GMP (`mpq`), FLINT (`fmpq`, `fmpq_mat`, with p-adic solving after Dixon, 1982) and numeric–symbolic solvers with rational reconstruction (Saunders and Wan, 2004). Exact or accurate matrix products on floating-point hardware by error-free splitting follow Ozaki, Ogita, Rump and Oishi (2008).

What this spec adds, as far as the literature reviewed shows:
- a Gaussian-unit tag, the sign and imaginary bits read as a quarter-turn count, on a floating-slash rational;
- the split into a one-byte tag and an unmodified U64, sized so that $2 + \log_2 64 = 8$ bits;
- a denominator stored minus one, so no pattern has a zero denominator and every pattern is a value;
- no rounding at all, with promotion along the ladder instead;
- a shared-denominator vector form, paired with an exact float-BLAS matrix product, as part of the format's definition.

## Evidence

Q72 was implemented as a prototype in Python with compiled (Numba) kernels, in both layouts: **elementwise**, a G8 and a U64 per element, and the **vector form**. Only Q72 was built; values that do not fit were promoted to an exact Python stand-in for the rest of the ladder, so timings that involve promotion say nothing about a designed Q144. 23 correctness tests ran first, including an exhaustive check on a 16-bit word that every pattern decodes and every value has one canonical encoding. Every exact result below was checked against an independent exact computation (every element, for elementwise operations), and an independent review of the code, tests and fairness was done before the run. One core of an Intel Xeon, single-threaded BLAS and FLINT; times are medians of interleaved rounds.

### Precision

Rounding is not part of the format; it was measured only to compare how finely Q72 covers the reals. Q72 values are densest near complicated fractions and sparsest beside simple ones, so precision has a typical and a worst case.

| To match Q72's… | a float needs a significand of | so an IEEE-style format of |
| --- | --- | --- |
| typical precision (median relative error $6.4 \times 10^{-20}$, random reals at every magnitude) | 63 bits | 71 bits |
| worst-case precision ($1.2 \times 10^{-10}$, beside the integer 1) | 33 bits | 41 bits |

The format sizes assume the 8-bit exponent needed to cover Q72's range of $2^{-63}$ to $2^{64}-1$. Typical precision is the same at every magnitude, about $6 \times 10^{-20}$, against float64's $4 \times 10^{-17}$. The worst case is at magnitude 1 and improves steadily toward either end of the range ($4.4 \times 10^{-16}$ at $10^{\pm 12}$, $10^{-17}$ to $10^{-19}$ at $10^{\pm 15}$ to $10^{18}$). At the bottom of the range the gap is to zero, as with float underflow.

### Exactness

How often common values are exactly representable (20,000 samples each):

| Values | binary64 | decimal64 | Q72 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| money, 2 places, below $10^9$ | 3.9% | 100% | 100% |
| measurements, 3 places, below $10^6$ | 0.8% | 100% | 100% |
| measurements, 6 places, below $10^6$ | 0.01% | 100% | 100% |
| ratios $a/b$ of integers up to 1000 | 2.5% | 5.1% | 100% |
| means of 7 three-place measurements | 0.12% | 14% | 100% |
| products of two 3-place measurements | 0.05% | 16% | 10% |
| binary64 values | 100% | 0% | 0% |

Q72 holds every short decimal, every ratio of small integers and every mean of such values exactly. Products of two large decimals often need more than 64 bits and promote, and Q72 cannot hold most binary64 values, whose denominators are large powers of two.

### Speed

Against NumPy float64, which is inexact, on $10^7$ values (relative to NumPy, lower is faster):

| Operation | int64 fixed point (exact, unchecked) | Q72 vector form | Q72 elementwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sum | 1.1× | 1.3× | 18× |
| $a \cdot b + c$, 3 places | 1.4× | 3.2× | 490× (9% promoted) |
| $a \cdot b + c$, 2 places, small | 1.7× | 4.2× | 45× |
| group-by sum, 1000 groups | 0.9× | 1.5× | 3.7× |
| variance | overflows | 0.24× | 4.4× |
| variance, values near $10^6$ | overflows | 0.22× | 3.1× |

int64 fixed point is the same decimals held as int64 numerators over one denominator, written by hand; it is only correct when the programmer knows nothing overflows, and it was run only where that was proven. The Q72 vector form does the same arithmetic but checks overflow, widens to 128 bits or beyond when needed, handles imaginary values and reduces its results, at 1.2× to 3× the cost of the unchecked version. Its variance is faster than NumPy's because its integer sums are exact in one pass; NumPy's float64 variance of values near $10^6$ has a relative error of $3 \times 10^{-13}$, about three of its sixteen digits lost.

Against the exact standards, on 200,000 three-place decimals (relative to GMP `mpq` object arrays):

| Operation | FLINT `fmpq` | GMP `mpz` numerators | Q72 vector form | Q72 elementwise |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sum | 0.91× | 0.23× | 0.003× (350× faster) | 0.065× |
| $a \cdot b + c$ | 0.58× | 0.49× | 0.014× (74× faster) | 2.4× |

The elementwise layout pays for a gcd per operation, because every element owns its denominator; where results stay in Q72 its sums are 15× faster than GMP, but on products that promote it falls behind GMP. The vector form's advantage is the shared denominator, which GMP `mpz` numerators also get, at 36× to 80× the vector form's time.

**Matrix product.** For decimal matrices in the vector form, an exact product computed with float64 BLAS (see The vector form; one GEMM when every partial sum stays below $2^{53}$, otherwise 128-bit recombination of limb products):

| Product | NumPy float64 | Q72 exact GEMM | FLINT `fmpz_mat` | FLINT `fmpq_mat` | NumPy int64 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 500×500, 3 places | 3.6 ms | 12.6 ms | 46 ms | 78 ms | 53 ms |
| 1000×1000, 3 places | 24 ms | 62 ms | 352 ms | 476 ms | 857 ms |
| 500×500, 9 places (limbs, 128-bit result) | 3.6 ms | 26 ms | 72 ms | 149 ms | overflows |

The exact product takes 2.6× to 7× NumPy's time and is 2.8× to 6× faster than FLINT.

**Linear solve.** The exact solver chain (numeric–symbolic refinement, then p-adic lifting, then FLINT) with answers stored as Q72, against FLINT's exact solve. With a planted integer solution, $n = 500$: NumPy 2.8 ms, Q72 22.5 ms, FLINT 103 ms, with every entry fitting one Q72. With a random right-hand side the exact answer's denominators are hundreds of digits long: no entry fits a Q72, and the chain matches FLINT's time (624 ms against 757 ms at $n = 500$; 20 ms against 7.6 ms at $n = 100$).

**Pipelines.** A regression on 20,000 rows, a 100,000-row table pipeline with currency conversion and grouping, and a 100,000-point complex DFT stage, all exact, in the vector form: 1.9×, 13× and 1.8× NumPy's time; Python's `fractions` takes 650×, 390× and 660×. In the table pipeline, converted amounts need 128-bit magnitudes, and the elementwise layout promotes nearly every element.

### Summary

- Q72's precision is equivalent to a 71-bit float typically and a 41-bit float in the worst case; it is exact for decimals and ratios of small integers, where binary64 almost never is.
- In the vector form, exact arithmetic costs 1.3× to 4× NumPy float64 (and beats it on variance), 1.2× to 3× unchecked int64 fixed point, and is one to two orders of magnitude faster than GMP and FLINT.
- Elementwise Q72 is fast where results fit and slow where they promote; its value is as an interchange and storage format, with the vector form for computation.
- The exact matrix product on float hardware takes 2.6× to 7× NumPy's time and is 2.8× to 6× faster than FLINT.

## Open decisions

- **Reserved bits in narrow and wide tags.** Q24 and Q40 leave bits of the G8 unused, and Q144's G16 leaves seven. This spec makes them zero in canonical form and ignored on decode. They could instead carry something, such as a format tag, so that a stream of mixed widths is self-describing.
- **Errors in arrays.** Division by zero is an error for a scalar. For an array operation, it is open whether the whole operation fails, or the failing elements are reported separately while the rest complete.
- **Ordering of imaginary values.** Imaginaries are compared for equality only. Ordering them by their coefficient ($2i > i$) would be convenient for sorting, at the cost of an order that does not extend to complex values.
- **Arbitrary precision.** Above Q144 this spec fixes the semantics but not the representation. An interchange encoding (for example, a length-prefixed numerator and denominator after the G byte) would let values beyond the ladder be exchanged.
- **Approximation.** A conforming implementation never rounds. A separate, explicit best-approximation function, returning the nearest Q72 to a given rational, is natural to standardise alongside this spec, and the Evidence section measures what it would give, but it is not part of the format.
