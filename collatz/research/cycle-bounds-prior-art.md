# Prior art for lower bounds on cycle length (6 Nov)

Survey for a planned lower bound on the length of a non-trivial cycle (a cycle containing no unit) of Barina's 7x ± 1 on Z (verified to 10^15) and of the 5+2i map on Z[i] (verified to norm 3·10^9, catalog H8, H9). It follows convergents.md, Section 2, invariants-prior-art.md, row 9 (Section 3.9), symmetric-literature.md, Section 2.1, and literature.md, Sections 2.1 to 2.5, and does not repeat what they established unless a question needs it. Notation is that of catalog M2 and M9: n odd steps, V divisions, unit ε, rel = |1 - α^n/(επ^V)|. No paper or catalog entry was edited and nothing was committed. Section 6 contains a preliminary computation, done in a scratchpad script that is not in the repository.

## 2. Question 1: the 3x+1 bounds and the form of the argument

### 2.1 Works

1. R. P. Steiner, A theorem on the Syracuse problem, Proc. 7th Manitoba Conf. Numer. Math. Comput. (1977), 553 to 559. Only the trivial 1-cycle (one circuit: a run of increases followed by a run of halvings). Method: the circuit has a closed form, which forces 2^(K+L) - 3^K to be small against 3^K; Baker's theorem bounds K, and convergents of log2 3 finish. Not read; known through the surveys.
2. R. E. Crandall, On the "3x+1" problem, Math. Comp. 32 (1978), 1281 to 1292. As quoted by Halbeisen and Hungerbühler (Theorem 1): with p_k/q_k the convergents of log2 3 and k > 4, a cycle with n odd terms and minimum m has n > min(q_k, 2m/(q_k + q_{k+1})). Not read.
3. S. Eliahou, The 3x+1 problem: new lower bounds on nontrivial cycle lengths, Discrete Math. 118 (1993), 45 to 56. A nontrivial cycle has at least 17,087,915 elements (abstract, via ccchallenge), with verification to 2^40; its length has the form 301994a + 17087915b + 85137581c with nonnegative integers a, b, c (Chamberland, Section 5). The criterion, as restated by Halbeisen and Hungerbühler (Theorem 2): with k(m) the least k such that some fraction k/n(k) lies in (log2 3, log2(3 + 1/m)], every cycle C in N has |C| ≥ k(min C). Not read directly.
4. L. Halbeisen and N. Hungerbühler, Optimal bounds for the length of rational Collatz cycles, Acta Arith. 78 (1997), 227 to 239. Read. Theorem 3 sharpens Eliahou's criterion for positive rational cycles of length over 160 to |C| ≥ k(min C/0.9); Theorem 4 gives an optimal criterion L(m) through the explicit extremal rational cycle of each length. With verification to 2.12·10^14 (made unconditional by Oliveira e Silva's 3·2^50), integer cycles avoiding 1 have length at least 102,225,496. Breakpoints are convergent numerators of log2 3 (301,994, 17,087,915, 102,225,496), and the fact that q_16 log_3 2 is within 1/100 of an integer reduces lengths k q_16 (k ≤ 100) to one check. Theorems 3 and 4 are stated for positive cycles only.
5. Tempkin and Arteaga, reported by Chamberland (Section 5): length at least 272,500,658, of the form 187363077a + 272500658b + 357638239c. Original not identified.
6. T. I. Martiny, New lower bound on cycle length for the 3n+1 problem, talk, University of Pittsburgh, 18 March 2015. Read (slides). With min Ω above 1.08·2^60 and Oliveira e Silva's 5·2^60, the ratio of length to odd count lies in a short interval around log2 3, between Farey pairs of convergents, so the length is 630,138,877a + 10,439,860,591b + 103,768,467,013c with b > 0 and ac = 0, hence at least 10,439,860,591 (= p_21). The slides contain two arithmetic slips in listed sums, not in the bound.
7. J. Simons and B. de Weger, Theoretical and computational bounds for m-cycles of the 3n+1-problem, Acta Arith. 117 (2005), 51 to 70, doi:10.4064/aa117-1-3. No m-cycle (m local minima, or circuits) for m ≤ 68, raised to m ≤ 75 in a 2010 update (Hercher, introduction; Simons 2022 says m ≤ 75). Method, from Hercher and from memory [unverified, full text not reached]: each circuit gives a closed form, so an m-cycle gives 0 < (K+L) log 2 - K log 3 < (an explicit quantity decreasing exponentially in K/m); a lower bound for linear forms in two logarithms (Rhin's irrationality measure of log 3/log 2 or Laurent, Mignotte and Nesterenko) caps K; the gap is closed by continued fractions of log2 3 and a computer search over the remaining parity patterns. Hercher quotes their upper bound K < 1.4784 m δ^m with δ = log2 3. Santos (arXiv:2005.00346) quotes from them a length above 8.3485·10^15 with more than 5.2673·10^15 odd numbers; the context of that figure (it may be a bound for m ≤ 68 cases) was not checked.
8. J. L. Simons, On the nonexistence of 2-cycles for the 3x+1 problem, Math. Comp. 74 (2005), 1565 to 1572. Steiner's method for two circuits.
9. T. Brox, Collatz cycles with few descents, Acta Arith. 92 (2000), 181 to 188. Finitely many cycles with fewer than 2 log|C| descents, effective, by a Baker-type bound (bibliography II, entry 18).
10. C. Hercher, There are no Collatz-m-Cycles with m ≤ 91, J. Integer Seq. 26 (2023), Article 23.3.5; arXiv:2201.00406; corrigendum to the proof of Theorem 21 (dated 14 June 2026 by Wikipedia's reference list; it repairs an inequality for T sums and leaves the results). Read. History: Eliahou K > 10^7; Hercher and Puchert K > 7.2·10^10 (Hercher, Über die Länge nicht-trivialer Collatz-Zyklen, Die Wurzel, 2018). Theorem 16: δ < (K+L)/K < δ + (1/(3K log 2)) Σ T(n_i), with T(n_i) = Σ_t 1/C^t(n_i) summed from each local minimum n_i, which replaces 1/min by an average and is the key improvement over Eliahou. Corollaries 17 and 19 bound Σ T(n_i) by (97/54) m/X_0 and (7/5) m/X_0. Lemma 22 is the continued-fraction lemma for the smallest denominator in an interval. Theorem 27 removes m, and Corollary 29 shows that verification to X_0 = 3·2^69 gives K > 1.375·10^11 odd terms.
11. D. Barina, Improved verification limit for the convergence of the Collatz conjecture, J. Supercomput. 81 (2025), article 810, doi:10.1007/s11227-025-07337-0. Read. Verification to 2^71; "the length of a non-trivial cycle rises to 355 504 839 929", citing Hercher 2023. Wikipedia gives 217,976,794,617 with the shortcut map and 355,504,839,929 without. The two numbers differ by 137,528,045,312, which is K; so K ≥ 137,528,045,312 odd terms, K + L ≥ 217,976,794,617 steps of T(x) = (3x+1)/2, 2K + L of the plain map. The repository seanbreeden/collatz-cycle-bounds independently gets 72,057,431,991 odd steps at 2^71.02 with the min-based (Sinisalo) interval and Stern-Brocot search, and says the min-based method reaches 137,528,045,312 only at 2^71.88; Hercher's averaged form is what gives that value at 2^71.
12. R. Ghosh, New lower bounds for the size of a non-trivial loop in the Collatz 3x+1 and generalized px+q problem, arXiv:0907.3086 (2009). Read. From 3^m < 2^(S_m) < (3 + 1/a_min)^m splits cycles by the fractional part of m log2 3 and gives explicit bounds on a_min; extends the formulas to px+q, positive cycles only.
13. Also listed but not read: Sinisalo, On the minimal cycle lengths of the Collatz sequences (preprint, Oulu, 2003); Rozier, Upper bound on the elements of a Collatz cycle (2022); K. Knight, Collatz high cycles do not exist, Discrete Math. 349(3) (2026), 114812, doi:10.1016/j.disc.2025.114812. ccchallenge.org lists formalizations of Eliahou, Halbeisen and Hungerbühler, Lagarias 1990 and Hercher as ready to audit.

### 2.2 The argument to imitate

Two routes, used together in every recent bound.

The interval route (Crandall, Eliahou, Halbeisen and Hungerbühler, Martiny, Sinisalo, Hercher's Theorem 16). Multiply x_{j+1}/x_j over the cycle: 2^(K+L) = Π (3 + 1/x_j) over the odd elements. With all x_j > X_0 (verification), (K+L)/K lies in (log2 3, log2(3 + 1/X_0)), or in Hercher's sharper interval with the average of 1/x_j. The smallest denominator of a fraction in that interval (continued fractions, Stern-Brocot, or Hercher's Lemma 22) is a lower bound for K, and all admissible K are nonnegative combinations of the denominators of a Farey pair (Eliahou's a, b, c form). The sign is known for 3x+1: 2^(K+L) > 3^K for positive cycles, so the interval is one-sided and intermediate fractions matter (convergents.md, Section 5, cycle through -17).

The circuit route (Steiner, Simons, Simons and de Weger, Hercher's m ≤ 91). Structure inside the cycle (circuits, local minima) gives a closed form for each element, which makes |(K+L) log 2 - K log 3| exponentially small in K/m; a Baker-type lower bound then bounds K for each m, and reduction plus search excludes the rest. This route gives no bound on K by itself; it gives nonexistence for small m.

Verdict: known. Current best: K ≥ 1.375·10^11 odd terms (Hercher's method with Barina's 2^71), no m-cycles for m ≤ 91.

## 3. Question 2: generalized integer maps and 7x ± 1

### 3.1 Works

1. R. P. Steiner, On the "Qx + 1 problem," Q odd, Fibonacci Quart. 19 (1981), 285 to 288, and II, 293 to 296. Read (scans). For 5x+1 the only circuits that are cycles are 1 → 8 → 1 and 13 → 208 → 13 (17 lies on a double circuit); for 7x+1 (fixed digit +1) the only circuit is the trivial one. Method: a Diophantine equation (2^(k+l) - Q^k)h = 2^l - 1, Baker's theorem, and continued fractions of log2(5/2) and log2(7/2) to 1200 places. Part II states the general-Q equation and leaves the general case open. One-circuit results only, no length bound.
2. Ghosh, arXiv:0907.3086 (2.1, item 12): α_m(p, q) = q/(p(2^(1/m) - 1)) and β_m(p, q) for px+q, positive cycles, fixed digit. This is the Eliahou-type min-element inequality written for general p, without a computed length bound for any p > 3.
3. J. L. Simons, On the (non-)existence of m-cycles for generalized Syracuse sequences, Acta Arith. 131 (2008), 217 to 254, doi:10.4064/aa131-3-2. Keywords "generalized Collatz problem, cycle existence, linear forms in logarithms", MSC 11J86. Full text not reached, so which maps it covers (qx+r with fixed digit, as the title suggests) is [unverified]. It is the most likely source of the Simons and de Weger route for other multipliers and must be read before claiming novelty for the circuit route.
4. J. L. Simons, Cycles of higher-order Collatz sequences, NNTDM 28 (2022), 48 to 63, doi:10.7546/nntdm.2022.28.1.48-63. Read. Map x_{n+1} = (x_n + 2x_{n-1} + q)/2 on odd terms. For 1-cycles the linear form has three logarithms including the algebraic number (1+√17)/2; Matveev's bound (through Evertse's explicit lemma) gives |Λ| > (e(k+l))^(-1.32·10^18), against 0 < 2^(k+l) - a_{k+1} < 17.232((1+√17)/2)^(0.816k), so k < 3.89·10^20; a Dujella and Pethő reduction (generalized Baker and Davenport, convergent q_49 ≈ 5.8·10^21) brings this to k ≤ 94, and a search finishes. 5. E. Belaga and M. Mignotte, Embedding the 3x+1 conjecture in a 3x+d context, Exp. Math. 7 (1998), 145 to 151, and later reports; E. Belaga, Acta Arith. 106 (2003), 197 to 206. Through bibliography annotations (invariants-prior-art.md, 3.9): bounds on the number of cycles with at most k odd elements (Baker and Wüstholz), length/oddlength between log2 3 and log2(d+3), tables of 2^l - 3^k. Not read.

7. Barina, 7x ± 1, arXiv:1807.00908; CMST 28(4) (2022), 143 to 147. Read again: no cycle equation, no continued fraction of log2 7, no length bound; only the cycles through ±1 and 0. 8. Santos, On the Collatz general problem qn+1, arXiv:2005.00346: lists cycles of 5n+1 and 181n+1, proves Mersenne q have only the trivial cycle in its sense; no length bound.

10. Steiner-type closed forms for symmetric maps: none found. Lu Pei's map and Matthews's least-absolute-residue generalizations (symmetric-literature.md, 3.3) were not found with cycle-length bounds.

### 3.2 Verdict

Partly known for fixed-digit maps: Steiner's circuit results for 5x+1 and 7x+1, Ghosh's min-element inequality for px+q, Simons 2008 for generalized Syracuse m-cycles (unread). Not found: any numerical lower bound on cycle length for 5x+1, 7x+1, mx+d with m > 3, for Barina's 7x ± 1, or for any sign-symmetric map. Not found either: a treatment in which the digits d_j/x_j have both signs, so that the approximation of log2 m is two-sided. For 7x ± 1 that is the generic case: the product identity Π |7 + d_j/x_j| = 2^V holds with factors on both sides of 7, so V/n lies in (log2(7 - 1/B), log2(7 + 1/B)) and both convergents of log2 7 and fractions on either side count. For cycles up to sign (ε = -1) the identity is the same in absolute value, so ε adds nothing on Z; the "sign" of the plan is the sign of V log 2 - n log 7, and it is free.

## 4. Question 3: cycle equations in rings, angle conditions, Baker in number fields

### 4.1 Works

## 5. Question 4: computational tools

1. Smallest denominator in an interval (Z). Continued fractions or Stern-Brocot descent at sufficient precision: Hercher's Lemma 22; the Python package seanbreeden/collatz-cycle-bounds (exact Stern-Brocot, with a test that the convergent-only shortcut by Legendre's theorem is 4.11 times weaker at 2^68); PARI/GP contfrac, contfracpnqn, bestappr; mpmath. 4. LLL reduction for several logarithms and for complex forms: A. K. Lenstra, H. W. Lenstra and L. Lovász, Math. Ann. 261 (1982), 515 to 534; B. M. ## 6. Plan for 7x ± 1 and 5+2i

### 6.1 7x ± 1

1. Interval step (Eliahou, Sinisalo). Every odd |x| ≤ 10^15 reaches ±1 (Barina for x > 0, negation for x < 0), so every odd element of a non-trivial cycle has |x| > B = 10^15. From x_{j+1}2^(v_j) = 7x_j + d_j, Π |7 + d_j/x_j| = 2^V, each factor in (7 - 1/B, 7 + 1/B), so V/n lies in the open interval (log2(7 - 1/B), log2(7 + 1/B)), of width about 4.1·10^-16. Find its smallest denominator. Preliminary (scratchpad, mpmath at 60 digits, not interval arithmetic): log2 7 = [2; 1, 4, 5, 4, 5, 4, 1, 29, 1, 4, 8, 1, 1, 2, 1, 31, 10, ...], convergent denominators 1, 5, 26, 109, 571, 2393, 2964, 88349, 91313, 453601, 3720121, 4173722, 7893843, 19961408, 27855251, 883474189, ...; the smallest denominator in the interval is n = 27,855,251 with V = 78,199,576, so a non-trivial cycle of 7x ± 1 would have at least 27,855,251 odd steps and at least 106,054,827 steps of Barina's f. To be redone with exact arithmetic before it is quoted.
2. Two-sidedness. The interval is symmetric about log2 7 up to second order, so both neighbours of the bracketing Farey pair count, unlike 3x+1 (Section 2.2). The admissible n are a q + b q' for the Farey pair bracketing the interval, as in Eliahou.
3. Averaged refinement (Hercher, Theorem 16). Replace 1/B by the mean of 1/|x_j|, using |log Π(1 + d_j/(7x_j))| ≤ Σ 1/(7|x_j| - 1); this needs an analogue of Hercher's growth lemma between local minima (orbits from a local minimum rise by 7/4 per step with v = 2), which has to be proved for 7x ± 1.
4. Circuit route (Steiner, Simons and de Weger, Simons 2008 if it covers fixed digits only). On a step with v = 2, x + d/3 is multiplied by exactly 7/4, but d = ±1 depends on x mod 4 and can change along a run, so Steiner's closed form holds only on runs of constant digit. A circuit theory for 7x ± 1 has to track the digit pattern; this is new work, and it is what would make Baker useful. Baker step: two logarithms (log 2, log 7), Laurent 2008 or Laurent, Mignotte and Nesterenko, then Baker and Davenport reduction with the continued fraction of log2 7.
5. Raising B: the bound grows like B^(1/2) generically, with jumps at convergent denominators (the next is 883,474,189, needing an interval about 1000 times narrower).

### 6.2 The 5+2i map

1. Interval step in two dimensions. Every nonzero z with N(z) ≤ 3·10^9 reaches a unit (H8, H9), so every odd element of a non-trivial cycle up to units has N(z_j) > B = 3·10^9. With w_j = d_j/(α z_j) and |d_j| ≤ √5, |w_j| < r = (5/(29B))^(1/2) ≈ 7.58·10^-6, and επ^V = α^n Π(1 + w_j). Exact necessary condition, testable in integers: N(επ^V - α^n) < 29^n((1 + r)^n - 1)^2. Equivalently, |Λ| < nρ with ρ = r/(1 - r), where Λ has real part (V ln 2 - n ln 29)/2 and imaginary part Vπ/4 + cπ/2 - n arg α for some integer c: a simultaneous approximation of log2 29 and of arg α/(π/4) with error growing linearly in n (catalog M2, M24).
2. Preliminary (scratchpad, double precision, n ≤ 2·10^6): the first n satisfying |Λ| < nρ is n = 1901, with V = 9235 (|Λ| = 0.01183 against nρ = 0.01441). Records of |Λ| in n: 1, 2, 4, 6, 27, 64, 225, 514, 1901, 2190, 4091, 8696, 67153, 298791, 307487, 673431. So a non-trivial cycle up to units would have at least 1901 odd steps and about 9235 divisions. To be confirmed with the exact integer test of item 1.
3. Strength. Generic best simultaneous approximations in dimension 2 have error of order n^(-1/2), against an allowed error nρ, so the bound grows only like ρ^(-2/3), that is like B^(1/3) in the norm bound; raising B to 3·10^10 would gain a factor of about 2.2. Beyond the first survivor almost every n with a good record survives, and the survivors become dense near n of order 1/ρ ≈ 1.3·10^5, so the interval step saturates early. The averaged refinement (6.1 item 3) and a Gaussian analogue of the circuit route are the only ways past it.
4. Baker step: Λ = n log(5+2i) - V log(1+i) - c log i, three logarithms in Q(i); Matveev (complex form) or Baker and Wüstholz give |Λ| > exp(-C log n) with an explicit C; only useful against an upper bound on |Λ| that decreases in n, which needs circuit structure. Reduction: LLL on the lattice built from real and imaginary parts (de Weger, Smart), not a single continued fraction.
5. Novelty. The angle condition and the use of best simultaneous approximation for a Collatz cycle bound were not found (Section 4.2), so the 5+2i bound would be new; the 7x ± 1 bound would be the first for Barina's map but by a standard method (Sections 2.2, 3.2).
