## 0. Why, and the author's decisions (Oct 29, 2026)

**Why.** Round 3 measured Design 3 (interning) on streams of single operations ([`../10-interning/test/results-r3/results.md`](../10-interning/test/results-r3/results.md)): Q32-hash beat Q32-index on time on every job, by 1.6 to 57 times. This round puts Design 3's hash store into two common computations on matrices: first **addition**, $C = A + B$ elementwise, where each entry of $A$ meets one entry of $B$ once; then **multiplication**, $C = A \cdot B$, where values are reused by construction: every entry of $A$ and of $B$ is used $n$ times.

The author's decisions:

1. **Rows.** The textbook control, **TC**, and five interned builds with the hash store: two ways to test cross matches (**V**, **M**) times two table sizings (**fixed**, **grow**): **Hash-V-fixed**, **Hash-M-fixed**, **Hash-V-grow**, **Hash-M-grow**; and **Hash-V-grow-map**, which differs from Hash-V-grow in one thing only, how a growth rewrites the cells (item 4). The rewrite method affects creation and V/M affect operations, so the map method is added on V only. **The index store is dropped.** Every structure is named and counted in §3; nothing else may exist in a build.
2. **TC** is what a standard library rational type does: a 64-bit numerator and denominator, in lowest terms, gcd-first, with the special cases of `../10-interning/lib/src/tc3.c` (v0.3 §17.3). Fixed width; not adaptive.
3. **Per-matrix stores.** Each of $A$, $B$ and $C$ has its own store: the matrix's rationals (its cells) and one open-addressing table of its whole numbers, with linear probing, a multiplicative hash in `uint64_t`, −1 as the empty mark, and slot = handle (as v0.3 §8).
4. **Two table sizings.** **Fixed:** sized from the dimensions, $T \ge 2n^2 + 2$, never resized (§5.3). **Grow:** starts small and doubles before it passes half full; each doubling reinserts every whole number into a new table and, since slot = handle, rewrites every handle in that matrix's cells in one pass (§5.6). Two rewrite methods are tested: **lookup** (Hash-V-grow, Hash-M-grow), a pure replacement: read the value at the old handle in the old table and look it up in the new table, whose slot is the new handle; and **map** (Hash-V-grow-map), through an old-slot → new-slot array recorded during reinsertion.
5. **0 and 1** are stored at handles 0 and 1 in every matrix's store at creation (v0.3 §17.2).
6. **Handle width** per matrix: a power of two chosen from the matrix's dimensions, in both sizings; in grow, handles change value on growth but never width. The tag bit sits at the bottom of the handle field, so zero-extension preserves a handle.
7. **Same inputs, same width.** $A$ and $B$ hold the same values at the same 64-bit width in every build. Only $C$ picks its integer width, a power of two (8, 16, 32, 64), from the running maximum.
8. **The arithmetic core is 64-bit in every build**, as TC's (the 08 module). In multiplication, products and partial sums are temporaries and are never stored. A cell of $C$ is stored only when finished. A value that does not fit 64 bits is an **error** in every build, as in the 08 module.
9. **Cross matches** (a whole number of $A$ against one of $B$). 0 and 1 are detected by handle in every matrix. Equality across $A$ and $B$ is tested two ways: **V** compares the values; **M** (the author's "merge" variant) uses a **translation from $B$'s handles to $A$'s**, built once after $A$ and $B$ are complete, always $B \to A$ ($A$ is the left operand), so a cross match is one handle comparison. A whole number of $B$ that $A$ lacks gets no entry. **The translation is a new structure, approved by the author for testing** (§7.2).
10. **Two measures of time**, each with its own verdict: **creation** (building the stores) and **operation** (the addition or the multiply).
11. **Timing method:** round 3's (rule-r3 items 4–6): the idle check, the disturbance check by process with the pilot's host allowance, the single runner in the background, and the process's own CPU time as a cross-check.
12. **The sweep:** $n \in \{16, 32, 64, 128\}$ by $\rho \in \{1, 4, 16, 64\}$, 16 jobs per job set ($n = 8$ dropped); its justifications are in rule-m1, Workloads (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026).
13. **Two operations, two runs.** Matrix **addition**, $C = A + B$ elementwise, and **multiplication**, $C = A \cdot B$, each with its own job set. The builds, the rows and the rule do not change between them. They are two separate timed runs, each with its own pilot, hard stop, results and report (all 16 jobs in both sets; hard stop 40 minutes and pilot projection limit 30 minutes per run, approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026): **the addition run first; the multiplication run only after the author has seen the addition results.** Addition goes through the same rows, the same V/M cross tests (§6.1) and both sizings.

## 1. Scope

In scope: the six builds, their structures, matrix addition and multiplication, instrumentation and correctness checks. Out of scope: the index store; other matrix operations (subtraction, scaling, division); values past 64 bits (an error); a fill sweep; a profiling build (M-9). Nothing in `../10-interning/` is changed.

## 2. Terms

As v0.3 §3, with:
- **Matrix** $X$ of $r \times c$ **cells**. $A$, $B$ and $C$ ($C = A + B$ in addition, $C = A \cdot B$ in multiplication) are $n \times n$ (square only in this round).
- **Whole number:** a non-negative integer stored in a store; a rational's numerator magnitude or its denominator.
- **Handle field:** $w_h$ bits holding $2k + t$, where $k$ is the handle and $t$ the tag: the sign for a numerator, 0 for a denominator. Handles are compared as $\lfloor f/2 \rfloor$. Reading a field into a wider integer by zero-extension gives the same $k$ and $t$.
- **Bound** of a matrix: $K = 2rc + 2$, the most whole numbers its cells can name (two per cell) plus 0 and 1.
- **Fixed table size:** $T_\text{fix} = 2^{\lceil \log_2 K \rceil}$, the smallest power of two at least $K$.
- **Table size** $T = 2^t$: $T_\text{fix}$ in the fixed rows; the current size in the grow rows.
- **Width** $w$ of a store: the bits of each stored whole number, two's complement, so a whole number $v$ needs $\text{bitlen}(v) + 1$ bits. $w = 64$ for $A$ and $B$; $C$'s runs from 8 to 64.
- **Temporary:** a 64-bit signed numerator and a 64-bit denominator held in local variables during one cell's computation.
- **Cross test:** one of the comparisons of v0.3 §9 between a whole number of $A$ and one of $B$ (§7).
- **Growth** (grow rows): one doubling of a table, with its reinsertion and its rewrite of the cells (§5.6).
- **The operation:** the matrix addition of an addition job, or the matrix multiplication of a multiplication job (§6).

## 4. TC

TC's arithmetic is `tc3.c`'s: the multiply and the addition of v0.2 §10 with v0.3 §17.3's cases and order, on 64-bit values through the 08 module. `tc3.c`'s operations take handles into its list (`operand()` reads `c->P[h]`; `begin()` checks $x, y < N$) and append every result, so this round's TC is a new source, **`lib/src/tcm.c`: `tc3.c` with its arithmetic function bodies unchanged and only the operand and result interface changed**:
- **unchanged:** the comparisons, the case tests and their order, the gcds and gcd work, the exact divisions, multiplications and additions, the known-answer skips and the overflow steps, line for line;
- **operands:** each operation takes two pairs $(m, d)$ of `int64` values instead of two handles: read from the cells of $A$ and $B$ (addition's `add`; multiplication's `mul`), or the local pairs $s$ and $p$ (multiplication's `add(s, p)`). `operand()` forms its magnitudes and sign from the pair as before;
- **results:** `store` writes the result to the destination the caller names, a cell of $C$ or a local pair ($p$ or $s$, in multiplication), instead of appending it;
- **entering:** `tc_enter`'s reduction is unchanged; its `store` writes the cell of $A$ or $B$ being entered (§5.7's order);
- **`begin()`'s bounds check** has no handle to check. It is replaced by the matrix loops' own bounds ($0 \le i, j, k < n$, which hold by construction) and, in the test build, by an assertion that each operand pair is valid: $d \ge 1$, in lowest terms, $m \ne -2^{63}$. `begin()` keeps setting the phase and context.

`tc3.c` itself stays unchanged and hash-checked as the diff's base; the diff is reviewed line by line, and **changes only where operands come from and where results go** (§14).

**The TC gate** (rule-m1 item 3): on every job, the job's sequence of operations (an addition job's additions; a multiplication job's multiplications and additions) is also run through `tc3.c` itself (results appended to its list (g)), and `tcm.c`'s case and gcd counts (gcds, gcd work, exact divisions, multiplications, additions) MUST equal `tc3.c`'s, operation for operation.

In multiplication every product and partial sum is a local pair; in both operations only finished cells are written. An overflow of 64 bits is an error, reported as `tc3.c` reports it. $B = 16$ bytes per cell.

## 5. The interned stores

### 5.1 The cells, structure (a)

A fixed array of $rc$ cells, allocated at creation from the dimensions. Each cell is two handle fields of $w_h$ bits: the numerator's (handle, sign tag) and the denominator's (handle, tag 0). A cell is in lowest terms, denominator at least 1, zero as $+0/1$ (handles 0 and 1). Bytes: $2 w_h rc / 8$.

### 5.2 Handle width, from the dimensions

- **Fixed:** $w_h$ is the smallest of 8, 16, 32 with $w_h - 1 \ge \log_2 T_\text{fix}$.
- **Grow:** a grown table can reach $2 T_\text{fix}$ slots (§5.6 item 6), so $w_h$ is the smallest of 8, 16, 32 with $w_h - 1 \ge \log_2 T_\text{fix} + 1$.

Either is fixed at creation and never changes. On the sweep both rules give the same $w_h$, so the cells are the same size in every hash row:

| $n$ | $K$ | $T_\text{fix}$ | $w_h$ (both) | cell bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | 514 | 1,024 | 16 | 4 |
| 32 | 2,050 | 4,096 | 16 | 4 |
| 64 | 8,194 | 16,384 | 16 | 4 |
| 128 | 32,770 | 65,536 | 32 | 8 |

(8-bit handles hold only for $n \le 7$ in fixed and $n \le 5$ in grow, so the sweep does not reach them; the unit tests do.) **Confound:** from $n = 64$ to $n = 128$ both the size and $w_h$ change (16 → 32 bits, cells 4 → 8 bytes), so that step cannot separate the two.

### 5.3 The table, structure (b)

As v0.3 §8 and §17.2. $T$ slots at width $w$, every slot −1, then 0 at slot 0 and 1 at slot 1. A store tests $v = 0$ and $v = 1$ first (0 probes), else probes from $h(v)$ by $s \leftarrow (s + 1) \bmod T$ and stops at $v$ (found) or at an empty slot (new). The probe loop is bounded by $T$; a full table is an error, detected, and cannot occur ($W \le K \le T_\text{fix}$ in fixed; $W \le T/2$ in grow). Bytes: $T w / 8$, always, used or not.
- **Fixed:** $T = T_\text{fix}$, allocated at creation, never resized. No fill limit (as round 3).
- **Grow:** starts at $T_0$ and doubles (§5.6).

**Hash function:** round 3's, with $t = \log_2 T$ bits: $h(v) = \lfloor ((v \cdot \phi) \bmod 2^{64}) / 2^{64 - t} \rfloor$, $\phi = \text{9E3779B97F4A7C15}_{16}$, computed in `uint64_t`. **It depends on the value and $T$ only, not on the table's width.**

### 5.4 Widths of $A$, $B$ and $C$

- **$A$ and $B$:** $w = 64$ from creation, in every build; no widening. Their whole numbers are the same in every hash row.
- **$C$:** starts at $w = 8$ holding 0 and 1. Storing a whole number $v$ (a 64-bit temporary): probe for $v$, comparing each entry sign-extended to 64 bits; if found, return its slot; if new: in the grow rows, grow if §5.6's trigger is met; then, if $\text{bitlen}(v) + 1 > w$, **widen** (§5.5) straight to the smallest of 16, 32, 64 that holds $v$; then write $v$ into the empty slot found by probing the current table.

### 5.5 Widening $C$ (open problem 2: the simplest correct handling)

Allocate the widened table (w) (§3), $T$ slots at the new width; copy every slot, in order, by sign extension, so −1 stays −1 and every whole number keeps its value and slot; make (w) $C$'s table (b) and free the old one, before the store call returns. Slots do not move, so **no handle in any cell changes**. $C$ widens at most 3 times. Each widening is counted (with its slots copied) and timed in the operation part. On the sweep, $C$ widens in its first few stores, one to three copies per job. In multiplication it jumps from 8 to 64 bits (its whole numbers need more than 32 bits); there the 16- and 32-bit steps are exercised only by the unit tests. In addition its whole numbers need at most 32 bits, and it may step 8 → 16 → 32 when its first values fit 16 bits, or stay at 16 on a job whose values all do; the exact model predicts every step, and the gates check it (§10 items 2 and 7).

### 5.6 Growing (grow rows only)

1. **Start size (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, M-4):** $T_0 = 16$, a chosen floor.
2. **Trigger (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, M-5):** before a new whole number is written, if $W + 1 > T/2$, the table grows. So a grow table is never more than half full: the standard load-factor limit for linear probing.

   **Flagged: this is a fill limit, which the author rejected for round 3's fixed table** (v0.3 §0 item 3). **Why growing needs one:** a growing table must decide when to grow, and the trigger is that decision. Growing only when completely full would run each table to 100% fill before every doubling. There a linear-probing store of a new value costs about $\tfrac12 (1 + 1/(1 - \alpha)^2)$ probes at fill $\alpha$, which has no bound as $\alpha \to 1$, and the last store before a doubling would probe the whole table. At half full a new value costs about 2.5 probes on average. A fixed table has no such decision to make, so round 3 needed no limit.
3. **One growth**, inside the store call that triggers it. The new table (n) is (b)'s replacement; old and new coexist only during the copy.
   1. allocate (n), $2T$ slots at the store's width, every slot −1, then 0 at slot 0 and 1 at slot 1;
   2. **reinsert:** for each old slot $s = 2, \ldots, T - 1$ in order that holds a whole number $v$, probe (n) from $h(v)$ with $t + 1$ bits to an empty slot $s'$ and write $v$ there;
   3. **rewrite the cells:** one pass over every handle field of the matrix written so far, in the order of entry (§5.7): every field of the cells already stored and, when the growth comes while storing a cell's denominator, that cell's numerator field; each handle field $f$, with handle $k = \lfloor f/2 \rfloor$ and tag $f \bmod 2$, becomes $2k' + (f \bmod 2)$, the tag kept, where $k'$ is found by the row's method:
      - **lookup** (Hash-V-grow, Hash-M-grow): read $v$ = old table$[k]$, and find $v$ in (n) (0 and 1 by the test, else by probing from $h(v)$); its slot is $k'$. No other array;
      - **map** (Hash-V-grow-map): during step 2, also record $r[s] = s'$ in the slot map (r), $T$ entries of $w_h$ bits (0 → 0, 1 → 1, empty slots unused); then $k' = r[k]$;
   4. make (n) the table (b); free the old table (and (r), in the map row).

   The store then probes for its new value in the grown table and writes it. A store into $C$ that needs both a growth and a widening grows first, then widens. *The trade:* lookup probes (n) once more per handle field rewritten; map reads one array entry instead, at the cost of (r)'s $T w_h / 8$ bytes during the growth.
4. **Handles change value, never width:** $w_h$ is fixed from the dimensions (§5.2).
5. **$\tau$ is untouched by growth:** it is built after $A$ and $B$ are complete (§7.2), and neither grows afterwards.
6. **Size bound:** $T \ge 2W$ after every store and $W \le K \le T_\text{fix}$, so a table that has grown has $T \le 2 T_\text{fix}$ (the grow rule of §5.2). One exception: at $n = 1$, $T_\text{fix} = 4$ while $T_0 = 16$, so $T \le 2 T_\text{fix}$ fails; the table never grows there ($W \le 4$), and its 16 handles still fit the 8-bit field the rule gives ($w_h - 1 = 7$ bits). In general $T \le \max(2 T_\text{fix}, T_0)$.
7. **Where it is timed:** a growth of $A$ or $B$ falls in the creation part, its reinsertion and rewrite included. **A growth of $C$ falls in the operation part,** since $C$ is built by the operation (flagged: the author asked that growth be timed under creation; that holds for $A$ and $B$). Every growth is counted: growths, whole numbers reinserted, probes while reinserting, cell fields rewritten, and, in the lookup rows, probes while rewriting. So the map method also touches the operation part, through $C$'s growths; its pairing with Hash-V-grow is judged on creation only (rule-m1 item 7).

### 5.7 Order of entry, and the numerator's field first

**Order of entry, in every build:** the cells of $A$ are entered in row-major order, then the cells of $B$ in row-major order ($A$ fully before $B$); the cells of $C$ are filled in row-major order during the operation. Within a cell, the numerator comes before the denominator. The slots of every table, the moments of every growth, and the set of "fields written so far" that a growth rewrites (§5.6 item 3) all follow from this order, and the model (§10 item 2) uses it.

In every hash row, a cell is stored in this order: store the numerator's whole number and **write the numerator's handle field into the cell**; then store the denominator's whole number and write its field. This holds when entering $A$ and $B$ and when storing each cell of $C$ during the operation, addition or multiplication. So when the denominator's store triggers a growth, the numerator's handle is already in the cell, and the rewrite (§5.6 item 3) reaches it; no handle is ever held only in a local variable across a store. In the fixed rows the order changes nothing, and it is kept for uniformity.

## 6. The operations

Every job is either an **addition job** or a **multiplication job** (rule-m1, Workloads). The builds, rows and structures are the same for both; only the operation differs. In both, $A$ is the left operand and $B$ the right.

**The cross tests are the same four in both operations:** the comparisons of v0.2 §9 between a whole number of $A$ and one of $B$, made in every operation, before a case is chosen: $b = d$, $a = c$ (in magnitude, so $c = \pm a$), $a = d$, $c = b$. They are made by V or M (§7). The ±1 tests ($a = b$, $c = d$) and the zero and integer tests ($a = 0$, $c = 0$, $b = 1$, $d = 1$) compare an operand's handle with 0 or 1 within one matrix; they are not cross tests.

### 6.1 Addition (run first)

For each cell, in row order, $C_{ij} = A_{ij} + B_{ij}$, with $x = A_{ij}$ and $y = B_{ij}$:

```text
function cell(i, j)                                 addition, all builds
    let s = add(A[i][j], B[i][j])                   v0.3 §17.3: cases, order, gcd-first
    store s as C[i][j]                              TC: write the pair; interned: store both whole numbers (§5.4)
end function
```

1. **`add`** is v0.3 §17.3's addition, cases and order unchanged: `zero` ($x + (-x)$) · `zero_x` · `zero_y` · `int_both` · `int_x` · `int_y` · `cross` · `den` · `num` · `general`.
2. **Its operands are cells**, one of $A$ and one of $B$, so $A$'s and $B$'s values meet in every cell's add. Its tests:
   - **within a matrix, by handle:** `zero_x` ($x$'s numerator handle 0), `zero_y` ($y$'s numerator handle 0), the integer tests ($x$'s or $y$'s denominator handle 1, for `int_x`, `int_y`, `int_both`);
   - **across $A$ and $B$, by V or M, exactly these four:** $b = d$ (case `den`, and with $a = c$ and opposite signs, case `zero`), $a = c$ (case `num`, and `zero`), $a = d$ and $c = b$ (case `cross`).

   Whole numbers are read by slot when a case needs them, once per distinct handle (v0.3 K-Q3).
3. **One add per cell, no temporary beyond its result**, which is stored at once. The store hints (v0.3 K-Q1) do not apply: the result's handles belong to $C$'s store, not to $A$'s or $B$'s, so an operand's handle cannot be reused in $C$ (0 and 1 are found by the store's 0/1 test, v0.3 §17.2). The numerator's field is written to the cell before the denominator is stored (§5.7).
4. **$C$'s whole numbers** are at most $2 \cdot 32767 \cdot 720$ in magnitude and $720^2$ as denominators, so $C$ needs at most 32 bits (rule-m1, Workloads).

### 6.2 Multiplication (run second)

For each cell, in row order, with $x = A_{ik}$ and $y = B_{kj}$:

```text
function cell(i, j)                                 multiplication, all builds
    let s = 0/1                                     a temporary
    for k = 1, ..., n
        let p = mul(A[i][k], B[k][j])               v0.3 §17.3: cases, order, gcd-first; a temporary
        let s = add(s, p)                           v0.3 §17.3 on two temporaries
    end for
    store s as C[i][j]                              TC: write the pair; interned: store both whole numbers (§5.4)
end function
```

1. **The algorithms** are v0.3 §17.3's multiply and addition, cases and order unchanged, gcd-first, every result in lowest terms, through the 08 module unchanged, in every build. The first addition of each cell is case `zero_x`.
2. **Operands of `mul`** are cells. Its tests are made as v0.3 §9 and §17.3, before a case is chosen:
   - **within a matrix, by handle:** zero ($x$'s numerator handle 0, $y$'s numerator handle 0), integer (denominator handle 1), $\pm 1$ (both handles 1);
   - **across $A$ and $B$, by V or M:** the same four, $b = d$, $c = \pm a$, $a = d$, $c = b$ (§6 above).

   Whole numbers are read by slot when a case needs them, once per distinct handle (v0.3 K-Q3).
3. **`add`** here has two temporaries as operands, so all its tests compare values, in every build; it makes no cross test.
4. **No intermediate is stored.** The store hints (v0.3 K-Q1) do not apply. The store of a finished cell tests 0 and 1 first (v0.3 §17.2), then stores each whole number, the numerator's field written to the cell before the denominator is stored (§5.7).
5. **Overflow** (both operations). Any result past 64 bits is an **error** in every build: the operation returns an overflow status and the step, as `tc3.c` does. As in the 08 module, a result equal to $-2^{63}$ (`INT64_MIN`) is an overflow. The sweep's values cannot overflow (rule-m1, Workloads).

## 7. Cross tests: V and M

### 7.1 V: compare values

A cross test reads the whole number of $A$ and that of $B$ by slot and compares them as 64-bit values. No structure is added. **Counting:** the reads that V makes for its cross tests ($a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, each once per distinct handle) are counted as part of the cross tests ("cross reads"), not as K-Q3 reads; the arithmetic then uses those values without reading again. M makes no read for a cross test; its reads are all K-Q3 reads, made when a case needs them. So V and M differ in reads and cross tests by design (§10 item 4).

### 7.2 M: a translation from $B$'s handles to $A$'s (new structure, approved for testing)

The author named this variant a merge. No merged store is built: $A$'s store is used as it stands, with one translation, always from $B$ (the right operand) to $A$ (the left).

| # | Structure | Shape | Each entry | Bytes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (t) | Translation $\tau$ | one position per slot of $B$'s final table: $T_B$ | the slot of $A$ holding the same whole number, or **none** ($2^{w_{h,A}} - 1$, never a handle) | $T_B\, w_{h,A} / 8$ |

1. **Built once**, at the start of the operation (addition or multiplication), after $A$ and $B$ are complete (so after any growth of theirs): for each whole number of $B$, a **find** in $A$'s store (its probe, without the write; $A$ is not changed). 0 and 1 map to 0 and 1.
2. **A whole number of $B$ that $A$ lacks gets no entry:** nothing is added to $A$ for it, and its position in $\tau$ holds none, as do $B$'s empty slots. (Approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, M-7: $\tau$ is indexed directly by $B$'s slot, so every slot has a position; "no entry" is the none mark.)
3. **A cross test** is $k_A = \tau[k_B]$: one comparison of two handles of $A$.
4. **Freed** at the end of the operation. Its build time is part of the operation time and is also reported on its own.

## 8. Creation and operation

The driver reads the clock only between calls. Three parts per run (rule-m1 item 4):
- **creation:** `create` of $A$, $B$ and $C$ (cells allocated; tables emptied, with 0 and 1) and entering every value of $A$ and $B$ from (in-A) and (in-B) (reduce gcd-first as v0.2's entry, then store, numerator's field first, §5.7), with every growth of $A$ and $B$ in the grow rows;
- **operation:** the addition or the multiply: building $\tau$ (M rows), every addition (and, in multiplication, every product), and storing every cell of $C$, with its widenings and, in the grow rows, its growths. $\tau$'s build time is also reported alone; the growths' time is not separated from the stores (it is counted, §9);
- **read-out:** reading every cell of $C$ into the driver's read-out array (out). Reported, not judged.

## 9. Instrumentation

**Counts** (counting build), per part: products and additions by case; gcds, gcd work, exact divisions, multiplications, additions (the core); cross tests and cross reads; K-Q3 reads; per store: stores (found, new), probes and longest store, $W$, final $T$ and fill $W/T$, final $w$, $C$'s widenings and slots copied; in the grow rows, per store, growths, whole numbers reinserted, reinsertion probes and cell fields rewritten; for the M rows, $\tau$'s finds (found, none) and their probes.

**Memory**, after creation and at the end of the operation (just before $\tau$ is freed):
- TC: 16 bytes per cell;
- interned: $\sum_X \big(2 w_h rc / 8 + T w / 8\big)$, plus $\tau$'s bytes in the M rows, over $3n^2$ cells;
- **fixed loses on memory by design:** its tables are sized for the bound $K$ whatever the values, so on the sweep it takes, against TC's 16 bytes per cell (the upper end at $n = 128$, where cells are 8 bytes and $\tau$ 4 bytes per entry):
  - **multiplication** ($C$ 64-bit): Hash-V-fixed 2.25–2.5 times, Hash-M-fixed ($\tau$ included) 2.42–2.83 times;
  - **addition** ($C$ 32-bit): Hash-V-fixed 1.92–2.17 times, Hash-M-fixed 2.08–2.5 times (a little less on a job whose $C$ stays at 16 bits).

  This raises the severe-memory-loss flag on every fixed job except addition's Hash-V-fixed at $n \le 64$, and is reported, not a surprise;
- **grow is the data-dependent one:** each table holds between $2W$ and $4W$ slots (at least $T_0$), so its bytes follow the distinct whole numbers, not the bound. $C$ is nearly unshared in multiplication, where its table can reach fixed's size or, at most, twice it. In addition $C$ has at most $s^2$ distinct values, so it is shared where $s$ is small against $n$ ($s \le n$: $n = 16$ at $\rho = 16$ and 64, $n = 32$ and 64 at $\rho = 64$), and there grow's table for $C$ stays far below fixed's;
- **the peak during a growth**, in every grow row: the old table and (n) at once, plus (r) in Hash-V-grow-map, beside the other matrices' bytes at that moment; accounted from the counts, and as heap, read by the memory build inside the growth just before the old table is freed (a read that changes no control flow). It is reported for each grow row and compared in the pairing Hash-V-grow-map against Hash-V-grow (rule-m1 item 7), with no verdict;
- the heap figure as rule-r3 item 7 (`mallinfo2`).

**Timing:** §8, with round 3's driver method (v0.3 §11.3).

## 10. Correctness

1. Every cell of $C$ of every run against Python's `fractions` matrix sum or product.
2. Against the generator's model, exactly: each final table, slot by slot (sha256); $W$, $T$, $w$, $C$'s widenings, $w_h$, probes and longest store per store; in the grow rows, every growth (when, whole numbers reinserted, probes, fields rewritten); $\tau$ position by position; every case count.
3. **Call for call:** in the counting runs, the per-product and per-addition gcds, gcd work, exact divisions, multiplications and additions of every hash row MUST equal TC's.
4. **V against M**, within a sizing: the same case for every addition and product, the same read-out, the same core counts and the same store counts. Cross tests, cross reads, K-Q3 reads and $\tau$ are reported per variant and are not required equal (§7.1).
5. **Grow against fixed**, within V and within M: the same read-out, cases, core counts, $W$ and found/new splits per store; slots, probes and handles differ by design.
6. **Map against lookup** (Hash-V-grow-map against Hash-V-grow): the same tables slot by slot, the same cells after every growth, the same everything except the rewrite probes of lookup and (r).
7. **Audits** (test build), per store: v0.3 §12.2 (Q32-hash) and §17.2 item 6, plus: every entry fits $w$; every cell's handles name used slots; in the grow rows, fill at most one half and every cell's value unchanged by each growth; for the M rows, $\tau[k]$ holds $A$'s slot of $B$'s whole number $k$, or none exactly when $A$ lacks it or $k$ is empty.

   **Tests** (untimed), for both operations: every case of addition with operands from two matrices (`zero`, `zero_x`, `zero_y`, `int_both`, `int_x`, `int_y`, `cross`, `den`, `num`, `general`), each of its four cross tests matching and not matching, by V and by M (a whole number of $B$ that $A$ lacks included); every case of multiplication with operands from two matrices (`zero`, `one_x`, `one_y`, `int_both`, `both`, `den`, `num`, `cross_ad`, `cross_cb`, `int_x`, `int_y`, `general`), each of its four cross tests matching and not matching, by V and by M, and its `add(s, p)` on temporaries; each widening of $C$ (8 → 16 → 32 → 64), with every slot and handle unchanged after it; each growth from $T_0$ by both methods, with every cell's value unchanged, both methods giving the same handles, and 0 and 1 still at 0 and 1; a growth and a widening in one store of $C$; **a growth triggered by a denominator's store**, in $A$, in $B$ and in $C$, by both methods, with the same cell's numerator handle rewritten and its value unchanged; a growth of $C$ mid-operation, in addition and in multiplication; $\tau$ built from grown $A$ and $B$; $w_h$ at the dimension thresholds of both rules (8, 16, 32 bits); a 64-bit overflow, and a result of $-2^{63}$, return the error in every build; values whose home slot is 0 or 1, and a probe that wraps; $\tau$ with no match, all matching, and 0 and 1; the six builds against each other on random matrices.

## 11. Limits and errors

- Every build: 64-bit values; an overflow is an error.
- Hash rows: handle capacity $2^{w_h - 1}$ at least $T_\text{fix}$ (fixed) or $2 T_\text{fix}$ (grow); a full table cannot occur and is still detected (abort with a message).
- The sweep's values fit 64 bits with a wide margin (rule-m1, Workloads), so no overflow occurs there; one after the gates is a bug.

## 12. Open problems (flagged)

1. **Resizing a hash table changes slots** (deferred by the author for round 3; now tested). **Fixed avoids it:** $T$ and $w_h$ are fixed from the dimensions before the first insertion, $A$ and $B$ never widen, and the hash depends on the value and $T$, not the width, so $C$'s widening moves no slot (§5.5). **Grow meets it head on:** each growth reinserts every whole number and rewrites every handle of the matrix, by lookup or through the slot map (r) (§5.6), and the round measures what that costs. It is not solved for a table shared across matrices, whose handles would sit in several matrices' cells.
2. **Integer widening mid-build** ($C$ only). Handled by copying, slot for slot (§5.5).
3. **The fill limit of grow** (§5.6 item 2) is a fill limit, which the author rejected for round 3's fixed table; it is used here only because growing needs a trigger, and the author approved it for grow on Oct 29, 2026.
4. **"The rationals" read as the cells** (approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026, M-1): each cell is a pair of whole-number handles, and the whole numbers are what is stored once, as in round 3. A list of distinct rationals referred to by the cells would be a third structure per matrix; it is not included.
5. **Fill.** Fixed's fill is low by construction ($T \ge 2n^2 + 2$): for $A$ and $B$ up to about 28% (at $n = 16$, $\rho = 1$) and about 20% at $n = 128$; for $C$ up to about 50%, in both operations. Grow's lies between 25% and 50% once past $T_0$. This round is not a fill test.

## 13. Choices (all approved by the author, Oct 29, 2026)

- **M-1.** The cells are the rationals; whole numbers are interned (§12 item 4). Row names TC, Hash-V-fixed, Hash-M-fixed, Hash-V-grow, Hash-M-grow, Hash-V-grow-map, Hash-V-fixed-null; prefixes as §3.
- **M-2.** $K = 2rc + 2$, $T_\text{fix} = 2^{\lceil \log_2 K \rceil}$; $w_h$ by §5.2's two rules.
- **M-3.** $C$'s whole numbers stored as $w$-bit two's complement, starting at $w = 8$; −1 empty.
- **M-4.** Grow's start size $T_0 = 16$, a chosen floor.
- **M-5.** Grow's trigger: $W + 1 > T/2$ (a fill limit; §5.6 item 2).
- **M-6.** Growth inside the store call, reinsertion in old-slot order, one rewrite pass over the fields written so far, in the order of entry (§5.7), over the cells stored so far; grow before widen; the map recorded during reinsertion.
- **M-7.** $\tau$ indexed directly by $B$'s slot, with none for a whole number $A$ lacks (§7.2 item 2).
- **M-8.** Widening $C$ by copy (§5.5); the textbook loop $s = 0$; $s \leftarrow s + x y$ (§6); $\tau$ and $C$'s stores, widenings and growths in the operation part; `create` of all three matrices and $A$'s and $B$'s growths in the creation part.
- **M-9.** No profiling build in this round.
- **M-10.** In addition, the store hints (v0.3 K-Q1) are not applied, since the result's handles belong to $C$'s store (§6.1 item 3).
